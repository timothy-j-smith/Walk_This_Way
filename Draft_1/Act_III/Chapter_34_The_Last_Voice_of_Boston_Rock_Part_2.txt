## CHAPTER 34: THE LAST VOICE OF BOSTON ROCK PART 2

### **The Final Broadcast**

August 2003. WBCN's final night.

I'm sitting in Studio B, holding the demo tape that's been in my personal collection for thirty years. The tape that proves two boys had once found the wavelength where all voices mattered, before America convinced them they were supposed to be enemies.

The studio is smaller than I remember, but it feels like a cathedral. The walls are covered with acoustic foam that makes everything sound larger, more significant, more worthy of the moment that's about to happen. The equipment is state-of-the-art, the kind of gear that makes bedroom dreams feel like radio reality.

But tonight, it's all going to die.

WBCN is closing. The Rock of Boston is becoming just another casualty of the digital age, another victim of algorithms that can predict your taste but can't recognize when a song might save your life. Another casualty of artificial intelligences that can analyze your preferences but can't understand the difference between what you want to hear and what you need to hear.

But before we go, I want to play you something. Something that was never supposed to be heard. Something that proves that the connection still exists, if we're brave enough to tune in.

### **The Story**

"Ladies and gentlemen," I say into the microphone, my voice carrying the weight of fifty-six years of breaking bands and watching dreams die, "this is Maxanne Sartori, and for the next five minutes, you're listening to the final broadcast of WBCN, 104.1 on your FM dial."

I look at the demo tape in my hands, at the proof that authentic music had once been possible, and I make a decision that will define the rest of my life.

"Before we go," I say, "I want to play you something. Something that was never supposed to be heard. Something that proves that the connection still exists, if we're brave enough to tune in."

I pause, my mind wandering, somewhere. 

"But roads leave traces," I continue, my voice growing stronger. "Even when they're paved over, built around, forgotten. The old paths persist under the new ones. The signals remain, waiting for someone to find them again."

### **The Demo**

I slip the tape into the player, and for the next four minutes and forty-three seconds, the sound of Nick Johnson and Jackie Marsh singing their Queen/Aerosmith mashup fills the airwaves for the last time.

The sound of two boys who'd once been inseparable, who'd once believed that music could bridge any divide, who'd once found the wavelength where all voices mattered.

The sound of what could have been.

When the song ends, when the last note has faded into the silence of the studio, I look at the clock.

11:59:30 PM.

"Ladies and gentlemen," I say, my voice thick with tears, "this is Maxanne Sartori, signing off for the last time. WBCN, the Rock of Boston."

11:59:59 PM.

The ON AIR goes dark. The booth falls silent. One last time. WBCN is dead. But somewhere in Sudbury even in a town divided by water and waste, you can still hear two boys harmonizing in the garage of memory. Still proving that anything is possible when you are a kid. Still waiting for the rest of us to catch up.

The silence stretches out like an old New England highway, but if you listen close enough you can still hear them singing. Rocking to Queen and Aerosmith.  

You can always hear them singing if you remember how to listen.  

Walk this Way, I think to myself, yeah, WALK THIS WAY.

THE END.
