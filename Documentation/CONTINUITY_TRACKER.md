## GEOGRAPHIC FACTS

### **Sudbury, Massachusetts**
- **Location:** 20 miles west of Boston
- **Route 20:** Runs east-west (Boston Post Road, connects to city)
- **Population in 1979:** ~15,000 (estimate)
- **Dividing Features:**
  - **Hop Brook:** Runs north-south (named for hops from old mill)
  - **Town Dump:** East-west dividing line
  - Creates FOUR QUADRANTS

### **The Four Quadrants**

**NORTHWEST: THE RAMPERS (Upper Class)**
- Colonial houses, circular driveways, 3-car garages
- Professional landscaping, vacation homes on Cape
- Harvard-educated families, private lessons
- **Jackie's territory**

**NORTHEAST: THE RATS (Working Class)**
- Row houses, triple-deckers
- Tree-named streets (ironic - trees cut down)
- DIY repairs, weekend camping to NH
- **Nick's territory**

**SOUTHWEST: THE REGS (Faculty/Intellectual)**
- Modest houses filled with books
- BU professors, graduate students
- Education as currency
- Aspiration over achievement

**SOUTHEAST: THE ROLLERS (Rural/Farmers)**
- Farms, mechanics, self-sufficient
- Cavicchio Pig Farm location
- Utility over aesthetics
- Drive young, handle firearms

### **Key Locations**

**Hop Brook:**
- Runs north-south through Sudbury
- Empties into Sudbury River
- Kids fish from both banks
- The bridge = wooden, old, where friendship died

**The Dump:**
- Saturday morning gathering place
- Only democratic space (all quadrants mixed)
- Closed 1987 (EPA groundwater contamination)
- Became: Cavicchio Greenhouses (largest in New England by 2024)

**Cavicchio Pig Farm:**
- 300 acres of "Yankee pragmatism"
- Pigs eating garbage, feeding pigs
- Smell "could peel paint off houses a mile away"
- Northeast quarter smelled it most
- Family bought dump land, converted to greenhouses

**Conservation Land:**
- 100+ acres between quadrants
- Pine and oak, old growth
- Dirt bike trails (northeast kids)
- Walking paths (southwest kids)
- Conflict zone (different uses of same space)
