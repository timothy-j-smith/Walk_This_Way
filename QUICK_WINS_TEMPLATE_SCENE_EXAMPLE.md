## THE SCENE:

**November 1976 - Two weeks after Dan Johnson's death**

The garage door was open. Nick sat on an overturned milk crate, guitar across his lap, not playing. Just holding it. The duct tape on the neck was coming loose again.

Jackie's shadow fell across the concrete floor before he did.

"Hey."

Nick didn't look up. "What do you want?"

"I brought you something." Jackie held out a cassette. "Queen's new album. I thought—"

"I don't need your charity."

"It's not charity. It's—"

Nick stood. The guitar clattered against the crate. "I said I don't need it."

Jackie's hand dropped. The cassette dangled from his fingers. "I just thought you might want to listen. That's all."

"Right. You thought." Nick's knuckles were split. He'd been working on the car. His father's car. The one that didn't run. "You don't think. You just do. Like you know what I need."

"I don't—"

"You don't know shit." Nick's voice cracked. He cleared his throat. "You don't know what it's like."

"I know you're hurting."

"You don't know." Nick picked up the guitar. Held it like a weapon. "You think you can fix it with a cassette tape. With words. You can't fix it."

"I'm not trying to fix it. I'm trying to—"

"What? Help?" Nick laughed. A bitter sound. "I don't need your help. I don't need anything from you."

Jackie took a step back. "Nick—"

"Get out."

"Please. Just talk to me."

Nick's grip tightened on the guitar neck. "I said get out."

Jackie stood there. The cassette still in his hand. The silence stretched. Then he turned. Walked away. His footsteps on the driveway. The sound of a car door. An engine starting.

Nick sat back down. Put the guitar aside. Picked up a wrench. Started working on the car again. The metal was cold. His hands shook. He tightened his grip. Kept working.

The garage door stayed open. The November wind came through. Cold. Empty.
