Part 1: Fishing for Seaweed

2022. His first thought as he awoke was of the elephants trotting down the Boston Post Road, the same thought or perhaps the same dream as when he awoke from the coma.

<<Agent note — KEEP. This opening line is strong. Elephants on a road — specific, strange, immediate. The coma mention is a hook. The reader wants to know: what coma? Why elephants?>>

Thomas turned his head and the sand shifted against his cheek like warm grain. The Nauset Light beacon swept overhead, still turning after all these years even though the ships no longer needed it. He felt the sun pressing down heavy and close, the way a hand presses on a wound to stop the bleeding. Rashford had been sitting beside him for quite some time — Thomas could tell by the depth of the impression in the sand. A question was coming. Thomas could always feel the questions before they arrived now, the way he could feel the big waves building offshore before anyone else saw them. He lifted his head from the sand.

<<Agent note — OPENING REWRITE: Three key changes. (1) Your lighthouse beacon idea is IN — a light that keeps turning even when the world has moved on. Like Thomas. (2) Original said "He was conscious now and conscious that Rashford had been sitting beside him." That TELLS us Thomas is aware. The rewrite SHOWS it through the sand impression — time revealed through physical detail. (3) "He had to show restraint, good judgment, and not let anyone in too close" — that's the narrator explaining Thomas's strategy. CUT. His guardedness now shows through how he observes carefully before engaging. The reader FEELS his caution without being told. Also added: Thomas sensing the question coming like sensing waves — ties his heightened senses to the ocean metaphor that runs through the whole story.>>

"How come you are not climbing this weekend?" Each word came out of Rashford flat and separate, like items placed one at a time on a conveyor belt. Kids used to call him HAL for that voice. Rashford had loved it — did the red-eye thing with a flashlight at parties. But the flatness didn't feel like a joke anymore.

<<Agent note — RASHFORD INTRO: Original was "Rashford spoke in a strange digital-like monotone. He always had. On occasion, when they were younger and bored, Rashford's friends would call him 'HAL,' a nod to Arthur C. Clarke's '2001: A Space Odyssey' and popularized by Stanley Kubrick's 1968 movie. Instead of feeling teased or embarrassed, Rashford relished this comparison. Thomas was Rashford's best friend before the accident, but now there was an awkwardness between the two friends. One day this had caught Thomas by surprise—it was as if they were two magnets that once attracted, but now repelled; one could feel that invisible sphere and force that kept the repelling magnets pushed apart."

That's a BACKSTORY DUMP. The entire HAL explanation, the 2001 reference, the friendship history, the magnet metaphor — all in one paragraph. The rewrite: (1) SHOWS the robotic voice through a concrete image (conveyor belt). (2) Reduces the HAL backstory to one vivid memory detail (red-eye flashlight trick). (3) CUTS the magnet metaphor entirely — the reader FEELS the distance through the scene. (4) "But the flatness didn't feel like a joke anymore" — one sentence does what the original's magnet metaphor tried to do in three. Trust the reader.>>

Thomas's skin prickled. The sand was doing something to his arms — each grain a hot pinpoint, thousands of them, a constellation of tiny burns. Since the accident, his nerve endings had opinions about everything. The beach towel's cotton threads. The sunscreen's chemical slick. Rashford's question hanging in the salt air. Even that had a texture now — questions felt like sandpaper dragging across the inside of his skull. He had liked how things had been before. He cherished those times the way you cherish a room you can no longer enter.

Family and friends liked to remind him that he was so very lucky to be alive, that it had been a true miracle surviving a 120-foot rock climbing fall. None of them had to live inside this body.

<<Agent note — THOMAS'S CONDITION: Original said "He was convinced that his sensitivities had doubled, perhaps even tripled since the accident, particularly the nerves on the surface of his skin." That's TELLING us about his condition like a medical report. The rewrite puts us INSIDE his body — we FEEL the sand grains as hot pinpoints, we feel the question's texture. "Even that had a texture now" is how Thomas experiences his new reality. The reader doesn't need to be told his senses have changed — they experience the change alongside him.

Also: "He cherished those times the way you cherish a room you can no longer enter" — that's a MONEY SENTENCE. Specific, concrete, earns its emotion. Compare to the original: "He had liked how things had been before, how he cherished those times now." Same idea, but the rewrite gives it an image the reader can see and feel.

Last line: "None of them had to live inside this body." — Replaces the original's "but they didn't have to experience the unpredictable crackling wildness that shot throughout his body or the reoccurring nightmares." Same anger, fewer words, more punch. King's rule: when the emotion is strong, the sentence should be short.>>

2000. The elephants had escaped from the Wayland Memorial Day Circus held in the parking lot of Star Market — someone simply forgetting to properly latch the holding gate. Once Big Ellie pushed through, the others followed in unison like good soldiers performing their circus show. Thomas remembered their eyes, especially the baby elephant's eyes. Joy, fear, confusion, and perhaps a bit of disobedience. Or payback.

<<Agent note — ELEPHANT ESCAPE: Mostly kept intact — this section was already strong. Trimmed slightly: cut "a mixture of" before the list of emotions. Just list them. And broke "payback" onto its own short fragment for punch. "Or payback." — that pause makes the reader feel it. King does this constantly: the short fragment after a list that reframes everything.>>

Those were good days. The end of middle school, the smells of summer, navigating center field at Haskell Field during the Babe Ruth tournament. Sarah Peacock. She was ever present on his teenage brain, and Thomas had done absolutely nothing about it.

The elephants and the circus animals never returned to Wayland — the town couldn't afford the insurance after the debacle. Star Market still sponsored a scaled-down version with cotton candy and amusement rides, their colored lights spinning dizzily in every direction, and perhaps another teenager's faint hope of summer romance. But what's a circus without elephants?

Sarah Peacock never said goodbye. Her family packed up shortly after Memorial Day and moved to Chicago without much fanfare. Shortstop Scooter McIntosh claimed he'd kissed Sarah on her last night in town during the chaos of the elephants' escape. Things got crazy for a brief time. Then she was gone.

<<Agent note — SARAH PEACOCK: Two changes. (1) Added "and Thomas had done absolutely nothing about it" — this is your truth from your ((author note)) about being too shy. That line, placed simply and without explanation, hits harder than a paragraph of backstory. The reader feels the regret. (2) Ending: changed "Sarah T. Peacock and elephants" to "Then she was gone." Simpler. Final. The period lands like a door closing. Your original line was whimsical; this version carries the weight of real loss. The full name (Sarah T. Peacock) can come back later — maybe when Thomas looks her up, or when he sees something that reminds him. Save it.>>

2022. Thomas was sitting now, his hands wrapped around his knees, the smooth slippery feeling of his shaven legs radiating throughout his whole body. The daily fifteen-minute body shaves were better than feeling as if every hair follicle was a lightning rod. He let his head hair grow back — that didn't bother him. He was trying to grow a mustache, but even after weeks, not much was coming in. Funny — as a pre-teen he'd been first in his class to sprout an unwanted wire-like mustache that he refused to shave, and his friends had never let him forget it.

It was his brain that worried him the most. Something was still not quite right in there — some scrambled code in the operating system that no reset button could fix.

<<Agent note — THOMAS SITTING: Mostly kept intact — this section was already good because it's PHYSICAL and SPECIFIC. The shaved legs, the lightning-rod follicles, the failed mustache — these are concrete details that show Thomas's condition through his body. Minor tightening: (1) Cut "He had decided that" — just state it. (2) "some scrambled code in the operating system that no reset button could fix" — compressed your original's computer metaphor into one tighter line. Same image, fewer words.>>

As the waves pounded the surf, kids in knee-length cut-offs jumped them in a funny ping-pong unison, like piano keys played one after the other. Another group rode waves with color-coordinated swimsuits and boogey boards strapped to bony wrists. But the boy who stood out was the one sea-casting a rod three feet taller than himself. Every time he reeled in the line, there was nothing but a bulk of seaweed — mung — tangled on the hook. The boy pulled it off and cast again. Never discouraged.

"Mirko's fishing for seaweed!" one of the boys squawked, and the others joined in: "Fishing for seaweed, fishing for seaweed!"

Mirko just kept smiling, casting his line further and further, paying no attention to his friends or to the two seagulls fighting over a half-eaten hot dog.

<<Agent note — MIRKO: Almost unchanged. This section was already SHOWING not TELLING. You wrote this naturally — no narrator explaining that Mirko is persistent or brave. Just a boy, a rod, and seaweed. The reader assembles the meaning. This is your instinct working. Trust it.>>

"It really is something." Thomas spoke aloud, lost in his own thoughts, still looking out toward the black-blue depths of the Atlantic. "Out on the horizon, you see calmness. But they are building. Building beneath the surface."

Rashford interrupted: "Sally says she's going to bring Jackie-O. She said that last time was a fluke." Dead silence, broken only by the boys' chant.

"Fishing for seaweed, fishing for seaweed..."

"A fucking fluke?" Thomas let the hot sand sift through his swollen fingers the way sand flows through an hourglass. "A fluke, huh, is that what Sally said?" He pronounced it like it was French. "Fluke," he repeated.

"Well, she didn't say it like that, but that's the gist of it." Rashford paused. For a moment his voice shifted — warmer, almost human. "Look, you got to snap out of it. Wake up. You're doing great. What do you have to lose?" Then the warmth disappeared and he was pointing at the ocean. "Look at that one coming!"

<<Agent note — DIALOGUE SECTION: Mostly preserved — this dialogue was already working. Key change: Rashford's voice shift. Original said "for a moment, his voice and cadence changed and became more personal and less robotic." That's TELLING. The rewrite says "his voice shifted — warmer, almost human." Still brief, but "almost human" is more evocative than "more personal and less robotic." And "Then the warmth disappeared" shows the shift back without saying "Rashford yelled back in his normal tone and cadence." SHOW the change, don't label it.>>

About a quarter mile offshore, where more often there is calmness, a trained eye could see a giant wave building. Thomas knew how the ocean worked — had known since he was a child. The large waves formed slowly, building energy beneath the surface, growing as they approached unsuspecting swimmers and surfers. He had taught Rashford to read these signs over the years. The smaller waves — the ones that looked big to most people — built quickly and close to shore. False alarms. But they still attracted ambitious bodysurfers and still pounded them into the sand. Surfers who hadn't yet learned the ocean's secrets.

There were many more secrets to learn, Thomas thought.

<<Agent note — WAVE KNOWLEDGE: Tightened slightly but mostly preserved. This passage works because it's CONCRETE ocean knowledge that doubles as metaphor without ever saying "this is a metaphor." The ocean has secrets. Thomas knows some of them. There are more to learn. That's the whole novel in three sentences, and you never explain it. Good instinct. Kept "There were many more secrets to learn, Thomas thought" as its own paragraph for emphasis — it's a MONEY SENTENCE.>>

The wave caught everyone's attention. No disguising it. The weak sprinted to shore. The strong swam out to meet it. And some — Thomas could see them clearly — got caught in between. Unable to get their legs moving fast enough for shore, not enough momentum to swim toward the wave and crest it before it broke. Hesitators. Trapped between going and coming, committing and not committing. He had seen this his whole life — on waves, on rock faces, in the eyes of people making choices they couldn't take back.

<<Agent note — THE HESITATORS: Original said "When one hesitates, even for just a brief moment, one is inevitably trapped between going and coming, coming and going, committing or not committing, right and wrong, good and evil, black and white. Alive or Dead. And Thomas was still alive. And his aliveness was coming back alive." The problem: "right and wrong, good and evil, black and white" is a list of abstractions. And "his aliveness was coming back alive" is reaching. The rewrite keeps the concrete image (hesitators on the wave) and extends it to one new specific detail: "on rock faces, in the eyes of people making choices they couldn't take back." This connects the wave to climbing (foreshadowing The Wall) and to the human stakes — without listing abstract concepts. The "Alive or Dead" line is cut here but should return later in the novel at a moment when it's EARNED, not stated as philosophy.>>

Thomas remembered how he used to lower his head and take on the waves, no matter what. No matter the size. He remembered the biggest ones throwing him, thrashing him, spinning him blind — until he learned the ocean's deepest secret. If he couldn't swim out fast enough to crest a wave, and couldn't ride it to shore, there was a third option. A place at the very bottom of the ocean floor, below the turbulence. Still. Silent. Safe.

He had never told anyone about this place. Not even Rashford. He believed that if he shared the secret, the magic of it would disappear.

Below the turbulence, he thought. Below the turbulence is how to survive.

<<Agent note — BELOW THE TURBULENCE: Restructured for pacing. Original had this as one long paragraph. The rewrite breaks it into three beats: (1) the memory of fighting waves, (2) the secret place, (3) the rule — "Below the turbulence is how to survive" — isolated as its own line for maximum impact. This is your best metaphor in the entire draft. It deserves its own space on the page. It deserves to breathe. The reader needs to sit with it.>>

Rock climbing was the same. Thomas knew this in his body. He might need to start back slowly, but he couldn't keep sprinting to shore. He had to face the challenge. He had to face The Wall. He had to go back to Saddle Mountain.

<<Agent note — THE WALL: Original had an entire paragraph about fight/flight/freeze and the three types of wave-people. That was the narrator EXPLAINING the metaphor that the ocean scene had already SHOWN. CUT. The reader already understood fighters, fleers, and freezers from watching the wave. They don't need a lecture about it. The rewrite goes straight from the ocean truth to the climbing truth — same principle, different wall. "He knew this in his body" — because since the accident, Thomas's body knows things his mind hasn't caught up to yet. That's the story's central nervous system.>>

"What else did Sally say?" Thomas asked.

<<Agent note — END OF PART 1: Unchanged. This line is perfect as-is. A question that opens the door to Part 2. Simple. Direct. The reader wants to know too.>>
