Part 1: Fishing for Seaweed

2022. His first thought as he awoke was of the elephants trotting down the Boston Post Road, the same thought or perhaps the same dream as when he awoke from the coma.

Thomas turned his head to the left and felt completely engulfed in warmth, the Nauset Light beach sand below him and the mid-day sun directly above him. ((Show the beach's light Beacon still operating all these years even after the ships no longer used them or something like this image.)) He was conscious now and conscious that Rashford had been sitting beside him for quite some time. Thomas knew that a question was coming and that he had to show restraint, good judgment, and not let anyone in too close. Thomas lifted his head from the sand.

"How come you are not climbing this weekend?" Rashford spoke in a strange digital-like monotone. He always had. On occasion, when they were younger and bored, Rashford's friends would call him "HAL," a nod to Arthur C. Clarke's "2001: A Space Odyssey" and popularized by Stanley Kubrick's 1968 movie. Instead of feeling teased or embarrassed, Rashford relished this comparison. Thomas was Rashford's best friend before the accident, but now there was an awkwardness between the two friends. One day this had caught Thomas by surprise--it was as if they were two magnets that once attracted, but now repelled; one could feel that invisible sphere and force that kept the repelling magnets pushed apart.

Thomas's mind raced backward and forward. Forward and backward. He was convinced that his sensitivities had doubled, perhaps even tripled since the accident, particularly the nerves on the surface of his skin. He had liked how things had been before, how he cherished those times now. He was conscious of his new condition for almost every moment of every day, and the days ticked off slowly. Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday... repeat. There was not much rest or relief. This was his new reality now. Family and friends liked to remind him that he was so very lucky to be alive, that it had been a true miracle surviving a 120-foot rock climbing fall, but they didn't have to experience the unpredictable crackling wildness that shot throughout his body or the reoccurring nightmares. Thomas was still conflicted with the knowingness that he felt oddly distant from his best friend, so it was somewhat soothing to dream about elephants as his mind completed its final dream state before waking.

2000. The elephants had escaped from the Wayland Memorial Day Circus held in the parking lot of Star Market--someone simply forgetting to properly latch the holding gate. Once "Big Ellie" pushed through the gate, the others followed in unison like good soldiers and as if performing their circus show. Thomas remembered their eyes, especially the baby elephant's eyes. It was a mixture of joy, fear, confusion, and perhaps a bit of disobedience or payback. These days had been a fun and chaotic time, the end of middle school, the smells of summer, navigating the green grass center field at Haskell Field in the evening's Babe Ruth tournament and trying to figure out how to gain Sarah Peacock's attention. She was ever present on his teenage brain. The elephants and the circus animals never returned to Wayland, the town not being able to afford the insurance after the 2000 debacle. Star Market still sponsored a scaled-down circus with cotton candy, annoying amusement park rides with their colored lights dizzily spinning in every direction, and perhaps another teenager's faint hope of summer romance, but what's a circus without elephants? Sarah Peacock never did say goodbye as her family packed up shortly after Memorial Day and moved to Chicago without much fanfare. 2022. Thomas was sitting now, his hands wrapped around his knees, the nice smooth and slippery feeling of his shaven legs radiated throughout his whole body. He had decided that the daily fifteen-minute body shaves were much better than feeling as if every hair follicle was lightning-rod alive. He let his head hair grow back as this didn't provide any sensitivity issues. He was trying to grow a mustache, but even after weeks of trying, not much was coming in. As a pre-teen, he was the first to grow an unwanted wire-like mustache that he did not shave; all his friends made good fun of him and now, ironically, no hair wanted to grow above his lip. It was really his head and more specifically his brain that worried him the most. He knew that something was still not quite right and perhaps responsible for the changes in his senses. He wished that he had a reset button like the little red one on his personal computer that would somehow, miraculously, bring back to life a madly scrambled operating system. There was still the prominent fracture scar on his left leg that ran from his knee to his ankle, but this didn't give him any issue as this region had lost feeling altogether.

As the waves continued to pound the surf, several boys and girls wearing knee-length cut-off shorts jumped the waves in a funny kind of ping-pong unison like piano keys played one after the other. Another group rode the waves with color-coordinated swimsuits and boogey boards attached to bony wrists. One boy that stood out was sea-casting a rod that stood three feet taller than the boy. Every time the boy would reel in the line there would be a bulk of seaweed known as "mung" entangled on the lure's hook. The boy would diligently pull all the mung off and cast again, never discouraged.

"Mirko's fishing for seaweed!" one of the boys squawked, and then the other boys joined in, "Fishing for seaweed, fishing for seaweed," they repeated.

Mirko just kept on smiling, casting his line further and further and paying no attention to his friends or to the two seagulls fighting over a half-eaten hot dog.

"It really is something. Out in the horizon, you see calmness, but they are building. Building beneath the surface." Thomas spoke aloud, lost in his own thoughts as he continued to look out toward the black-blue depths of the Atlantic.

Rashford interrupted the moment: "Sally says she's going to bring Jackie-O, she said that last time was a fluke." There was dead silence only broken by the boys' chant.

"Fishing for seaweed, fishing for seaweed..." the boys laughed.

"A fucking fluke?" Thomas let the hot sand sift through his swollen fingers the way sand flows through an hourglass. "A fluke, huh, is that what Sally said?" Thomas emphasized the word fluke, as if pronouncing the word with a French accent. "Fluke," he repeated.

"Well, she didn't say it like that, but that's the gist of it." Rashford paused and, for a moment, his voice and cadence changed and became more personal and less robotic. "Look, you got to snap out of it. Wake up. You're doing great. What do you have to lose?" Rashford paused again and was suddenly intrigued by the waves. "Look at that one coming!" Rashford yelled back in his normal tone and cadence.

About a quarter mile offshore, where more often there is calmness, a well-trained eye could see the building of a giant wave. Thomas knew how the ocean worked since he was a young child; the large waves formed slowly, building up their energy beneath the surface and growing in size as they approached unsuspecting swimmers and surfers alike. Over the years Thomas had taught Rashford how to look for all of the subtle signs. The smaller waves, which appeared large to most, built up quickly and closer to shore. These waves were more of a false alarm, but still attracted the ambitious bodysurfers and often pounded them into the shoreline--surfers who had not yet figured out the ocean's secrets. There were many more secrets to learn, Thomas thought.

Rashford's wave caught everyone's attention; there was no disguising this wave. The weak sprinted to shore, the strong swimming or paddling out to battle with the mighty wave. It occurred to Thomas that sometimes one gets caught in between, unable to get one's legs moving fast enough to run to shore and certainly not enough momentum to start swimming toward the wave and crest the wave before it breaks. When one hesitates, even for just a brief moment, one is inevitably trapped between going and coming, coming and going, committing or not committing, right and wrong, good and evil, black and white. Alive or dead. And Thomas was still alive. And his aliveness was coming back alive.

Thomas reminisced about how he would always lower his head and take on the waves, no matter what, no matter the size or how far they were breaking from shore. He remembered the biggest of waves throwing and thrashing him around until he learned another one of the ocean's secrets. If he didn't have enough time to swim out and crest over the wave, or become one with the wave and bodysurf it to shore, he could always find that special space at the very bottom of the ocean floor, below the turbulence. This was his own safety zone that he could return to in times of trouble or in times of play and he had never talked about this secret with anyone, not even Rashford. He kept this secret to himself, believing that if he shared the secret with anyone, then the magic of that place would disappear. Below the turbulence, he thought, below the turbulence is how to survive. ((yes, this is my truth))

Life was kind of similar to how we deal with waves, Thomas believed. There were those that took chances and thrived on these challenges. Others would quickly rush to shore and never break out of their comfort zone. The third group were the "hesitators" or the ones that would freeze for one reason or another. Fight, flight, freeze. The hesitators would get the worst of it, the worst of the waves. Rock climbing was like this too, and Thomas instinctively knew this as well. He might need to start back slowly, but he couldn't rush to shore anymore. He had to face the challenge. He had to face The Wall. He had to go back to Saddle Mountain.

"What else did Sally say?" Thomas asked.

Part 2: Hippo Lizzy White

Mirko jammed the cork end of his pole deep into the moldable sand, keeping the reel out of harm's way and the rod secure like a beach umbrella.

"What else did Sally say?" Thomas repeated.

"Hippo Lizzy White is born..." Rashford sing-songed uncharacteristically. "That's what she said, Hippo Lizzy White is born. It's in all the news."

"Albino," Thomas spoke out loud.

Thomas removed the sunscreen from his backpack. He streaked the lotion across his shaven body and it melted like butter on a frying pan--the white lotion becoming both sticky and clear. After rubbing the lotion-turned-oil into his body, he plunged his hands into the fine white beach sand, allowing the sand to glove his sticky hands, reminding him of an old Shake & Bake print ad. Good old Shake & Bake chicken. Thomas's body was mostly white now and in some regions it looked opaque, so he had to be especially careful in the sun. He kept his socks on his feet even at the beach and remained cautious of his hands. On occasion, even without exposure to the sun, his hands would blister and exfoliate prematurely, so he had decided if he were to return to climbing that he must wear leather gloves for grip and protection, and cotton-less fine wool socks on his feet. Cotton, while soft, retained too much moisture and would exacerbate his feet blisters. Thin kangaroo leather driving gloves would do the trick for his hands. When he had fingered these gloves over the past few weeks, the kangaroo skin felt more natural than his own hands, as if the thin layer of kangaroo was truly the outer layer of his own skin now--it was both an odd and interesting sensation.

"Is Sally going nuts now too?" Thomas inquired pragmatically.

Rashford simply shrugged as he pointed out at the approaching wave. The rogue wave was about to reach its apex and for a moment it appeared to have a consciousness of its own, momentarily pausing before cresting and then releasing a roar into the wind. White caps crested the top and thundering vibrations sent sunbathers scrambling, abandoning beach towels, lunches, chips, soda pop, and their Best Seller novels. The wave came crashing to shore with ferocity. A well-sculpted and greying elderly gentleman was reading Peter Benchley's 1974 classic, Jaws, as this novel was having a resurgence due to numerous sightings of great whites along the beach in recent summers. The return of great whites to the Cape Cod shores was driven by the return of a favorite prey: grey seals, now protected by federal law. Previously, seals were shot on sight by fishermen who saw them as competition. Now they were numerous and their frequent appearance in pursuit of fish, and playful antics in the surf, entertained swimmers and surfers but also put them in grave danger. Nature's imperative, balancing prey and predator, was at work. Predator and prey, prey and predator, always connected in some symbiotic fashion. Jaws was now a new reality on the Cape Cod shores and not many people saw this coming--fiction becoming reality, versus reality being made into fiction. Three great whites had already been spotted, tagged, and now being tracked as summer commenced. Townspeople were worried not only about their beaches, but about the summer tourist trade that brought a rush of income from Memorial Day through Labor Day.

Saturday. New Hampshire. The cloud formation had changed dramatically from the start of the day's deep and clear blue skies as the sun welcomed a new day rising. The group of four continued up the westside of Saddle Mountain, they had been hiking for close to six hours in total with few breaks along the way. The clouds forming above were elongated like jet engine contrails, slowly shaping and reshaping until they finally and subtly disappeared into the horizon. As they climbed the single-track path, quietly and deliberately through inward-leaning arborvitae trees shaped by the wind, the trees becoming smaller and shorter as they rose in elevation. Rashford led the group, followed by Sally, Thomas, and Jackie-O, each of them shouldering an overnight pack and climbing gear. The terrain on the trip was challenging and Rashford had planned it that way. There was a need for engagement, traction and concentration of the mind, body, and spirit--the way Thomas had taught him over the years. This was Rashford's way of giving back even though he too was aware of their drifting friendship.

"You are moving well," Jackie-O offered as she stopped to catch her breath.

Ever since she had turned twelve, even family members had called her "Jackie-O," for her striking resemblance to a younger Jackie Onassis. She could have been her granddaughter and some mistakenly believed that she was. Ivory skin tone, naturally sculpted eyebrows, thick dark hair and a stoic profile. She had tried bleaching her hair one summer thinking that she could rid the nickname, but this ended up being a disaster and brought on additional attention, attention that she did not seek. Her real name was Melinda Oldfield, and she had been known as "Mindy-O" ever since the 3rd grade when Principal Pelletier had decided to place both Melinda Oldfield and Melinda Gregorian in the same homeroom. "Mindy-O" turned into "Jackie-O" in her teen years and while she objected at first, she simply grew into her name, body, and personality as she somewhat awkwardly matured from childhood into a teenager. She doesn't recall when it happened exactly, but just one day, "Jackie-O" stuck for good; she believes it was in her 13th year.

Thomas was sweating so much that he was glistening in the sun. He offered his water bottle to Jackie-O and waved Rashford and Sally onward. He was never fond of silly nicknames, but he too went along with it just as Jackie-O had, for the name did have a nice ring to it whether out of familiarity or simply from being a sweet-sounding three-syllable name that rolled eloquently off the tongue.

"I love the smell out here, can you feel it?" Thomas inquired.

Jackie-O tried deep breathing. Air in through her nose and exhaled through her mouth in an almost Zen-like slow-motion. She paused for a moment like the cresting rogue wave. "What exactly do you like?" she asked.

Thomas had removed his kangaroo driving gloves and had his pink palms faced skyward. He absorbed the smell throughout his whole body with his palms leading the way. Thomas's true sense of smell had vanished after the accident, which is why some of his other senses were often magnified and sometimes painful, at least that is what Dr. Toloukian had said. Dr. Toloukian had called it a form of vasculitis, which is a disease that causes the wall of a person's blood vessels to thicken, sometimes cutting off blood supply to certain organs and tissues. Why this had happened after the accident was a bit baffling and Thomas had a hard time believing all of the doctors after what happened in the hospital, the Toothpaste Factory, and in the psych ward. The doctors had been wrong, for he had survived. Survived. Life simply didn't feel so black and white anymore and the Toothpaste Factory experience left a bad taste in his mouth as if he was sucking on a roll of pennies and unable to spit them out. He tried not to think about the Toothpaste Factory and instead remembered the doctors during his last bout of physical therapy as they had all been kind, understanding and helpful.

"A forest fire in the distance," Thomas spoke out loud, eyes closed. "The thickness, the denseness, the carbon dust on my tongue. Reminds me of my father burning dry oak leaves in our backyard. I used to love how the acorns would sometimes explode."

"Where are you from, I don't think Sally ever told me," Jackie-O inquired.

"Originally, right here," Thomas pointed to the ground, the woods, the mountain. "New Hampshire. Live free or die." He saluted. He saluted to no one and then realized that the saluting gesture held no real meaning to him, he simply repeated, "Live free or die, live free or die."

Jackie-O palmed her water bottle, took another sip and placed it back into the side pocket of her pack, double-checking to make sure that the spout had been closed. "It's nice to see you out, Thomas."

Thomas and Jackie-O continued up the trail without saying another word. At least an hour had passed and Thomas wanted to catch up to the other two. As Thomas followed Jackie-O he paid particular attention to her movements. Ankles, knees, hips and shoulders all gliding effortlessly, her head perfectly aligned and balanced even in the contorted motions of bouldering. She is graceful like an athlete, Thomas thought. Perhaps the silly nickname was somehow fitting even though he didn't know much about Jackie Onassis and whether she was athletic, but she had appeared to have a certain grace and dignity as seen through the pages of Life Magazine. An exhilarating moment came on suddenly. Instead of the sharp spiky feeling pulsating through his body, Thomas felt the spinal chill that shot down to all his extremities, a wonderful feeling, a sense of relief, and at that moment he realized that this was the first time since the accident that he had interest in another woman. There was a real starkness to this experience and Thomas wondered what Jackie-O was feeling at this very moment. He wondered if the younger "Mindy O" was still somewhere deep inside of her too. Before the accident Thomas always had a knowingness that his younger self was riding shotgun alongside his adult self and in this moment of reflection, he was really missing his younger self; he missed that little boy very much.

Arriving at the marker that read, "Saddle Mountain Camp, Elevation 8,035 feet," Rashford and Sally paused on a large slab of granite left by the glacier that carved and reshaped all the northeast mountains. The granite was as smooth as porcelain, because of glacial abrasion and decades of use by hikers and climbers as a welcome resting point. Rashford looked up into the skyline, the few trees remaining swaying side to side in the cool evening air. The hike had taken them close to eight hours to reach the camp. The surrounding shrubs looked like stunted pine trees with an elaborate above-ground root system that was interlocked with small granite rocks, appearing as if the root system could squeeze water from granite.
