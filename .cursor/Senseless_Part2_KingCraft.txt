<<STRUCTURAL NOTE -- THE SALLY/BPD REVELATION>>

Timothy, I need to talk to you directly here, outside the rewrite, because this is the most important editorial decision in Part 2.

In your original, when Thomas detours to look over The Wall, the narrative suddenly shifts into Sally's POV and delivers the ENTIRE revelation in one paragraph:
- She has BPD
- She let the rope go ON PURPOSE
- She was carrying their baby
- She wanted to scare Thomas
- She didn't expect him to fall
- She's convinced herself it was his fault
- She repeated Humpty Dumpty three times as prescribed by Dr. Kathy
- Sally smiled

That paragraph is the NUCLEAR CORE of this novel. The moment the reader learns Sally caused the fall -- intentionally -- changes EVERYTHING that came before and everything that comes after. It reframes every interaction, every glance, every "Does he know?"

And right now, you're GIVING IT ALL AWAY IN ONE PARAGRAPH.

This is like showing the audience the killer in the first act. The mystery evaporates. The tension deflates. The reader has nowhere to go because you've already told them everything.

Here is what I recommend -- and this is the biggest structural suggestion I'll make:

SPLIT THIS REVELATION ACROSS THE ENTIRE NOVEL.

Part 2 should give the reader HINTS, not answers:
- Thomas looks over The Wall and feels ice water in his veins
- He reads the accident report -- Rashford saved him, Jackie-O helped, but Sally's role is unclear
- "There was always Sally" -- ominous, unexplained
- Sally stares at him. Something in her eyes. A flash of something he can't name.
- She repeated Humpty Dumpty to herself. (The reader doesn't know why yet.)
- Sally smiled.

The BPD diagnosis, the intentional rope release, the pregnancy -- these should surface LATER. Chapter by chapter. Piece by piece. The way Thomas himself is piecing it together through his "knowingness."

The reader should discover Sally's truth at the same pace Thomas does.

Below, I'll write Part 2's version of this scene -- the HINT version, not the reveal version. You decide if this approach works for you.
