His first thought as he awoke was not of unabashed elephants but of pearls. White pearls. There was nothing wrong with pearls, Thomas thought. He remembered dipping his mother's pearls into his mouth when he was much younger. Now he could sift through the beads with his swollen fingers and feel the smoothness -- and more interestingly, feel the degrees of hardness of every object. Every molecule, every atom possessed a hardness, a density, and along with hardness came a degree of softness. Solid, liquid, pearls, people, vapor -- everything had a yin-yang hardness-softness ratio.

One wouldn't think of pearls as being soft. But he could feel the softness within each one, and this yin-yang could be seen in the color too -- the ultra-bright, almost purplish pearls typically the hardest of all. He figured if he could crack open a pearl, he could peel back the layers one by one to reach the original seed. He'd been told that pearls form like an onion, layer upon layer. But his intuition told him a pearl would crack differently -- more like an iceberg breaking away from a glacier.

He tasted Jackie-O's pearl earrings. Salty. Like swimming in the Mediterranean as a younger man. The salt was both repulsive and attractive, and he wasn't sure whether to spit or let it absorb into his tongue. The taste reminded him that he needed to brush his teeth before bed, which triggered a memory of the Toothpaste Factory.

He shuddered in silence. And what followed was a resounding defiance to finally cut ties with the Toothpaste Factory. To let it go for good.

2022, December. The sky was near black. Thomas and Jackie-O skated together on Heard's Pond, foot-thick ice. Embracing.

It made him feel as if he could fly. No resistance. Just a pure uninhibited glide, as if he were born to skate, as if his feet were not touching the ground and he had been given new wings. His heart flowing, gliding -- not beating or pounding as it normally did. A flowing heart feeling. How to explain this change?

When he was gone into another existence, he had visions of either becoming one or being perpetually stuck in the Toothpaste Factory. He knew which feeling he liked better.

The full moon poked out from behind a large night cloud and finally revealed itself. Thomas and Jackie-O skated side by side. Their bodies touching but not knowing where. Perhaps their hands were clasped, but he did not know this for certain. He felt like touching the top of her head and anointing her. He wanted to scream out loud. He wanted to call everyone he knew and tell them of this experience, yet he also wanted to share it with no one. How could this be?

To be so down, so blue, so gone -- but now there was ice beneath his feet, and he could feel the hardness-softness through his steel-bladed skates. Gliding and gliding. Skate to skate. A night so perfectly still that radiated goodness, kindness, togetherness. His skates had become one with his body, with his being. Sad, happy; past, present; dead, alive. THE BIG LESSON OF PART 3:
Your strongest writing happens in TWO modes:

(1) DIALOGUE -- When your characters talk, you disappear. You stop explaining, stop narrating, stop teaching. You let people talk the way people actually talk: around the point, never quite saying it, leaving the real meaning in the gaps. The father phone call. The eyelid scene. The "What else did Sally say?" refrain. These are your best passages and they are ALL dialogue.

(2) STREAM OF CONSCIOUSNESS -- When you drop the narrator entirely and let Thomas's raw mind loose on the page. The Toothpaste Factory passage. The color trip from Part 2. No punctuation, no grammar rules, just the electricity of a mind fighting to stay alive. This is your most original contribution to the manuscript.

Your weakest writing happens in a third mode:

(3) NARRATOR EXPLANATION -- When you step back from the characters and explain their psychology, their history, their medical conditions, their motivations. This is where the backstory dumps live, the research dumps, the "telling" that weakens the "showing."

The novel you're building should lean HARD into modes 1 and 2. Mode 3 should be used sparingly -- like salt. A little makes the meal. Too much kills it.
