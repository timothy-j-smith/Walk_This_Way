CHAPTER 35: "GO YOUR OWN WAY"

---

Chuck Morrison read the card.

"Slot seven. From the southeast corner of Sudbury — where the bacon pays the mortgage — Rumours."

Applause polite at first. Then louder when Patty walked on barefoot, boots in her left hand, Martin case in her right, flannel over the Rumours T-shirt, pig on her clothes whether the room knew it or not.

She kicked the boots under the riser edge and stepped up. Stage wood warm where the lights had been cooking it two hours. Sweat and amp dust and burnt-resistor tang from Nick's set still hanging in the air. She set the Martin on the strap — spruce top darkened with age, scratch along the lower bout catching the gel light the blue of a bruise. Soft case open behind her amp, shoelace through the broken pull tab trailing over the riser.

Robin Bosshard came up left with the bass. Kristy Wesslen took the second mic and the acoustic. Steve Bankuti was already behind the kit — snare stand turned a quarter-inch, sticks crossed on his knee, kick pedal with the hinge that squeaked on beat two like his father's truck door.

Patty leaned into the mic. Did not say anything. Tuned the B string. B was always the one. Nodded at Robin.

Stage-left wing: Nick leaning on a flight case, towel around his neck, hands in his pockets. Stage-right: Jackie standing when he was trying not to be seen, black piano jacket buttoned, hands at his sides.

Patty saw both of them the way you see a thing you are not going to look at twice. She looked back at the neck of the guitar.

---

*Now here you go again, you say you want your freedom.*

She held the word *go* — not show-off long, long enough. C chord clean under it, ring finger on the third fret where it had buzzed Monday in Memorial Congregational and she had laughed out loud at herself. The auditorium came down off the Aerosmith ride. A booster-club mother halfway down the aisle with a tray of Cokes stopped mid-stride and did not put the tray down. Row H: a man put his arm around his wife's shoulder and left it there.

Patty let the held note fall into the next line.

No talk between songs. B string again. Robin on a tuning peg. Kristy sipped water. Steve wiped palms on his jeans. The room waited the way a room waits when it has agreed, without anyone saying so, that the person on stage gets to choose the pace.

She'd cut *Rhiannon* at breakfast. Her mother had set down the coffee pot and Patty had said *not tonight* out loud to the kitchen and her mother had nodded without asking why. Three songs. She'd told the band in the dressing room ten minutes ago. Nobody argued.

---

Count-in for *Silver Springs.*

Learned last week in the basement of Memorial Congregational — Kristy's dubbed cassette, four parish women who came Thursdays to arrange altar flowers standing in the doorway with hands in cardigan pockets, listening. Grandmother in a Sudbury Southeast kitchen in 1954, radio on the Frigidaire. Mother in the same kitchen in 1966 with a baby on her hip.

Long held second chorus — *you'll never get away from the sound of the woman that loves you* — Patty was not thinking about Nick. Not thinking about Jackie. Thinking about the four women in the church basement and the two women in her kitchen. Private inheritance in her hands going out through the mic. The note bloomed. Kristy came in a third below on the second *loves you*, voices braided. Steve's ride shimmered under like water over flat stones.

Row H: the man's face in his hand. House lights half up so the judges could take notes — Patty could see the first fifteen rows as clearly as her own fingers. Chorus fell. She stepped back off the mic. B string one more time.

Robin looked at her. Patty nodded.

---

Steve's count-in — four clean clicks of maple on maple, in her head since November.

Bass on the Lindsey Buckingham pulse. Kristy's rhythm guitar on the one. Patty opened her mouth.

*Loving you / isn't the right thing to do.*

The hum had lived under her breath seven months — in Wentworth's room when Nick's amp was plugged in and Jackie's hands were on the keys, in the barn when the sows sighed, folded with seven letters in the camp notebook. Now it came all the way out. Not a fragment. The whole song, full voice, bare feet on warm wood, Martin D-28 on a hand-me-down strap.

First chorus: the room found her. Second chorus: Kristy's harmony over her like a second sky, Robin walking the Buckingham line, Steve's snare on two and four like a carpenter driving a nail — one hit, clean, no flourish.

One beat: Nick at the back of stage-left. One beat: Jackie at the back of stage-right. Not looking at each other. Both looking at her. She did not hold either look. Back to her fingers.

*You can go your own way. Go your own way.*

Bridge. Last chorus. Last chord. Flat of her right palm on the strings.

One beat of silence.

Then it came.

Not polite. Rows standing in a wave from the middle outward. Four hundred throats, twice that many hands. Something shouted near the back — her name or just a sound. The booster mother clapping with the Coke tray balanced on her forearm.

---

Patty lifted the Martin off her shoulder. Knelt on the riser. Laid it in the case, zipper as far as it would go, shoelace through the pull tab — the knot she used on the barn gate. Boots by the laces in her left hand. Case in her right.

Off stage-left past the monitor, past the flight case Nick had moved, into the dim wing.

Kristy put a hand on her shoulder. Two seconds. Took it off.

Steve did not know what to do with his face. "Hi."

"Hi," Patty said.

She stood with her boots in her hand and listened to the applause do what applause did when it was not going to stop on its own — go on until somebody on the crew brought the house lights up the rest of the way.

Somewhere down the hall the judges' room door was still closed.
