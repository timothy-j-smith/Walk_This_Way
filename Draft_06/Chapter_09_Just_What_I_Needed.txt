ACT I: TOYS IN THE ATTIC

---

CHAPTER 9: "JUST WHAT I NEEDED"

BOSTON. August 11, 2009 — still

Three hours and forty-seven minutes left.

Studio B. Past two a.m. Behind the glass, my engineer — college kid, Bruins hoodie, thinks music starts with a laptop — has queued The Cars. "Just What I Needed." Ric Ocasek's guitar figure bleeds through the monitors at low volume, clean and hungry.

I take a sip of coffee. Cold hours ago. Still drink it.

I've been feeding you 1979 — the Battle, the tape, the end of the runway. Tonight we rewind. Not to the first jam. Not even to the friendship. To the Tuesday in April when two thirteen-year-olds looked at a middle school talent show flyer and thought: why not.

Outside the booth, August still burning on the glass. Inside: Sudbury in spring — cut grass, warm asphalt, thirteen-year-olds with four minutes they hadn't spent yet.

I reach for the cassette on the desk. Don't play it. Not tonight.

Nick told me years later, in Studio C — engineer late, coffee worse, neither of us polite. Jackie on Dan's workbench, feet dangling, Nick fighting a Zeppelin riff on the acoustic. Mrs. Reed had mentioned the talent show in music class. May 15th.

"We should enter," Jackie said.

Nick looked up. "Why would we do that?"

"Because we're good," Jackie said. "Because we're really good, and nobody knows it except us."

An ambulance wails down Tremont. Behind the glass the kid looks up from the board. I lean into the mic.

May 15, 1975. Ephraim Curtis Middle School. Seven-thirty p.m.

The folding chairs are waiting.
