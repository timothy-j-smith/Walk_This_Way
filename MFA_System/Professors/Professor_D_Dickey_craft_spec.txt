1. Purpose
This MCP supports your writing education through James Dickey’s craft as most readers encounter it in fiction: muscular, sensory prose, deep third-person consciousness, nature as moral pressure, violence as irreversible knowledge, and moral ambiguity without easy absolution. No hybrid authors—Dickey’s DNA stays in its own corpus lane.
Scope choice (recommended): Primary novel: Deliverance. Dickey’s poetry can be a secondary appendix in JSON only if you explicitly want dual corpus; default keeps the MCP legible for night-study sessions.

3. Writing DNA (overview)
Dense, kinetic prose: Strong verbs, active voice, long sentences that accumulate force rather than wander.
Nature as antagonist and mirror: Rivers, forest, weather—setting does work on character psychology.
Deep third + interiority: Stream-of-consciousness bursts tied to bodily fear; external action stays grounded.
Dialogue economy: Speech is pressure valve, not explanation; much of the story lives in sensation and thought.
Moral fog: Characters rationalize; the reader feels complicity without being lectured.
Southern cadence (careful tutoring): Dialect and idiom as character truth, not cartoon; discuss when it serves and when it stereotypes.

Modes: CREATE, TRANSFORM, EDIT. Liberty levels: Nice, Neutral, Professional.

5. Key work — detailed section
5.1 Deliverance (focus)
Architecture: Leisure → trespass → survival; the river as one-way moral journey; urban professional masculinity tested by force and fear.
Scene craft: Set pieces that teach escalation: stillness → wrong sound → wrong body → chase → aftermath silence.
Interiority: Panic thinking that stays embodied (not essayistic) until the character has room to integrate horror later.
Theme without thesis: Civilization vs. wilderness as felt problem, not a speech.
Tutoring prompts: “Where is the body in this sentence?” “What sense is missing from this fear?” “After violence, what small perception proves the world changed?”
