1. Purpose
This MCP supports your writing education through Stephen King’s craft as you actually want to use it: granular American realism, situation-born moral pressure, working-class and institutional texture, dialogue that sounds overheard, and revision habits that favor clarity and earned emotion over “literary” varnish.
Explicit learning lane (yours): You respond strongly to New England material truth (weather, work, class, buildings, procedure) and to psychological / moral fiction—not to creature / supernatural horror as a default study path. Misery remains a useful secondary reference (pressure-cooker craft) when you want it; the primary spine for deep novel-level study is “Blind Willie.”

3. Why “Blind Willie” fits what you are trying to learn
“Blind Willie” is King operating closer to moral realism + procedure + shame than to a haunted-house engine:
Ordinary surfaces, extraordinary conscience: Street-level routine, paperwork, performance of identity, and the way public roles hide private judgment.
Guilt as plot fuel, not gore: Vietnam-era moral residue expressed through behavior (what the character does with his hands, his schedule, his lies) more than through spectacle.
Material specificity: The kind of named, tactile dailiness you can study for your own New England / American scenes without importing a horror premise.
Voice control: First-person self-justifying narration—useful for tutoring unreliable or self-exculpating POV without turning the workshop into “write a monster story.”
Tutoring guardrail: Discuss violence, race, class, and penance with ethical framing—craft of depiction, not sensationalism.

4. Writing DNA (overview) — King without the horror mandate
Situation-first storytelling: People in predicaments; let them try to work free; plot emerges from pressure + character choice.
Character drives plot: If a beat feels false, fix motivation, secret, need, not a cleverer monster beat.
Stakes beyond “boo”: Fear of being exposed, fear of irreversible moral compromise, fear of ordinary life cracking—same toolkit, different emotional register.
Voice: Conversational, sensory, regionally grounded when you choose; humor as survival; plain language that still carries.
Pacing: Patient accumulation; sentence rhythm as music; the patient setup that makes a late turn feel inevitable rather than “twisty.”
Revision ethics: Second-draft discipline—tighten, sharpen image transmission, kill darlings when they stop serving the reader.

5. Standard engine (neutral naming)

Modes: CREATE (idea), TRANSFORM (excerpt), EDIT (long passage / chapter). Liberty levels: Nice, Neutral, Professional — nice ≠ dishonest (same critical rule as your full toolbox docs: delivery changes, truth does not).
5.1 Liberty level — quick calibration (example shape)
Use the same scene rated 4/10 thought experiment your longer design uses:
Nice: Name what is alive, then give three concrete upgrades—no inflation.
Neutral: “Works / fails because…” with one revision focus (e.g., cut expository self-defense; add a physical action that contradicts the narrator’s claim).
Professional: Prioritized failure modes + revision plan + honest score if you asked for one.

6. Natural scenarios (workflows)
Create — “I have a seed.” Smallest input (image, sentence, moral trap). Full A.B.S.H. journey in guidance: situation → scene DNA → cut/add pass on a paragraph prototype.
Transform — “Make this paragraph truer.” Medium input. Fast surgical pass: specificity, rhythm, dialogue honesty.
Edit — “This chapter feels soft.” Largest input. Deep pass: pacing, shame/guilt mechanics, ending cost, optional revision report grouped by principle.

7. “Writer’s Toolbox” — four shelves (King craft, non-horror emphasis)
Bottom to top (adapted from your private full design, re-aimed at moral realism / material truth):
Foundation: Strong nouns and verbs; first right word; grammar as clarity, not decoration.
Style: Active voice; death-to-adverbs as default advice (know when to break for voice); conversational line.
Scene craft: Situation tests; dialogue that reveals; description that finishes in the reader’s imagination; performance of self under pressure.
Theme & resonance: Theme as bone—holds the body, not shouted from a bullhorn; emerges from choices and images.
Image transmission (neutral name for “telepathy”): Signal = concrete detail, action, sense, cost; noise = explanation, label emotions, stacked modifiers.

9. Key work — deep section: “Blind Willie”
Use this section as the template depth you will later mirror for other authors’ focus texts in their specs.
Architecture: Present-day ritual vs. past that will not stay buried; the scam/performance as moral machine; city grid as moral geography (different from Maine, but same King obsession with where life happens materially).
POV & reliability: First-person self-story; track what the narrator needs to believe vs. what the reader is allowed to doubt—tutor with precision, not mockery.
Prose moves: Bureaucratic texture, calendar time, body-as-evidence; shame carried in habit rather than in speechifying.
Aftermath-as-craft: Consequences live in next Tuesday, not only in the “big night.”
Tutoring prompts: “What object would betray him if it shifted one inch?” “What kindness does he perform to buy peace?” “Where is he explaining guilt instead of enacting it?”
