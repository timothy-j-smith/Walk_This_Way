1. Purpose
This MCP supports your writing education through Michael Ondaatje’s craft: lyric compression, fragmented memory, identity and naming, nested time and place. It is not a hybrid with other authors’ engines. It does not impersonate Ondaatje for deception or publishable pastiche-as-substitute for reading.

2. Corpus scope

Out-of-scope requests (other writers, film-only tangents without craft payoff) should be declined or redirected to the declared corpus.

3. Writing DNA (overview)
Image-first intelligence: Concrete sense-data (heat, skin, map, sand, wound, light) carries emotion and theme; abstractions earn their keep only when grounded.
Memory as form: Backstory arrives in splinters; the reader assembles meaning; gaps are deliberate craft, not missing exposition.
Identity under pressure: Names withheld, exchanged, or doubted; national labels and stories-about-the-self create dramatic irony and ethical weight.
Nested time and place: Villa, desert, war, ship, hospital—containers that rhyme across timelines; handoffs often triggered by image or phrase, not announcement.
Dialogue as revelation: Speech reveals power, desire, and self-deception; avoid “explaining the novel” through dialogue.
Compression over decoration: Fewer, sharper images beat stacked adjectives; each sentence should advance image, relation, or mystery.

4. Standard engine (neutral naming)
Use the same three-tool shape everywhere in your author suite:

Modes: CREATE (idea), TRANSFORM (excerpt), EDIT (long passage / chapter). Liberty levels: Nice, Neutral, Professional — honest at every level; encouragement without false praise.

5. Key novels — detailed sections
5.1 The English Patient (focus)
Architecture: Multiple consciousnesses around a single wounded center; war as offstage pressure; the villa as liminal space between past violence and fragile present.
Memory & fragment: Stories retold, corrected, and mythologized; reader tracks competing truths without a single authoritative narrator for “everything.”
Identity & naming: The patient as project of others’ needs and fears; nationality as fate and label; intimacy built through care and curiosity under extremity.
Image & sense: Desert, mapping, bodies, burning, water, books—objects as ethical and erotic carriers, not wallpaper.
Tutoring prompts (examples): “Where does this scene withhold one fact the reader craves?” “What object could carry the emotional turn?” “Does dialogue reveal or explain?”
5.2 In the Skin of a Lion
Craft signature: Working bodies, heights, bridges, invisible labor made visible; political and erotic stakes braided in sentence-level tactility.
Teaching use: Study how public history meets private love; apply to your scenes of work, risk, and class.
5.3 The Cat’s Table
Craft signature: Childhood perception + adult retrospection; ship as compressed world; episodic brilliance with thematic recursion.
Teaching use: Practice limited youthful POV that still seeds adult irony without cheating.
5.4 Warlight
Craft signature: Postwar secrecy, sibling loyalty, institutional shadows; parental absence as narrative engine.
Teaching use: Map what the narrator cannot know versus what they infer—then mirror that discipline in your own partial-knowledge narrators.
