CHAPTER 27: "TURN THE PAGE"

---

Three hours into the broadcast. A little under an hour left.

The Heaven Hill paper cup has gone soft at the rim. I refill it with coffee — cold from the pot in the break room, the way it always is after midnight. My hands on the console are steady. Guilt doesn't shake you forever. It just sits down beside you and waits for the cue.

The ON AIR sign holds its red. Behind the glass, the kid finally put the phone down. Quit scrolling about an hour ago. He's watching me the way you watch an old drunk at a bar who's about to say something true.

The Neve 8068 under my fingers was old when I first sat in this chair in 1970. Faders worn smooth at the tips from a thousand hands. Coffee ring from '86 still there, exactly where I left it. Studio B still smells like the forty years of cigarette smoke nobody bothered to shampoo out of the walls, even after the state banned it in 2003. Some of that smoke is mine. Pall Malls, '73 to '76. I quit the year I quit smoking. One vice at a time.

On the wall above the console, the old NAB cart rack — steel frame, thirty slots — is empty. Just the dust shadows where the brown-and-cream carts used to sit. Little gray rectangles. Ghosts of rotation. In '75 I could put my hand on any one of those carts in the dark and know the song by the weight of the tape. Now it's a laptop and a playlist somebody in Texas printed out. The kid runs the laptop. The kid does what the laptop tells him. Nobody in this building has their hand on a song anymore.

The Pink Floyd LP is still cued on the turntable, needle up. Waiting. To my right, the demo cassette: "Demo - Nick/Jackie - un-Authorized - May 1979." Warm from my palm. I haven't put it down since the ON AIR light came on.

---

You heard January. Kowalski's charts. Article 7, Section 3 on the third page of a packet nobody reads until they need an exit. I told you where the tie language came from. I didn't tell you what I did with it.

The Battle of the Bands was my idea.

Not Tom Andrews's. Tom ran it. Tom executed. The seed was mine — pitched with Tom in the winter of '78, when the station was looking for something to do in the spring that wasn't another remote from a dealership parking lot. Regional high school contest. WBCN sponsorship. A recording session for the winners. Find the next kid who could make you feel something before the labels did. That was the whole point. Not a referendum on Sudbury. Not two names I didn't have yet. Discovery.

Sudbury turned out to be a town with a deep bench — more young musicians per square mile than any map would predict. A freshman band went on first and cleared out before the room knew what it was watching. Other kids from that hallway would make careers the room didn't stay for. Nick and Jackie were the ones it couldn't stop watching — and they were the band that never was.

I judged it as WBCN. I still thought I was the woman from the Brickyard who put conviction on the air because it deserved to be heard.

---

I'm going to tell you what I did in the judges' room before you hear them in that auditorium later tonight. Facts only. You render the verdict.

May sixteenth, 1979. Lincoln-Sudbury Regional High School auditorium. Four hundred and forty-three seats. I sat on the panel between two people whose names I have let myself forget, clipboard on my knees, pencil in my hand.

The rubric on that clipboard was mine. Technical execution. Arrangement. Stage presence. Originality. Little boxes. Little numbers. Originality and emotional connection weighted double — the language Tom and I wrote so discovery could win on paper. Also weighted so neither side of Hop Brook was going to win clean on arithmetic alone. I knew that going in. I wrote it that way.

Article 7, Section 3 was on page three. "In the event of a tie in the final round, tied contestants shall share the studio recording session equally." Boring line. Sponsor-friendly. I'd added it at my kitchen table in January, before I knew Nick Johnson's name, before I'd met Jackie Marsh in a garage on Dutton Road in March. I wasn't aiming it at two boys. I was aiming it at a room that might need an exit.

When the scores landed close — when the room was already humming with sides — I did not recuse myself. I did not throw out the clause. I did not pretend I didn't see it.

I wanted them both in Studio C. I wanted the photograph. I wanted the story.

Hop Brook divided those kids. The cafeteria divided them. Dan Johnson's crane cable in November of '77 divided them. Dr. Marsh's kitchen table divided them. I didn't create any of that.

I didn't kill their friendship. But I gave class warfare a microphone.

---

Eleven fifty-one. Nine minutes.

Freddie Mercury's voice comes through the monitors from somewhere deeper than analog. He's been gone eighteen years. Thirty years of medicine too late.

I stayed at WBCN through 1980. Left eventually — the industry moves you or breaks you. I carried a cassette I never played for anyone.

The station brought me back tonight because nobody else wanted it. I drew short straw. Walked in at nine o'clock to play eulogy for my own life. On paper I'm the guest of honor. In the actual room I'm a mirror.

Four decades of rock radio. The brochure says fifty-six because somebody in corporate couldn't count and nobody stopped them. Monday morning, 98.5 starts playing contemporary Christian out of this same transmitter. Jesus on the frequency we used to give Patti Smith. I don't know if that's irony or just America.

---

The Pink Floyd LP waits. "Shine On You Crazy Diamond." The memo says that's how WBCN goes out. Graceful. Elegiac. Ending by committee.

Nick closed his garage bay door last night on Dutton Road. Jackie went to bed in his clothes on Maple Street. Grace stood on a back step with her hand on a storm door she didn't open. The town is asleep. The auditorium is dark. The rules are already on the copier at Lincoln-Sudbury, third page down.

Tomorrow the lights come up.

The engineer behind the glass isn't blinking. I set the coffee cup down next to the demo tape. I lean into the mic.

Turn the page with me.
