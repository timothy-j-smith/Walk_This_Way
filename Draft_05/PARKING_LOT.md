## Scalpel guardrail — rhythm (LOCKED 6/10 — Ch 13 lesson, author confirmed)

**Why it exists:** First drafts combine rooms and dump ideas (school + diner in one chapter, etc.). Scalpel separates architecture; **hose** restores voice. Over-scalpel without hose = **telegram prose** — the failure mode we caught in Ch 13.

When cutting thesis/metaphor, **do not cut sinew**. Telegram prose = stacked subject-verb beats (She saw X. She saw Y. He went inside. Nick went to his room.) — reads like summary, not King/Lehane/Ondaatje register.

**Rule:** pressure on both sides — long sentence loads, short sentence fires (one hammer per scene: *Forty feet.* / *They sat.*). Fragments OK for **catalog** (cafeteria geography, clique texture, diner booth census); not for **plot recap** or **witness beats**.

**Going forward (Ch 14+):** Every brick/scalpel pass ends with **read-aloud**. If three lines sound like a police report, merge before save. Default register = flowing third-person with earned pauses — **not** column paragraphs. Professor O applies this automatically on new chapters.

**Process:** Press on; no full Ch 1–12 rewrite unless read-aloud flags a section. **Pass 2 rhythm spot-check:** Ch 9–11 frame trims. Lesson: `tmp/ch13-rhythm-lesson.md`.
