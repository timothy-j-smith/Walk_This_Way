CHAPTER 34: "TRAIN KEPT A-ROLLIN'"


---

He was in the wings when Jackie's voice cracked.

Nick had been watching the gap between the curtain and the proscenium wall — a slice of stage, the back of Jackie's head at the Yamaha — running the set in his head the way a guy runs a road before he drives it. Then Jackie hit the note in *Somebody to Love* and the note did not come out the way eleven years of lessons had built it to come out. It cracked. The auditorium did the thing four hundred and twenty-seven people do when they are holding one breath together — a half-second where nobody coughed.

Nick felt it in the knuckle of his left hand.

He had come up the side stairs planning to drop a hammer — louder, faster, dirtier — and what had just happened out there was not something a hammer could answer. The set was the set. Four songs.

---

Crown Jewels came off the way bands come off a stage they had just done the thing on.

Jackie passed him six feet out, did not look at him. Max Wesslen touched Nick's shoulder with two fingers going by — not comfort, acknowledgment, the bass player's whole vocabulary. Nick did not try to say what that meant.

"Walking the Line," the stage manager said, not loud. "On deck."

The Tele was Mr. Wentworth's. Late-sixties butterscotch blonde, clearcoat rubbed off where the teacher's forearm had lain for twenty years. Wentworth had met him at the music-room door at seven and handed over the gig bag without taking it out. *Back Friday.* Two steps away, over his shoulder: *The neck'll pull sharp under the lights — keep your ear on the B.* Nick had not trusted his voice to say thanks.

Least-torn jeans. Black t-shirt with nothing on it. Dan's work boots, a size too big, two pairs of socks, the left heel lifting a quarter-inch when he walked.

Nick rolled his neck once. Closed his left hand, opened it. The pads on his fingertips met the strings and stopped. Still his hand.

Stephen Murphy at his shoulder. David behind the Ludwig with the cracked crash riveted by a brass washer. Mark at the Rhodes that buzzed on the B below middle C, thumb already off that key.

They walked through the curtain-break.

---

Heat first.

Four par-cans twelve feet up, putting light down the way a kitchen window in August puts the sun down. Scalp sweating before the monitor. Somebody else's hairspray and gaffer's tape and Lysol on the microphone grille. First three rows pale ovals; past that, dark.

Grace Johnson third row left. Purse on her lap. Both hands on the clasp.

Stephen's Univox hummed, cord curled at his ankle. Nick plugged the Tele into the Fender Twin on loan from the band room. Low E with the side of his thumb. The amp said yes. Volume to seven. Tone to eight. He stepped to the mic. Did not lean in.

"Thanks."

One word. He stepped back. Did not look at the wings where Jackie had gone.

David counted under his breath — *one-two-three-four* — and Nick dropped the opening riff of *Walk This Way* on the downbeat.

Joe Perry's thing — three fingers, pick on the downstroke, muted chug between accents, accent on the two-and. You did not ease into *Walk This Way*. It came in at your waist and lifted. Stephen's bass underneath on the second bar. David's kick tucked in. Mark laid a Rhodes chord on top like a sheet going over a bed.

Nick did not sing the verses. Tyler's syllables lived in a mouth nobody on this stage had. He carried the vocal line on the top two strings, octaved, syllable-tight; the chorus he hit with open A and let the band find him. The northeast section came up on the third bar. A kid in front yelled something that was not a word.

Tag. David hit the crash. The brass washer rattled against the bronze. The cymbal sounded like a trash-can lid. The house was loud.

Nick waited for it to come down. Did not speak. Turned the volume knob a half-notch. Looked at Stephen.

---

Stephen started *Sweet Emotion*.

The descending figure Tom Hamilton built the song on — low G, F-sharp, E, D. Stephen had practiced it in North Sudbury for a month and played it now like he had owned it his whole life. Nick let him walk alone four bars. Mark laid a Rhodes pad under, barely there. The house got quiet because the song wanted quiet.

Then the talk-box line without a talk-box. No Heil on Dutton Road. Tone knob halfway off, borrowed wah under his boot at sweep-middle. Fingered the line on D and G, rocked the toe a quarter-inch — not Perry's gear, a nasal rub, a close-mouthed vowel. It worked because it had to work.

Third row: Grace let go of the purse clasp. Hands flat in her lap.

Nick did not look again. The song demanded the fretboard.

Break and out the far end. Final crash. Feedback off the Tele caught the Twin and squealed high and clean. Nick's palm down on all six strings at the bridge — squeal died mid-note. Palm stung.

He nodded at Mark. Mark laid the one-bar figure for the next one on the Rhodes.

Nick hit the riff.

---

*Train Kept A-Rollin'.*

Not *keep*. **Kept.** Dan had the vinyl label right. Stephen had said *keep* in rehearsal twice and Nick hadn't corrected him because rehearsal wasn't the point.

The Aerosmith version — heavier, meaner, stutter on the downbeat Perry built off Jeff Beck and made his own. Stutter in the pick hand. Heel of the right palm muted, riff out of the Twin like sheet metal hit with a tire iron in rhythm. Stephen locked on. David's kick doubled the stutter. Mark stayed off the B.

Halfway through the second verse the tremor started in his left pinkie — the shake from Sunday rehearsals he hadn't told anybody about. He fingered it out by tightening his grip a half-pound. The train kept rolling. Copper at the back of his throat.

Double-time out. David's crash. The cracked cymbal rattled again. Nick stood with the Tele against his hip, chest heaving.

Three down. One left.

---

Mark played the piano intro of *Dream On* on the Rhodes that buzzed on the B below middle C.

Not a piano — a bell in the top, a roll an upright didn't roll. Mark's version on borrowed gear with a buzzing B, the thing Nick had been hearing since the summer he was fourteen in the garage on a nicked-up first album.

Nick came in on the open A.

No singing. Vocal line on the Tele, single-note, clean, no wah — Wentworth's butterscotch handling the melody the way Grace handled a plate she didn't want to break. Stephen to whole notes. David to the ride. Mark off the B.

His eye traveled when he didn't want it to. Third row left. Navy dress. Purse in her lap. Hands flat on the fabric the way they had been at the kitchen table the morning after Beechwood, waiting for the next thing.

She was looking at him. Not looking away.

Four hundred people and he held her there for the length of the solo's pickup bar. Any longer and he would not finish. He looked back down at the fretboard.

The solo. The Dan solo.

Dan in the garage one Sunday when Nick was fourteen, putting fingering into his left hand with a hand that had hefted ninety-pound bags of Quikrete and turned a wrench on Fords since 1952. *Thumb anchored, Nicky. Bend on the second. Breathe on the pause.*

Slow. Dan's way. The bend on the second fret bit the fingertip — Tele strings lighter than the acoustic's, cut different. He let the bite happen.

Out of the solo. Band rode the chord. Mark on the Rhodes. Stephen long. David wash on the crash.

Nick did not sing *Dream on, dream on*. No Steven Tyler on the Lincoln-Sudbury stage. He played the last chorus melody on the top string, clean, through the par-can haze, down off the high note to the A. Mark closed on the Rhodes chord. David let the ride die on its own.

Half a second the house was quiet the way Jackie had made it quiet.

Then it came.

Not the *Walk This Way* applause — something else. Wide, uneven, northeast loud first, then spreading. Nick stood with the Tele against his hip. Did not lift his arm. Nodded once, not at anybody in particular.

He unplugged. Laid the Tele flat on the Twin, strings up, the way Wentworth had shown him. Stephen said something with *Nicky* in it and clapped his shoulder.

Nick walked off stage left.

---

The stage manager had a clipboard and a headset on one ear. Did not look up.

"Ready Rumours," he said into the headset.

Nick walked past. Left hand shaking a small shake. Both hands in his jeans pockets.

Stage-right wing: Jackie standing the way Jackie stood when he was trying not to be seen, black piano jacket still buttoned, hands at his sides. Forty feet of velvet and gaffer tape between them.

Nick did not look twice.

Somewhere outside the lobby doors thunder finally broke over Sudbury — not applause, not a train, just weather moving in after a day that had been holding its breath since seven o'clock.
