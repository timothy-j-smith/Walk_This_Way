### ⚠️ STRATEGIC MISALIGNMENT (Issues Against Goals):

#### 1. PROSE IS TOO POLISHED
**Target:** 6/10 "Solid foundation"  
**Actual:** 7.5-8/10 polished prose

**Examples:**

**Line 22-23:**
> "Outside the booth windows, Boston glitters in the October darkness like broken glass in a parking lot—all glass towers and gentrification..."

This is beautiful, literary prose—but TOO crafted for "accessible language with occasional poetic touches."

**Line 56:**
> "The vinyl spines catch the booth's amber light like tombstones in a graveyard of sound."

Gorgeous metaphor—but too refined for "prioritize momentum over perfection."

**ISSUE:** This reads like final-draft polish, not foundation work.

#### 2. NO ACTUAL SCENES (Critical Structural Problem)

**Goal:** "Complete scenes, establish characters, show class divisions through concrete details"

**What Chapter 01 Delivers:** 
- 2,156 words of setup, framing, and promises
- Zero dramatic scenes
- No scenes between Nick and Jackie
- No Battle of the Bands action
- No moment of connection or conflict
- Minimal dialogue (one line from program director)

**DIAGNOSIS:** This is a 2,000-word introduction to a story we haven't started yet.

#### 3. MOMENTUM STALLS (Pacing Issue)

**Goal:** "Keep scenes moving forward" and "Prioritize momentum over perfection"

**Problem:** Nothing moves. Maxanne:
- Sits in booth
- Talks about what she's GOING to tell us
- Describes bourbon and headphones
- Gives backstory

The chapter ends with: "But first, we need to talk about a garage on Dutton Road..." 

**This means we STILL haven't started the actual story.**

#### 4. REPETITION (Technical Issue)

Steven Tyler bourbon gift mentioned TWICE:
- **Line 20:** Full description with note
- **Line 104:** Repeated almost verbatim

Suggests chapter assembled from different drafts without full integration.

#### 5. SHOWING VS. TELLING (Mixed Results)

**Good Showing (Lines 64-66):**
> "His mother worked doubles at Friendly's, came home smelling like fryer oil and other people's happiness..."

Physical, specific, evocative. ✅

**Telling Emotions (Line 32):**
> "The good kind that makes you remember why you started drinking in the first place."

Naming the emotion/effect instead of letting us feel it. ❌

## WHAT THIS CHAPTER ACTUALLY IS

This is a **PROLOGUE** or **TEASER**, not Chapter 01 of the novel proper.

It's the **frame around the story**, not the story itself.

**Structure:**
- **This chapter:** Maxanne in 2009, setting up the story

**Analogy:** Imagine if Stephen King's *The Body* started with 2,000 words of the narrator explaining he'll tell you about that summer. Readers would say, "Great, so... tell me."
