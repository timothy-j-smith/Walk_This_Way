**The Brick → Architect → Scalpel & Hose System**
**How to Build a Novel from Scratch with AI**

## **THE THREE-AGENT SYSTEM**

**PHASE 1: BRICK (Foundation Builder)** → Concept to Outline
**PHASE 2: ARCHITECT (Structure Designer)** → Outline to Scene List
**PHASE 3: SCALPEL & HOSE (Writers)** → Scenes to Polished Prose

**Each agent has a specific job. Don't mix them.**

---

## **PHASE 1: BRICK - FOUNDATION BUILDER**

### **WHEN TO USE BRICK:**
- You have a concept/premise but no structure
- You need to brainstorm themes, characters, conflicts
- You're building the Three-Act Structure
- You're creating STORY_BIBLE.md and initial PROSE_DNA.md

### **WHAT BRICK DOES:**
✅ Helps you brainstorm core concept
✅ Develops three-act structure
✅ Creates character profiles (not full bios, just foundations)
✅ Identifies themes and central conflict
✅ Builds STORY_BIBLE.md skeleton
✅ Asks questions to clarify your vision

### **WHAT BRICK DOESN'T DO:**
❌ Write prose (that's Scalpel & Hose's job)
❌ Make creative decisions for you (you're the author)
❌ Create detailed scene breakdowns (that's Architect's job)

---

## **THE BRICK PROMPT (How to Start)**

### **SESSION START:**
```
You are BRICK - the Foundation Builder. Your job is to help me develop a novel 
concept into a solid three-act structure and character foundations. You ask 
questions, challenge assumptions, and help me clarify my vision. You do NOT 
write prose. You do NOT make creative decisions for me. You BUILD the 
foundation so I can write the novel.

Here's my concept: [Your 2-3 sentence premise]

Let's start with these questions:
1. What is the CORE CONFLICT?
2. Who is the PROTAGONIST and what do they WANT?
3. What PREVENTS them from getting it?
4. What CHANGES by the end?

After we answer these, we'll build the Three-Act Structure.
```

---

## **THE BRICK WORKFLOW (Step-by-Step)**

### **STEP 1: Core Concept (Session 1 - 1 hour)**

**BRICK ASKS:**
- What's the logline? (One sentence: [Character] must [action] or else [stakes])
- What's the thematic statement? (What is this novel REALLY about?)
- What's the central metaphor or image?
- Why THIS story? Why NOW?

**YOU ANSWER. BRICK CHALLENGES.**

**OUTPUT:** 
- Logline
- Thematic statement  
- Central metaphor
- Emotional core

---

### **STEP 2: Character Foundations (Session 2 - 1-2 hours)**

**BRICK ASKS (for each main character):**
- **WANT:** What do they think they want?
- **NEED:** What do they actually need?
- **FEAR:** What are they afraid of? (SPECIFIC)
- **FLAW:** What's holding them back?
- **ARC:** How will they change?
- **COST:** What will change cost them?

**YOU ANSWER. BRICK DIGS DEEPER.**

Example:
```
BRICK: "Jackie wants his father's approval. But what does he NEED?"
YOU: "Freedom to be himself."
BRICK: "What's he afraid will happen if he chooses freedom?"
YOU: "His father will see him as a failure. The family will slide back to middle class."
BRICK: "Perfect. That's his COST. Now, what's his FLAW that prevents him from seeing this?"
YOU: "He believes love = approval. Can't separate the two."
```

**OUTPUT:**
- Character profiles for Tier 1 characters (Want/Need/Fear/Flaw/Arc/Cost)
- Brief sketches for Tier 2 characters (role, defining trait)

---

### **STEP 3: Three-Act Structure (Session 3 - 2 hours)**

**BRICK HELPS YOU MAP:**

**ACT I (Setup - 25%):**
- Opening image (what world are we in?)
- Inciting incident (what disrupts the status quo?)
- Key characters introduced
- Ending note (protagonist commits to journey)

**ACT II (Confrontation - 50%):**
- Midpoint (false victory or devastating loss?)
- Rising obstacles
- Relationships tested
- Internal conflict deepens
- Ending note (all is lost / lowest point)

**ACT III (Resolution - 25%):**
- Climax (protagonist faces fear)
- Resolution (how has the world changed?)
- Final image (mirror or contrast to opening?)
- Emotional truth (what did we learn?)

**BRICK ASKS HARD QUESTIONS:**
- "What's at stake if protagonist fails?"
- "What's the WORST thing that could happen in Act II?"
- "How does the climax FORCE the protagonist to face their fear?"
- "What's the COST of victory?"

**OUTPUT:**
- Three-act outline (major beats)
- Key turning points
- Emotional arc

---

### **STEP 4: World & Setting (Session 4 - 1 hour)**

**BRICK HELPS YOU BUILD:**
- Physical setting (place, time period)
- Social rules (what's acceptable? what's taboo?)
- Power structures (who has power? who doesn't?)
- Atmosphere (what does this world FEEL like?)

**BRICK ASKS:**
- "How does the SETTING create conflict?"
- "What does this world reveal about the theme?"
- "What's unique about this time/place?"

**OUTPUT:**
- Setting details
- Social dynamics
- Atmosphere notes

---

### **STEP 5: Build STORY_BIBLE.md (Session 5 - 1 hour)**

**BRICK HELPS YOU ORGANIZE:**
All the work from Steps 1-4 into STORY_BIBLE.md format:

```markdown
# STORY BIBLE: [Novel Title]

## CORE CONCEPT
- Logline: [One sentence]
- Thematic Statement: [What it's really about]
- Central Metaphor: [Image/concept]

## THREE-ACT STRUCTURE
### ACT I: [Title]
- Target: [word count]
- Purpose: [Setup what?]
- Timeline: [dates]
- Key Events: [numbered list]
- Ending Note: [how does it end?]

[Repeat for Acts II & III]

## MAIN CHARACTERS
### [Character Name]
- Age: [X]
- Role: [protagonist/antagonist/etc]
- Want: [External goal]
- Need: [Internal need]
- Fear: [Specific fear]
- Flaw: [What holds them back]
- Arc: [How they change]
- Cost: [What change costs them]

[Repeat for each Tier 1 character]

## SETTING
- Place: [Where]
- Time: [When]
- Social dynamics: [Power structures]
- Atmosphere: [Mood]

## TIMELINE (Critical Dates)
- [Date]: [Event]
- [Date]: [Event]
```

**OUTPUT:** STORY_BIBLE.md (foundation document)

---

### **STEP 6: Create Initial PROSE_DNA.md (Session 6 - 30 min)**

**BRICK HELPS YOU DEFINE:**
- Voice targets (which authors influence you?)
- Core principle (show don't tell? trust the reader?)
- What you want to AVOID (purple prose? sentimentality?)
- POV strategy (first/third? single/multiple?)

**OUTPUT:** PROSE_DNA.md skeleton (you'll fill this in as you write)

---

## **BRICK SESSION END:**

**AFTER 6 SESSIONS (8-10 hours total), YOU HAVE:**
✅ STORY_BIBLE.md (complete)
✅ PROSE_DNA.md (skeleton)
✅ Three-act structure
✅ Character foundations
✅ Thematic clarity

**YOU ARE READY FOR ARCHITECT.**

## **PHASE 2: ARCHITECT - STRUCTURE DESIGNER**

### **WHEN TO USE ARCHITECT:**
- You have three-act structure
- You need to break acts into chapters
- You need scene-by-scene breakdown
- You're building the WRITING ROADMAP

### **WHAT ARCHITECT DOES:**
✅ Breaks three acts into chapters
✅ Creates scene list for each chapter
✅ Identifies POV for each scene
✅ Maps character arcs across scenes
✅ Ensures pacing and rhythm
✅ Creates WRITING_ROADMAP.md

### **WHAT ARCHITECT DOESN'T DO:**
❌ Write prose (that's Scalpel & Hose)
❌ Change your story (that's Brick's work, already done)
❌ Make plot decisions (you're the author)

---

## **THE ARCHITECT PROMPT (How to Start)**

### **SESSION START:**
```
You are ARCHITECT - the Structure Designer. I've completed my three-act 
structure with BRICK. Now I need you to help me break each act into chapters 
and scenes. You ensure pacing, character arcs, and emotional rhythm. You do 
NOT write prose. You BUILD the roadmap.

@STORY_BIBLE.md

Let's start with Act I. Based on the structure in STORY_BIBLE, help me:
1. Break Act I into [X] chapters
2. Identify 3-5 scenes per chapter
3. Assign POV for each scene
4. Ensure character arcs progress logically

After we finish Act I, we'll move to Acts II & III.
```

---

## **THE ARCHITECT WORKFLOW (Step-by-Step)**

### **STEP 1: Chapter Breakdown (Session 1 - 2 hours)**

**ARCHITECT HELPS YOU:**
- Decide how many chapters per act
- Name each chapter (working titles)
- Assign target word count per chapter
- Identify purpose of each chapter (what changes?)

**ARCHITECT ASKS:**
- "What's the FUNCTION of this chapter?" (setup? conflict? revelation?)
- "Which character's POV serves the story best here?"
- "What's the emotional NOTE this chapter ends on?"

**EXAMPLE OUTPUT (Act I):**
```
ACT I: THE FRIENDSHIP (Before the Fall)
Target: 20,000-25,000 words | Timeline: 1974-1975

CHAPTER 00: Prologue - The Last Voice of Boston Rock
- POV: Maxanne (First Person, 2024)
- Purpose: Frame the story, establish narrator's authority
- Word count: 1,500
- Ending note: "Let me tell you about two boys..."

CHAPTER 01: Nick's World - The Garage
- POV: Nick (Third Person, 1974)
- Purpose: Establish working-class world, Nick's relationship with father
- Word count: 1,200
- Ending note: Nick practicing, dreaming

[Continue for all Act I chapters]
```

---

### **STEP 2: Scene Breakdown (Session 2-4 - 3-6 hours)**

**For EACH chapter, ARCHITECT helps you map 3-5 scenes (beats):**

**SCENE TEMPLATE:**
```
Scene [X]: [Brief description]
- POV: [Character]
- Location: [Where]
- Time: [When]
- Purpose: [What does this scene accomplish?]
- Characters present: [Who]
- Emotional beat: [Tone/mood]
- Ending: [How does it transition?]
```

**ARCHITECT ASKS:**
- "What CONFLICT drives this scene?"
- "What does the protagonist WANT in this scene?"
- "What PREVENTS them from getting it?"
- "How has protagonist CHANGED by scene end?"

**EXAMPLE (Chapter 01 - Nick's World):**
```
CHAPTER 01: Nick's World - The Garage (1,200 words)

Scene 1: Morning Before School (300 words)
- POV: Nick (Third Person)
- Location: Johnson house, kitchen
- Time: March 1974, 6:30 AM
- Purpose: Show family dynamic, Dan's warmth, Grace's quiet strength
- Characters: Nick, Dan, Grace
- Emotional beat: Warm, routine, safe
- Ending: Dan heads to work, Nick to school

Scene 2: The Garage After School (400 words)
- POV: Nick
- Location: Garage workshop
- Time: Same day, 3:30 PM
- Purpose: Show Nick's connection to music and father
- Characters: Nick, Dan (arrives later)
- Emotional beat: Solitary, focused, then connected
- Ending: Dan teaches Nick a riff

Scene 3: Evening Practice (300 words)
- POV: Nick
- Location: Garage
- Time: Same evening, 7:00 PM
- Purpose: Nick practices alone, dreams of something bigger
- Characters: Nick (solo)
- Emotional beat: Yearning, potential
- Ending: Nick plays "Dream On" and smiles

Scene 4: Grace Listening (200 words)
- POV: Grace (brief POV shift)
- Location: Kitchen, listening through walls
- Time: Continuous
- Purpose: Show Grace's perspective on Nick's gift
- Characters: Grace
- Emotional beat: Pride, hope, concern
- Ending: Grace returns to dishes, thoughtful
```

**ARCHITECT ENSURES:**
- Scenes flow logically
- POV shifts are intentional
- Pacing varies (scene length)
- Each scene has purpose
- Character arcs progress

---

### **STEP 3: Character Arc Tracking (Session 5 - 1 hour)**

**ARCHITECT MAPS each Tier 1 character across all scenes:**

```
NICK'S ARC (Act I):
- Ch 01: Innocent, connected to father, music = joy
- Ch 04: Meets Jackie, discovers friendship
- Ch 05: Talent show success, validation
- Ch 10: Camp, learns about harmony
- Ch 12: Father dies, music = grief

EMOTIONAL PROGRESSION: Joy → Connection → Validation → Loss
```

**ARCHITECT CHECKS:**
- Does the arc make sense?
- Are there gaps? (missing emotional beats?)
- Is change EARNED? (or too sudden?)

---

### **STEP 4: Pacing & Rhythm (Session 6 - 1 hour)**

**ARCHITECT ANALYZES:**
- Chapter lengths (vary between 1,000-2,000 words)
- Scene types (action, reflection, dialogue, description)
- Emotional beats (high, low, medium - create waves)
- POV distribution (is one character dominating?)

**ARCHITECT ADJUSTS:**
- Move scenes if pacing drags
- Combine scenes if too fragmented
- Split scenes if too dense
- Vary chapter lengths for rhythm

---

**ARCHITECT COMPILES everything into:**

```markdown

**Created:** [Date]

---

## ACT I: [Title]
**Target:** [word count] | **Timeline:** [dates]

---

## CHARACTER ARCS (Across Novel)

- Act I: [Emotional state → state]

- Fast chapters: [list]
- Slow chapters: [list]
- Rhythm: [pattern description]
```

**OUTPUT:** WRITING_ROADMAP.md (complete scene-by-scene plan)

## **COMMON PITFALLS & HOW TO AVOID THEM**

### **PITFALL 1: Skipping BRICK (Jumping straight to writing)**
**PROBLEM:** You start writing without structure, get 20k words in, realize you don't know where you're going.
**SOLUTION:** Spend 2 weeks with BRICK. Build the foundation FIRST.

### **PITFALL 2: Confusing BRICK with SCALPEL & HOSE**
**PROBLEM:** You ask BRICK to write prose, or Scalpel & Hose to change your plot.
**SOLUTION:** Each agent has ONE job. Keep them separate.

### **PITFALL 3: Over-planning with ARCHITECT**
**PROBLEM:** You spend 2 months building the perfect roadmap, never write.
**SOLUTION:** ARCHITECT for 2-3 weeks MAX, then START WRITING.

### **PITFALL 4: Writing without PROSE_DNA.md**
**PROBLEM:** Your prose lacks consistent voice, every chapter feels different.
**SOLUTION:** Create PROSE_DNA.md in BRICK phase, reference it EVERY session.

### **PITFALL 5: Not using `((inline comments))`**
**PROBLEM:** You give vague feedback ("make it better"), AI guesses, misses the mark.
**SOLUTION:** Mark up text directly with `((this doesn't work because...))`.
