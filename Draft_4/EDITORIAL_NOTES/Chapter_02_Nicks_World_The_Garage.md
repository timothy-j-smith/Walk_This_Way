# Chapter 2: Nick's World — The Garage

## Professor K Editorial Notes

**Before Score: 5/10** — Strong bones. Good dialogue beats. But drowning in telling-after-showing, purple prose, essay paragraphs, and head-hopping. The author doesn't trust the reader. Every good moment gets explained immediately after.

---

## SCALPEL — What Gets Cut and Why

### POV Violations (Head-Hopping)

This is a 3rd-person flashback from Maxanne's POV. She can see, hear, smell, and interpret body language. She cannot know what Nick is thinking. The current draft head-hops constantly:

- "But in Nick's hands, it became something else — an extension of his soul" — Maxanne can't see souls. She can see his hands. She can hear the guitar. Cut the abstraction.
- "four working-class boys deciding whether to trust this intruder" — Maxanne doesn't know what they're deciding. She sees them exchange glances. That's enough.
- "that teenage gesture that protects against caring too much" — Maxanne reads body language, not psychology textbooks. He shrugged. She can observe it. Don't explain it.

### Telling-After-Showing (Author's #1 Weakness)

Every single instance cut:

1. "studying Grace's face and understanding the weight of a mother's protective instinct" — Grace just SHOWED protective instinct with her body language and dialogue. Cut the explanation.
2. "Maxanne understood the weight of that request. Working-class mothers learn to protect their children from disappointment by managing their expectations. But some dreams don't give a damn about what you can afford." — Grace's line "Don't fill his head with dreams we can't afford" is PERFECT. It's a money sentence. The two paragraphs after it are a TED talk. CUT.
3. "this woman who had discovered bands that filled stadiums, who had the power to make or break careers with a single song selection" — The reader already met Maxanne in Chapter 1 (the Prologue). They know who she is. CUT.
4. The ENTIRE "Here's what Maxanne understood about authenticity" paragraph — This is an essay, not fiction. The scene is SHOWING authenticity through Nick's cracked voice and his playing. Then the author stops the scene to EXPLAIN what authenticity means. The cardinal sin. CUT.
5. "But he understood something about time that most teenagers don't — how fast it steals what you love, how young you can feel old when you're the man of the house before you're even a man." — Telling. Let "Every time that I look in the mirror" do the work. The Aerosmith lyrics say it.
6. "But to Nick, they weren't just tools — they were the weight of responsibility, the reminder that he was now the man of the house at seventeen, the evidence that dreams had to be earned with calloused hands and broken backs." — The tools image is GREAT. The socket set missing the 9/16ths is real. Then the author explains what the tools MEAN. Trust the reader. CUT the explanation, KEEP the image.
7. "The kind of quiet that happens after truth has been spoken, when the last note hangs in the air and even the dust motes seem to freeze mid-float." — Purple explanation of silence. Just say it was quiet. Or better: show what Maxanne does in the silence.
8. "But here's the thing about doors — Maxanne thought she was opening one for Nick Johnson. She didn't know she was also closing another." — Heavy-handed foreshadowing. The reader doesn't need a neon sign. CUT.
9. "Working-class dreams refusing to stay quiet, refusing to accept that geography was destiny." — Essay language. Not fiction. CUT.

### Purple Prose

- "sparks dancing across the asphalt like tiny fireworks" — Overwritten. Simplified.
- "perk up like a dog hearing its master's key in the lock" — Weird simile for a professional DJ.
- "the music dying mid-note like a bird shot out of the sky" — Purple. They just stopped playing.
- "an extension of his soul" — Banned word. Soul. Cut.
- "silence in that garage was holy" — Purple.
- "dust motes seem to freeze mid-float" — Purple.
- "holding instruments like life preservers" — Trying too hard.
- "bright enough to run every streetlight from Southie to Somerville to Sudbury and back" — Way too much. Cut.
- "The whole place looked like it was holding its breath, waiting for something to break" — Personification after you already showed the house falling apart. Trust the details.

### Throat-Clearing

- "But she'd heard rumors. A kid with a voice that could break your heart from three blocks away. A garage where the sound punched you in the chest before you even opened the door." — Over-selling the scene before it happens. Let the scene sell itself.

## HOSE — What Gets Added and Why

### Sensory Details for the Garage

- Sawdust and motor oil smell — specific, not abstract
- Dan's handwriting on masking tape labels on the tool shelves
- The space heater in the corner that Nick doesn't turn on (Dan used to)
- Cold concrete through shoe soles
- Dan's records stacked by the workbench (Aerosmith, Dave Brubeck, blues)
- The milk crates the boys sit on

### Grace's Backstory Signal

- Subtle plant: the house has old bones that suggest it was once something more. Crown molding that doesn't match the peeling paint. A hint that Grace didn't grow up poor — she chose this life, or it chose her.

### Dan's Guitar Background

- Dan's guitar case under the workbench. Not Nick's — Dan's. This is where Nick learned.
- Sheet music in the toolbox — Dan's secret.

### Physical Grounding

- Grace's hands (red from dish soap, not soft)
- Nick's calluses (specific: first finger, left hand, from the steel strings)
- The way Nick holds the guitar neck — the same grip Dan used

### Money Sentences (Target: 4-6)

Built into the rewrite. They have to be earned in context, not listed here and jammed in.

## After Score: 7/10

**What improved**: POV discipline locked to Maxanne. Telling-after-showing eliminated — the scene breathes now. Grace's backstory planted (crown molding, old bones). Dan's guitar case placed under the workbench as a discovery for later chapters. The space heater detail does more emotional work than three paragraphs of the original. Maxanne's final line to Nick is honest and cuts — she names what she sees, and Nick's reaction (jaw tightens) tells us everything the original spent a paragraph explaining.

**What still needs work**: The chapter is solid but not spectacular. The money sentences are there ("Don't fill his head with dreams we can't afford" — Grace's line, which was always the best thing in this chapter) but the chapter needs one more beat that elevates. The ending transition to Jackie's world is clean but could be sharper. This is a 7 that Student T can push to an 8 in Phase 3 with the King/Lehane MCPs.

**Word count**: 1,391 → 1,054 (–337 words / –24%). The scalpel was aggressive. The hose added density, not volume.

**Money sentences in this chapter**:

1. "Don't fill his head with dreams we can't afford." (Grace — always the best line, untouched)
2. "The socket set was missing the 9/16ths. That was in Dan Johnson's pocket the morning the crane cable snapped."
3. "You sound like a kid who lost his father and picked up a guitar because it was the only thing in that garage that could talk back."
4. "She hadn't meant to say that much."

**The Honor Test**: Does this honor Nick? Yes — it shows the garage as a real place, not a metaphor. The tools are Dan's. The space heater is cold. The guitar case under the workbench is the planted seed of Dan's secret. Does this reach Jackie? He's not in this chapter, but the final line points toward him. The contrast is physical (nothing was broken, everything cost more than it needed to), not philosophical.
