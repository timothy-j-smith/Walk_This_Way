# Chapter 4: The Geography of Sudbury

## Professor K Editorial Notes

**Before Score: 4/10** — Strong raw material. Terrible execution. This is a college essay about class geography, not a chapter in a novel. No characters act. No scene exists. It's 1,200 words of worldbuilding with no human heartbeat. The Dump details are great. The mini-bike trails are great. The Cavicchio history is great. All of it is buried in thesis-statement prose and "here's what this MEANS" explanations.

---

## SCALPEL — What Gets Cut and Why

### The Entire Structure

The chapter as written is omniscient-narrator essay. The novel doesn't have an omniscient narrator. Maxanne's booth voice (1st person present) or close 3rd-person flashback are the only POVs. This chapter has neither.

**Decision**: Convert to Maxanne booth narration. She's back on the microphone, painting Sudbury for her radio audience. This makes the worldbuilding feel earned — she's a DJ telling a story, not an author delivering a lecture. It also gives us her voice between the two flashback scenes we just had (garage, recital), which is structurally sound.

### Telling-After-Showing

Almost the entire chapter is telling. Specific cuts:
- "each section believing it represents the real America while the others are somehow less authentic" — thesis statement
- "These two lines—water and waste—created four distinct Sudburys" — essay topic sentence
- "It was the perfect metaphor for a town where paths crossed but never really connected" — naming the metaphor kills it
- "The mini-bike trails weren't just about recreation. They were about different definitions of freedom." — Essay thesis. Cut. The reader gets it from the details.
- The entire three-paragraph ending essay about the Battle of the Bands being a referendum

### Over-Explanation

- The Cavicchio paragraph explains why land matters after showing it
- The dump paragraph explains democratic principles after showing Saturday mornings
- The railroad paragraph explains the metaphor after establishing the physical detail

### Preserved Material

- Hop Brook as divider
- The Dump and Saturday democracy
- Cavicchio pig farm and the Ford story
- Mini-bike trails (two-stroke exhaust, angry hornet sound, mud)
- Conservation land as contested territory
- The four quadrants (compressed, shown through detail not explanation)

## After Score: 7/10

**What improved**: No longer an essay. Now a Maxanne booth chapter — she's on the air, painting Sudbury for her listeners. The worldbuilding earns its place because a character is delivering it with personality, bourbon, and radio rhythm. The four quadrants are compressed from paragraphs to lines. The Dump and mini-bikes get sensory treatment. The transition to the belt buckles chapter is clean: "Two kids. Two belt buckles. And a cafeteria where, for about five minutes, none of those lines mattered."

**Key structural change**: Converted from omniscient narrator essay to Maxanne 1st person present tense (booth chapter). This gives the reader a breath of Maxanne's voice between the two flashback chapters (Nick's garage, Jackie's recital), which is structurally correct per the dual-timeline design.

The Phish/Mike Gordon line from research could be woven in here (L-S connection) but would interrupt Maxanne's flow — save for a later booth chapter.
