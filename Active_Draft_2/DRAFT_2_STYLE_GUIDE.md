## 1.1. CORE PRINCIPLE: DENNIS LEHANE IS YOUR NORTH STAR

**Why Lehane works for this novel:**
- Working-class Boston voice (authentic to Nick's world)
- Short, punchy sentences (accessible, propulsive)
- Class shown through physical details, not explained
- Sensory specifics over abstractions
- Restraint over excess
- Truth over poetry

**Secondary influences:**
- **Richard Russo**: Patient pacing, dignity in small-town working-class life, objects that reveal character
- **Stephen King**: Page-turner accessibility, ordinary details building dread, teenage voice that rings true

---

## 1.2. THE THREE COMMANDMENTS

### 1.2.1. SHOW CLASS, DON'T EXPLAIN IT

❌ **Purple/Over-Explained:**
> "Grace embodied the dignity of the working class, her calloused hands a testament to years of honest labor in service of her family's survival against the crushing machinery of economic inequality."

✅ **Lehane-Style (Physical Details):**
> "Grace's hands smelled like fryer oil. The Friendly's apron hung on a hook by the door, ketchup stain near the left pocket. She'd worked a double."

**The Rule:** Class is shown through:
- What people smell like
- What's in their refrigerator
- How they pronounce certain words
- What they can't afford
- The condition of their belongings
- Where they shop
- What they eat for dinner

---

### 1.2.2. ELIMINATE PURPLE PROSE COMPLETELY

**Purple Prose = Abstract, Flowery, Over-Written**

❌ **BANNED FOREVER:**
- "Electricity crackled in the air"
- "The frequency of their connection hummed"
- "His soul resonated with the music"
- "Her voice soared like angels"
- "The garage pulsed with raw energy"
- "Calloused hands that spoke of honest work"
- "He played until his hands bled"
- "The music became one with his very being"

✅ **GROUNDED ALTERNATIVES:**

Instead of "electricity":
- "The wrench rattled against the vise grip when Nick hit E minor"
- "Hair stood up on Jackie's arms"
- "Grace's coffee went cold while she listened"

Instead of "frequency/humming":
- "Dan's toolbox, 82 Hz when certain chords were played"
- "The garage door hummed. Needed oil."
- "His father's voice, still there in the strings"

Instead of "soul/resonated":
- "Nick's throat hurt after 'Dream On'"
- "Jackie's fingers cramped on the high notes"
- "Patty closed her eyes and sang it straight"

---

### 1.2.3. SENSORY SPECIFICITY OVER ABSTRACTION

❌ **Abstract:**
> "The garage was filled with memories and the essence of his father's presence."

✅ **Sensory/Specific:**
> "The garage still smelled like WD-40 and sawdust. Dan's jacket hung on the nail by the workbench, red plaid, left sleeve torn at the elbow."

**The Five Senses:**
- **Smell**: Fryer oil, sawdust, cigarettes, car exhaust, cafeteria pizza, damp November air
- **Sound**: Not "beautiful music" but "the G-string was flat, needed tuning"
- **Touch**: Calluses (specific location: first finger, left hand), cold metal, rough concrete
- **Sight**: Not "beautiful" but specific: "blue Chevy with rust on the quarter panel"
- **Taste**: Cheap beer, cafeteria meatloaf, burnt coffee, grape soda

---

## 1.3. SENTENCE RHYTHM: THE LEHANE PATTERN

**Pattern: Short. Medium with character. Accessible but loaded.**

✅ **Example:**
> "Nick tuned the guitar. The G-string was flat—always was, ever since his father dropped it moving the lawn mower. He left it flat on purpose now."

**Breakdown:**
1. **Short declarative** (4 words): Establishes action
2. **Medium with detail** (11 words): Adds character/specificity  
3. **Longer reveal** (9 words): Emotional weight

**Practice this pattern:**
- Opening: Action (short)
- Middle: Detail (medium)
- Close: Meaning (accessible but weighted)

## 1.4. VOICE TARGETS BY CHARACTER/POV

### 1.4.1. NICK (3rd Person Close, Post-Dan's Death)

**Voice Qualities:**
- Grief manifest as silence, not explanation
- Aerosmith as religion (specific songs, not "the music")
- Working-class pride shown through objects, not stated
- Shorter sentences (he's withdrawn)

✅ **Example:**
> "The garage was cold. November cold, the kind that made your fingers stiff before you got through the first chord. Nick didn't turn on the space heater. His father used to. That was enough reason not to."

**Avoid:** 
- Over-explaining his grief
- Making him too articulate about his feelings
- "He missed his father terribly" (tell)
- Show through: what he won't touch, what he leaves unchanged, what he does exactly how Dan did it

---

### 1.4.2. JACKIE (3rd Person Close, Gilded Cage)

**Voice Qualities:**
- Classical training creates precision in his observations
- Pressure shown through physical symptoms (perfectionism as body sensation)
- Queen as aspiration (what he wishes he could be)
- Longer sentences (he overthinks)

✅ **Example:**
> "Jackie hit the high note in 'Bohemian Rhapsody'—the one that always made his throat hurt, the one his voice teacher said he was pushing too hard for, the one Freddie Mercury made sound effortless on the record. His throat hurt. That meant he'd done it right, or wrong. He could never tell the difference."

**Avoid:**
- Making him too self-aware of his privilege
- Explaining the gilded cage (show it)
- "He felt trapped by his father's expectations" (tell)
- Show through: what he practices vs. what he wants to play, physical space descriptions (formal dining room vs. Nick's garage)

---

### 1.4.3. MAXANNE (1st Person, 2009, Radio Broadcast)

**Voice Qualities:**
- Bourbon and regret (not maudlin, matter-of-fact)
- Radio rhythm (shorter, punchier than literary prose)
- 59 years old = no bullshit, direct (Maxanne is 29 in 1979, 59 in 2009)
- Angry/defiant about "celebration of closure"
- Carrying 30 years of guilt (shown through what she notices, not confession)

✅ **Example:**
> "Four hours left. That's what I've got. Four hours before this frequency becomes a Christian radio station—because apparently Jesus rocks harder than Aerosmith ever did. I'm 59 years old, sitting here with a bourbon that costs more than most car payments, and I drew the short straw. Nobody wanted the midnight shift. Nobody wanted to be here when the light goes dark."

**Avoid:**
- Over-explaining her guilt (show through what she confesses)
- Making her too poetic (she's a DJ, not a poet)
- Sentimentality (she's angry, not sad)
- "I felt terrible about what I'd done" (tell)
- Show through: what she remembers, what she kept (the tape), what she plays LOUD (defiance)

---

### 1.4.4. GRACE (3rd Person Close, When She Appears)

**Voice Qualities:**
- Former wealth = knows what was lost
- Exhaustion shown physically, not emotionally explained
- Dignity without self-pity
- Friendly's uniform as character

✅ **Example:**
> "Grace came home at ten-thirty, still wearing her Friendly's uniform. The ketchup stain by the pocket hadn't budged—she'd scrubbed it, gave up. Her feet hurt. They always did, but she only really noticed once she sat down."

**Avoid:**
- Explaining her sacrifice
- "She worked so hard for her son" (tell)
- Making her a saint (she's tired, sometimes short-tempered)
- Show through: what's in the fridge, what bills are on the table, how she sits down (weight off feet)

---

### 1.4.5. DR. MARSH (3rd Person Close, When He Appears)

**Voice Qualities:**
- Social climber shown through what he notices (status symbols)
- Regret about his own music buried deep
- Formal in public, different in private
- BU professor = educated but emotionally limited

✅ **Example:**
> "Dr. Marsh noticed the Johnson boy's guitar. An old Gibson, probably from the sixties. He'd owned one like it once, before graduate school. Sold it to pay for his dissertation typing. He'd told himself he was being practical."

**Avoid:**
- Making him a villain (he's trying to protect Jackie)
- Over-explaining his regret
- "He regretted giving up music" (tell)
- Show through: what he pauses on, what he won't say, how he touches Jackie's instruments (briefly, then pulls back)

## 1.5. SPECIFIC PROSE RULES

### 1.5.1. MUSIC DESCRIPTIONS

❌ **Purple:**
> "The music soared through the air like liquid gold, each note a perfect jewel of sound that pierced the heart with its raw beauty."

✅ **Grounded:**
> "The G-string buzzed on the third fret. Nick played the riff anyway—Aerosmith never sounded perfect on the record either. That was the point."

**Rules for Music:**
- Specific song titles, not "the music"
- Technical details (flat string, buzzing fret, wrong tempo)
- Physical sensation (throat hurts, fingers cramp, ears ring)
- Listener reaction (Grace stopped washing dishes, Jackie's father looked up from paper)
- Never: "soared," "liquid," "transcendent," "ethereal"

---

### 1.5.2. THE GARAGE

**The garage is Dan's cathedral. Treat it with PHYSICAL specificity, not spiritual metaphor.**

✅ **What to include:**
- Specific tools (wrench, vise grip, socket set)
- Smells (WD-40, sawdust, motor oil, old cigarettes)
- Sounds (door needs oil, concrete floor echoes, tools rattle at 82 Hz)
- Temperature (cold in winter, hot in summer, Dan's jacket still hanging)
- Dan's handwriting (on labels, on the workbench calendar)

✅ **The "Tools Vibrating" Concept (Not Purple):**

❌ **Purple:**
> "The tools sang in harmony with his soul, resonating on the same spiritual frequency."

✅ **Grounded:**
> "The wrench rattled against the vise grip when Nick hit E minor. Same thing happened when his father played that chord. 82 Hz, Dan used to say. The frequency of grief."

**Rule:** Physical resonance = real phenomenon. Tools actually vibrate at certain frequencies. Use scientific specificity (82 Hz), not spiritual abstraction.

---

### 1.5.3. BELT BUCKLES

**The belt buckles are VISUAL TOTEMS. They must stay.**

✅ **First Appearance (Age 12, Middle School):**
> "Jackie's belt buckle caught the cafeteria light—gold script, 'QUEEN' in that fancy lettering from the album covers. Nick noticed it the same moment Jackie spotted his: 'AEROSMITH' in chrome Gothic, the kind Steven Tyler wore on stage."

✅ **Later (Age 17, High School):**
> "Nick still wore the Aerosmith buckle. Chrome was dulled now, scratched from where it caught on his father's workbench. He'd thought about getting a new one. Decided not to."

**Rules:**
- Describe them specifically first time, briefly after
- Show their condition (dulled, scratched, polished)
- They're totems: Jackie keeps his perfect, Nick's gets worn
- At 17, they're no longer playful—they're battle flags

---

### 1.5.4. HOP BROOK & SUDBURY GEOGRAPHY

**Class shown through PHYSICAL GEOGRAPHY, not explanation.**

✅ **Northeast (Nick's World):**
> "The Johnson house sat three blocks from the Sudbury dump. On hot days in summer, you could smell it. Dan used to joke they got a discount on the mortgage for the view. He wasn't joking."

✅ **Southwest (Jackie's World):**
> "The Marsh house had a name—'Whitmore Estate'—even though the Marshes had only lived there four years. The previous owners put a bronze plaque by the mailbox. Dr. Marsh left it up."

✅ **Southeast (Patty's World):**
> "Patty lived near Cavicchio. Some days she smelled like pig. She stopped apologizing for it freshman year."

✅ **Northwest (Lincoln Sudbury Regional High School):**
> "Lincoln Sudbury sat out past the old farms, brick rectangle on a bare field. Bus lot out front, windows stuck shut half the year. Someone had carved 'Stink Suds' into the sidewalk by the gym. On JV game nights, the whole place smelled like popcorn and sweat." ((this needs some work because I wouldn't describe it like this, but I like having LS as the 4th Quadrant representation))


**Rules:**
- Smell, sound, sight (sensory)
- What you can afford vs. what you can't
- Hop Brook as PHYSICAL barrier (you cross it, or you don't)
- The Dump as class marker (proximity = poverty)

---

### 1.5.5. CAFETERIA & HIGH SCHOOL

**The cafeteria is the ARENA. Four cliques, visible through WHERE people sit and WHAT they wear/eat.**

✅ **Rampers (College-Bound, Elevated Platform):**
> "The Rampers sat on the platform—actual elevated seating, like the school architect thought they deserved a better view. Jackie sat there now. SAT prep books on the table, cafeteria pizza left half-eaten."

✅ **Rats (Glass Corridor, Alternative Kids):**
> "The Rats claimed the glass corridor. Smoking section was outside, but they'd smoke inside if they thought they'd get away with it. Band t-shirts (not Aerosmith or Queen—Clash, Ramones, shit you had to find at Newbury Comics). They had the best music taste and knew it."

✅ **Regs (Middle Tables, Majority):**
> "The Regs filled the middle tables. Regular kids, regular clothes, regular lunch. The ones who'd graduate and work at the hardware store or join the force or maybe go to state school. Nick used to sit here, before Dan died."

✅ **Rollers (Near Auto Shop Entrance, Car Culture):**
> "The Rollers sat near the auto shop entrance, close enough to smell motor oil. Dan had been a Roller in his day. Some of them still asked Nick about the Chevy his father was working on. He hadn't touched it."

**Rules:**
- Show cliques through LOCATION and OBJECTS
- Not "the popular kids"—specific groups with specific values
- Boundaries are visible (you don't cross tables casually)
- Food matters (who brings lunch vs. buys it, what they eat)

## 1.6. DIALOGUE RULES

### 1.6.1. AUTHENTIC TEENAGE VOICE (1978-79)

**Teenagers trying to sound adult = awkward, trying too hard, sometimes getting it wrong.**

✅ **Nick (Working-Class, Post-Grief):**
> "Whatever."  
> "I'm fine."  
> "Yeah."  
> (When pushed): "Look, I don't want to talk about it, okay?"

**Rules:**
- Shorter when guarded
- Longer when angry (then he spills)
- Boston accent markers (not every word, just occasional: "Yeah" not "Yes")
- Aerosmith lyrics when he can't find his own words

---

✅ **Jackie (Trained to Be Articulate, But Still 17):**
> "I appreciate your concern, but I've got it under control."  
> (When stressed): "I mean—I just—it's not that simple."  
> (When he drops the mask): "I don't know what I'm doing. I'm just... I'm just trying not to disappoint everyone."

**Rules:**
- Starts formal, breaks down under pressure
- Uses bigger vocabulary (training) but not pretentious
- Queen lyrics when he's trying to be theatrical
- "I mean" and "just" when he's uncertain

---

✅ **Patty (Southeast Sudbury, Independent):**
> "You guys are idiots."  
> "I'm not picking sides."  
> "Music isn't supposed to be a war."

**Rules:**
- Direct, no bullshit
- Not eloquent, but clear
- Practical ("That's not going to work")
- Fleetwood Mac references (Stevie Nicks as her model)

---

### 1.6.2. ADULT DIALOGUE

✅ **Grace (Exhausted, Direct):**
> "Nick, honey. It's after midnight."  
> "I pulled a double. There's meatloaf in the fridge."  
> "Your father would've liked that. Both of you winning."

**Rules:**
- Short sentences (she's tired)
- Practical details first
- Emotion shown through pauses, not words
- Boston accent when stressed ("It's wicked cold out")

---

✅ **Dr. Marsh (Formal, Controlled):**
> "Jackie, we need to discuss your preparation for the Juilliard audition."  
> "I want what's best for you."  
> (When his guard drops): "I played guitar once. Did you know that?"

**Rules:**
- Formal until he's not
- "We need to discuss" not "we should talk"
- Longer sentences (educated, careful)
- Can't quite say what he means (emotional limitation)

---

✅ **Maxanne (2009, No Filter):**
> "Four hours left."  
> "I'm fifty-nine years old, and I drew the short straw."  
> "Let me tell you about the band that never was."

**Rules:**
- Radio rhythm (shorter, conversational)
- Curse words when appropriate (she's angry)
- Direct, no euphemisms
- Self-aware about her guilt but not maudlin

## 1.7. MAXANNE'S 2009 CHAPTERS: SPECIAL RULES

**Structure:**
- Opens with TIME REMAINING ("Four hours left" / "Two hours" / "One hour")
- Radio broadcast rhythm (shorter paragraphs, conversational)
- Bourbon mentioned (physical detail, not metaphor)
- Studio B details (ON AIR light, headphones, bourbon glass)
- Transitions to 1970s story smoothly

✅ **Opening Pattern:**
> "[TIME]. That's what I've got left.  
> 
> [Physical detail about studio/herself].  
> 
> [Direct address to listener].  
> 
> [Transition to 1970s story]."

✅ **Example:**
> "Two hours left.  
> 
> The bourbon's half-gone. I should probably slow down, but I'm fifty-nine and this station's dying at midnight, so fuck it.  
> 
> I told you about how Nick and Jackie met. Now let me tell you how they ended.  
> 
> Spring 1979. Two months before the Battle of the Bands."

**Rules:**
- Keep it TIGHT (these chapters are shorter)
- No over-explaining the parallel (readers will get it)
- Anger/defiance, not sadness
- Ends with strong transition to next 1970s chapter

## 1.8. WHAT TO AVOID: THE BANNED LIST

### 1.8.1. BANNED WORDS & PHRASES

**Music:**
- ❌ "Soared" / "Liquid" / "Transcendent" / "Ethereal" / "Divine"
- ❌ "Electricity crackled" / "Energy pulsed" / "Frequency hummed"
- ❌ "His soul" / "Their spirits" / "Musical alchemy"

**Emotions:**
- ❌ "He felt..." (tell) → Show through action/sensation
- ❌ "She realized..." (tell) → Show through what she does next
- ❌ "They understood..." (tell) → Show through dialogue/action

**Class:**
- ❌ "Working-class dignity" / "Blue-collar pride"
- ❌ "Economic inequality" / "Class struggle"
- ❌ Any academic/sociological language

**General:**
- ❌ "Calloused hands" (unless you describe WHICH callus, WHERE, WHY)
- ❌ "Played until his hands bled" (teenage hyperbole, not reality)
- ❌ "The weight of..." (abstract weight)
- ❌ "Journey" / "Path" / "Destiny" (too abstract)

## 1.9. LEHANE SENTENCE EXAMPLES TO STUDY

**From Mystic River:**
> "The gun was heavy. He put it down."

**Analysis:** Two short sentences. Physical detail (heavy). Action. No explanation. We understand everything from what's NOT said.

---

**From Mystic River:**
> "Jimmy's father had been a thief. Not a good one."

**Analysis:** Statement. Immediate qualification. Humor through understatement. Shows character through what they DID, not who they WERE.

---

**From Gone Baby Gone:**
> "The neighborhood smelled like low tide and fried food and rage that had nowhere to go."

**Analysis:** Sensory (smell). Specific (low tide, fried food). Then emotional truth, but GROUNDED in the physical world (rage that had nowhere to go = specific to trapped neighborhoods).

## 1.10. PRACTICAL REWRITING PROCESS

### 1.10.1. STEP 1: Read the Draft 1 chapter

### 1.10.2. STEP 2: Identify the beats that MUST be preserved
- What events happen?
- What character moments matter?
- What dialogue is essential?

### 1.10.3. STEP 3: Rewrite with these questions
1. **Can I show this physically instead of explaining it?**
   - Not "he was sad" → "His father's jacket still hung on the nail"
2. **Is this sentence too long/abstract?**
   - Break into shorter. Add physical detail.
3. **Am I using banned words?**
   - Ctrl+F for: soared, electricity, frequency, soul, spirit, transcendent
4. **Does this sound like Lehane?**
   - Read it aloud. If it sounds "poetic," cut it.
5. **What does this smell/sound/feel like?**
   - Add sensory detail.

### 1.10.4. STEP 4: Read the rewritten chapter aloud
- Does it sound natural?
- Can you hear the voice?
- Is it too slow (cut) or too fast (add beats)?

## 1.12. SUCCESS CRITERIA FOR EACH REWRITTEN CHAPTER

✅ **Purple prose eliminated:** Zero banned words/phrases  
✅ **Lehane voice:** Short sentences, physical details, restrained  
✅ **Class shown:** Through objects, smells, spaces (not explained)  
✅ **Story preserved:** All key beats intact  
✅ **Voice appropriate:** POV-specific (Maxanne radio rhythm, Nick withdrawn, Jackie overthinking)  
✅ **Sensory:** At least 3 senses engaged per scene  
✅ **Reads aloud well:** Natural, not "written"  
✅ **Belt buckles:** Described specifically when they appear  
✅ **Music:** Specific songs, technical details, physical sensations  

---

## 1.13. FINAL REMINDERS

1. **Shorter sentences are an advantage** - naturally direct voice, common words, clear communication
2. **Your limited vocabulary is an advantage** - no temptation toward purple prose
3. **Hyperfocus is an advantage** - when structure is clear, you write FAST
4. **Dennis Lehane is your north star** - when in doubt, ask "Would Lehane write this?"
5. **The story is already there** - you're not rewriting story, you're rewriting PROSE
6. **Trust the blueprint** - we know where we're going
7. **Short sentences. Physical details. Truth over poetry.**
