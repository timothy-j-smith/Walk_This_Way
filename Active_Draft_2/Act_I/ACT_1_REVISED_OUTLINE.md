## Architecture

Act I establishes the world before the fall. Three timelines interwoven:

1. **1974-1975:** Nick and Jackie meet (ages 12-13), become friends, discover music together
2. **1975-1977:** Friendship deepens, first cracks appear, Dan dies
3. **2009:** Maxanne opens her final broadcast, introduces the story, establishes guilt

The Maxanne chapters (Prologue, Chapter 9) are first-person radio broadcast. All other chapters are third-person close, rotating POV between Nick and Jackie (and later Patty, Grace, Dr. Marsh).

### CHAPTER 1: "Belt Buckles and Disco Sucks"

- **POV:** 3rd person close, Nick and Jackie
- **Setting:** Ephraim Curtis Middle School cafeteria, September 1974 (ages 12)
- **Word Target:** ~4,000-5,000 words
- **Beats:**
  1. Cafeteria. Disco blasting. Cardboard pizza. Warm milk cartons.
  2. Jackie alone at corner table. New kid from Syracuse. Queen belt buckle catches fluorescent light.
  3. Nick enters. Aerosmith buckle catches sun, blinds Jackie.
  4. The approach. "Queen? Seriously?" "What's wrong with Queen?"
  5. The debate. Queen vs. Aerosmith. Shared hatred of disco.
  6. "Can we agree that disco sucks?" "Oh my God, finally someone who gets it!"
  7. The friendship begins. They sit together. First alliance.
- **Key Sensory Details:** Cafeteria smell (cardboard pizza, warm milk, floor wax). Disco bass through cheap speakers. Belt buckle reflections. Fluorescent light hum.
- **Style Notes:** Lehane short sentences. Show class through objects (Jackie's clothes picked by mother, Nick's clothes bought on sale). No narrator commentary on what it "means."

### CHAPTER 2: "The Garage"

- **POV:** 3rd person close, Nick and Jackie
- **Setting:** Johnson family garage, Dutton Road, Winter 1974-75 (ages 12-13)
- **Word Target:** ~4,000-5,000 words
- **Beats:**
  1. Jackie visits Nick's house for the first time. Crosses Hop Brook.
  2. The Johnson house described through Jackie's eyes. The garage.
  3. Dan's tools on the pegboard. WD-40 and sawdust smell.
  4. Nick plays "Sweet Emotion" on the pawnshop guitar. Jackie plays "Bohemian Rhapsody" melody.
  5. Tools vibrate at 82 Hz when Nick hits E minor. The wrench against the vise grip.
  6. They discover their voices blend. Something clicks.
  7. "We should start a band." "What would we call it?"
  8. Dan appears in doorway. Watches. Doesn't interrupt.
- **Key Sensory Details:** Cold garage concrete. WD-40. Sawdust. Tool rattle at specific frequencies. Guitar string buzz on third fret. Dan's calloused hands (specific: right thumb, cracked from cold).
- **Style Notes:** The garage described PHYSICALLY, not spiritually. Tools are tools. The vibrating is real physics (82 Hz = low E). Dan shown through what he does, not what he thinks.

### CHAPTER 3: "Camp Winnipesaukee"

- **POV:** 3rd person close, Nick and Jackie (with Patty POV at chapter end)
- **Setting:** Camp Winnipesaukee, New Hampshire, Summer 1975 (ages 13)
- **Word Target:** ~5,000-6,000 words
- **Beats:**
  1. Bus ride. Diesel and anticipation. Guitars in laps. Leaving Sudbury behind.
  2. Camp arrival. Pine cabins. Lake. Mr. Freedman, the music director.
  3. Nick and Jackie perform at camp talent show. First time outside Sudbury.
  4. The audience response. Something real happens on that stage.
  5. Patty Lally watches from the audience. Southeast Sudbury. Pig farm family. She sees it.
  6. After the show, by the lake. Patty approaches.
  7. "You guys are amazing. But the world we're going back to isn't set up for this."
  8. "What do you mean?" "You'll see. September always kills August."
  9. Last night. Promises. Addresses exchanged. The drive home in separate cars.
- **Key Sensory Details:** Pine sap. Lake water. Campfire smoke. Diesel exhaust from the bus. Stage lights (hot, too bright). Night air on the lake (cold, smells like woodsmoke and wet pine).
- **Style Notes:** Camp is a neutral zone -- geography and class dissolve here. Patty introduced through what she does, not biography. Her warning is earned through what she's observed, not prophetic wisdom. The chapter should feel like the last perfect moment before everything starts to change.

## Voice Reminders (from Style Guide)

- Dennis Lehane is the north star. Short sentences. Physical details. Truth over poetry.
- Show class through objects, smells, spaces. Never explain it.
- Music described through technical details and physical sensation. Never "soared" or "transcendent."
- Belt buckles described specifically. Condition shows passage of time.
- Hop Brook as physical barrier. You cross it, or you don't.
- Teenagers trying to sound adult = awkward, not eloquent.
