The Junction: South Sudbury station was located "at the junction of the Massachusetts Central Railroad...and the Framingham and Lowell Railroad, slightly north of Boston Post Road (U.S. Route 20) in South Sudbury." There was a "diamond crossing" where the two lines intersected.
Timeline: The north-south line (F&L) opened in 1871, and the east-west line (Massachusetts Central) opened in 1881, so the crossing was established in 1881.
Purposes:
The north-south F&L was "the first American railway designed to connect industries on a north-south line, running from Lowell to Framingham, then to New Bedford on the Massachusetts south coast"
The east-west Massachusetts Central "aimed to do the same thing from Northampton in western Massachusetts to Boston in the east. Along the way it could load products from significant industrial towns like Ware, Fitchburg, and Ayer; plus milk and agricultural products from dozens of small towns on the route."

The Railroad Junction at South Sudbury
North-South Line (1871): The Framingham and Lowell Railroad (F&L) opened between its namesake cities on October 1, 1871 - this was your north-south line. It was "the first American railway designed to connect industries on a north-south line, running from Lowell to Framingham, then to New Bedford on the Massachusetts south coast."
East-West Line (1881): The Massachusetts Central Railroad (MC) opened from Boston to Hudson, Massachusetts in October 1881 - this was your east-west line. The Massachusetts Central Railroad "aimed to do the same thing from Northampton in western Massachusetts to Boston in the east. Along the way it could load products from significant industrial towns like Ware, Fitchburg, and Ayer; plus milk and agricultural products from dozens of small towns on the route."
The Exact Location
South Sudbury station was located "at the junction of the Massachusetts Central Railroad...and the Framingham and Lowell Railroad, slightly north of Boston Post Road (U.S. Route 20) in South Sudbury." There was a "diamond crossing" where the two lines intersected. There's even an "aerial view of trains at Union Avenue" showing trains at this junction.
The Railroad Strategy
The railroads "sliced straight through Sudbury's flattest land" because "the north-south line labeled '(Old) Colony Railroad' drove as straight as it could through the town...It wasn't going to waste time or money meandering around natural features if it didn't absolutely have to." This explains why they cut right through what is now the Cavicchio property area.
The Modern Legacy
Both railroad lines are now being converted to rail trails - the Mass Central Rail Trail (east-west) and the Bruce Freeman Rail Trail (north-south). In 2022, a buried transmission line project between Sudbury and Hudson began construction under the former Massachusetts Central Railroad ROW, which subsidized the cost of building the Mass Central Rail Trail—Wayside, expected to complete in 2025.
So the old railroad paths you see running through the Cavicchio Greenhouses property (including the former dump site) are the remnants of what was once South Sudbury's major railroad junction - a critical transportation hub that connected Boston to western Massachusetts (east-west) and Lowell to the south coast (north-south). The junction operated from 1881 until passenger service ended in 1971, making it a nearly 90-year transportation landmark right in that area!
