## Key Discoveries Made During This Session

1. The Neighborhoods (Upton, MA) won the first WBCN Rock 'n' Roll Rumble in 1979 — Timothy did not know this when writing Walk This Way
2. Mike Gordon (Phish) grew up in Sudbury, attended Lincoln-Sudbury Regional High School
3. Maxanne Sartori left WBCN in 1977 (before the Rumble), went to A&R at Elektra Records
4. WBCN's actual final song was "Shine On You Crazy Diamond" by Pink Floyd
5. Bruce Springsteen's first radio interview was with Maxanne at WBCN in 1973
6. John Lennon's warning: "God help Bruce Springsteen when they decide he's no longer God"
7. The novel's personal stakes: Nick is based on a friend who passed, Jackie on a friend who is housebound with depression
8. An early reader called the novel "a love letter disguised as a novel"

## Key Quotes and Insights from the Research Session

### On the Novel's Purpose

"You aren't just writing a book; you are building a monument for Nick and a bridge for Jackie."

"Your reader was right: this is a love letter disguised as a novel."

"The novel works because it is a love letter disguised as a post-mortem. You are asking a profound question: Does authenticity matter if it can't pay the rent or please your father?"

### On the Frequency Metaphor

"Harmony suggests a finished, proper product — something Jackie was trained for. Frequency is raw physics. It is the vibration in Nick's garage that punched you in the chest."

"A harmony is resolved at the end of a song, but a frequency can linger as background radiation."

### On the Characters

"While Nick is the Grit and Jackie is the Gift, Patty is the Grace."

"Patty is the only person in the world who knows what they sounded like before Nick's father died and before Jackie was groomed for Juilliard. She is the witness."

### On the Ending

"The tragic ending is the right move. Not every rock story ends with a stadium; the ones that end with a join-up at a radio station and then a fade to black are the ones that actually stay with us."

"You are essentially doing for your friends what the Ramones did for the kids at The Rat — you are taking their resigned reality and turning it into something loud, raw, and undeniable."

### On the Neighborhoods Parallel

"Your fictional premise mirrors the reality of the Boston music scene — and The Neighborhoods in particular — so closely that linking your story to their history could give your novel an incredible layer of authenticity."

"The Neighborhoods won the Rumble but still failed to make it nationally. Your characters win the prize but still fail to escape their destinies. This is the authentic Boston rock story."

### On Resignation

"In 1970s Boston, the class divide was a physical wall. The working-class kid often felt resigned to a life of manual labor or local obscurity, while the wealthy kid was resigned to a pre-planned professional vision. Your novel explores the brief moment where music allowed them to refuse that resignation."
