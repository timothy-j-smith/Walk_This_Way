## Timothy's Act III directives (verbatim in spirit)

1. **No feel-good ending. No sappy. No cliche.** The novel — and Nick, Jackie, and Maxanne — deserve better. Earn every beat with specific physical truth. If a sentence could appear in a USA TODAY puff piece or a YA teen-musician novel, cut it.
2. **Finish strong with Dickey / King / Lehane in the prose.** Dickey's body-in-the-woods attention; King's plain-spoken suburban physical dread; Lehane's Boston class weight and Catholic-guilt grammar. Walk This Way is a rock and roll novel. The rhythm must match — short sentences where the drum hits, long sentences where the solo runs.
3. **Dialogue must be real, honest, true.** Nick at 17 and 18 does not speak in paragraphs. He speaks in working-class New England fragments. Jackie at 17 and 18 speaks in precise clauses that crack under pressure. Maxanne in the booth at sixty-three speaks in broadcast rhythm, bourbon-cadenced, cigarette-paced. Nobody gives a speech. Every time a character is about to give a speech, pick the one true sentence inside it and let the rest go.
4. **The final song reversal (Ch 34) is the novel's climax.**
   - The corporate-approved final song is **"Shine On You Crazy Diamond" by Pink Floyd** (this is the real WBCN final song per `RESEARCH_INSIGHTS.md`; the prior draft's "Comfortably Numb" is wrong — fix it).
   - Maxanne is holding the LP (or the 12-inch single, or the cued cart) in her hand. She is talking about Pink Floyd on air — what "Shine On" means, why it's a perfect elegy, why corporate picked it, why she almost plays it. **Her commentary on Pink Floyd must come first.** She needs to honor the choice before she refuses it.
   - Then — at the very last second, not four hours early — she says "fuck it" (or whatever terse equivalent the voice earns) and plays Nick and Jackie's demo instead.
   - WBCN goes dark at midnight. The real station did close at midnight (per Timothy). The demo must finish and the signal die right on the stroke.
5. **This novel honors Nick and reaches Jackie.** Nick (the real one) passed from ALS. Jackie (the real one) suffers severe depression and is housebound. If a sentence does not honor Nick or reach Jackie, cut it.
6. **"Make it count."** — in the words of the WBCN station manager.

## Prose music (King / Dickey / Lehane)

- **Short sentences where the drum hits.** "Nick set the guitar down. He closed the lid. He did not latch it."
- **Long sentences where the solo runs.** A single sentence can unspool for a hundred words if it is carrying one unbroken physical image or one honest thought; it breaks only where the breath breaks.
- **Lehane Boston.** A specific neighborhood, a specific bar name, a specific Route. Nobody refers to "Boston" without a cross street or a T stop.
- **King suburban dread.** The refrigerator ticking. The single bulb. The thing you do not say at dinner. The teenager who thinks he is alone in the house.
- **Dickey body-as-knower.** When something real happens, it happens in the hands, the jaw, the stomach. Not in the head. Characters learn through their bodies.
