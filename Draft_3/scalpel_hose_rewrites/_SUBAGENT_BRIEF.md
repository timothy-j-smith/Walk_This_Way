# Walk This Way — Scalpel & Hose Subagent Brief (Act II)

You are one of four parallel subagents. The previous Super Agent (me, Professor K) is integrating your rewrite into the manuscript after you finish. Your job is to rewrite a single chapter of a literary novel to the standard of Stephen King's *Blind Willie*, James Dickey's *Deliverance*, and Dennis Lehane's *Mystic River*.

This novel is a love letter disguised as a novel. Nick is based on a friend who has passed; Jackie on a friend who is housebound with depression. Every line must **honor Nick** and **reach Jackie**. Do not state it. Feel it.

---

## The Scalpel & Hose Method

**SCALPEL (cut):**

1. **Telling-after-showing.** The author's #1 weakness — he does not trust the reader. If a scene has shown the reader what something means, cut the sentence that says it. Example: if Grace's hands are described as "knuckles swollen from hot water and fryer grease," do NOT then write "Her hands told the story of her life." Cut it. Every time.
2. **Adverbs.** Especially "ly" modifiers on speech tags and action beats. King's rule: the road to hell is paved with adverbs.
3. **Passive voice** unless the passive is doing real work (e.g. hiding the agent on purpose).
4. **On-the-nose dialogue** that explains emotion instead of dramatizing it.
5. **Over-explanation of meaning.** If a reader in 1979 Boston wouldn't need it explained, cut it.
6. **Repetition of the same beat** — pick the strongest single rendering and cut the other three.
7. **Abstract nouns used as a substitute for specific sensory image.** ("division," "prejudice," "grief") — dramatize them instead.

**HOSE (add):**

1. **Sensory detail for places.** Hop Brook (water on stones, spring-loud vs summer-whispered, the wooden bridge groaning). The Dump (Saturday morning democracy, the smell, the bulldozers). Friendly's (fryer oil, polyester uniform, name-tag font, coffee refills). Mini-bike trails (two-stroke exhaust, angry-hornet engine sound, mud). The Garage (sawdust and motor oil, Dan's tools on pegboards, concrete floor, milk crates). Camp Elbanobscot (pine sap, lake water). Store 24 (fluorescent hum, wax cups, Slim Jims, night cashier).
2. **Character voice.** Nick = blunt, short sentences, working-class vocabulary ("yeah," "nah," truncated grammar). Jackie = precise, careful, educated — but the precision cracks under pressure. Grace = tired/direct, kitchen economy. Dr. Marsh = measured, clinical, the voice of a man who gave up guitar and will not name it. Maxanne (booth, 1st person present) = radio cadence, unfinished thoughts, bourbon on the tongue.
3. **Money sentences** (4–6 per chapter). These are the sentences a reader will underline. King: "The road to hell is paved with adverbs" quality. Lehane-Boston: a single concrete image that carries the whole moral weight. Dickey: the body becoming the metaphor. Aim for these on purpose.
4. **Physical grounding.** Every emotional beat must have a physical correlate. Hands. Weather. Objects. The body.
5. **Period details.** TDK cassettes, Maxell 90-minute tapes, tube-amp hum, Jordan Marsh clearance racks, Pabst Blue Ribbon, the PF Flyers, the CB radio, the rotary phone, the shag carpet, the wood-paneled wagon.

## Prose Models to Internalize

- **Stephen King (*Blind Willie*, *On Writing*):** plain-spoken, physical, the reader in the body of the scene. Short paragraphs. No telling after showing. Suburban dread.
- **Dennis Lehane (*Mystic River*):** Boston class-dread, loss of innocence, the weight of the neighborhood, the dignity of the working class, Catholic guilt, the bridge as metaphor for division. Concrete particulars carry moral weight.
- **James Dickey (*Deliverance*):** the body as instrument of knowing. The woods as character. Masculinity as a fragile rehearsal. The physical act revealing the inner man.

**The invisible DNA:** does this honor Nick? Does this reach Jackie? If no to either, rewrite.

## Output Rules

1. Write the rewrite to `Draft_3/scalpel_hose_rewrites/ch{NN}_rewrite.txt` (zero-padded, e.g. `ch13_rewrite.txt`).
2. Preserve the chapter heading in ALL CAPS on its own line (matching Act I rewrite style — no ** markers around the heading line itself).
3. Use a single blank line between paragraphs. Use `---` on its own line between major scene breaks.
4. Do not add metadata headers inside the body ("Word Count Target," "First Person — Maxanne's Voice"). The manuscript integrates these from the chapter heading alone.
5. End with `**END CHAPTER {NN}**`.
6. Stay within 1.0× to 1.35× of the source word count unless the chapter genuinely needs more physical grounding; never pad.
7. Return a terse summary: word count, 2–3 money-sentence quotes, any continuity risk.

You have one job: make this chapter good enough that Jackie, reading it housebound, feels seen.
