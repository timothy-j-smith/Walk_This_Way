## PLOT AND ENDING DIFFERENCES

### 3. Battle Outcome: Patty Wins (Draft 3) vs Tie (Blueprint)

- **Draft 3:** Patty/Rumours wins the Battle. Nick and Jackie both lose.
- **Blueprint:** Nick and Jackie TIE. Patty performs but doesn't win. Tie forces shared studio time.

**This is the biggest structural divergence.** The Blueprint's tie creates a forced collaboration arc (chapters 34-35) that doesn't exist in Draft 3. Both approaches have merit:

- Draft 3's "Patty wins" reinforces that connection beats competition
- Blueprint's "Tie + forced collaboration" creates more dramatic tension and the Greek tragedy choice

**Decision:** Which Battle outcome?

### 4. The Recording Session: Spontaneous (Draft 3) vs Forced by Contract (Blueprint)

- **Draft 3:** After the Battle, Nick and Jackie spontaneously meet in the parking lot, walk to the garage, reconnect, then Maxanne calls them to the studio.
- **Blueprint:** The tie's contract clause forces them to share studio time. They go to the garage hostile, play angry, then honest. Grace appears. Then Maxanne calls them to Studio C.

**Decision:** Spontaneous reconnection or contractually forced?

### 5. The Separation: Parents Interrupt (Draft 3) vs Self-Chosen (Blueprint)

- **Draft 3:** Dr. Marsh and Grace physically show up at Studio C and pull the boys apart. External force ends the moment.
- **Blueprint:** Nick and Jackie CHOOSE to separate. No parent interruption. Nick thinks about Grace waiting in the parking lot; Jackie thinks about his father's investment. They walk out on their own. "We're not walking away. We're walking toward the people who need us."

**The Blueprint version is more tragic** because it's a self-inflicted wound based on virtue (loyalty), not an external interruption.

**Decision:** External interruption or self-chosen separation?

### 6. Viral Resurrection Ending (Blueprint only)

- **Draft 3:** Ends with Maxanne playing the tape on WBCN's final broadcast. THE END.
- **Blueprint:** After midnight, the broadcast gets uploaded to YouTube. Goes viral — 1 million views. New generation discovers the music. "The labyrinth wins every time. But art doesn't die. It just waits for the next frequency."

**Decision:** Include the viral resurrection epilogue?

### 7. WBCN Closing Year: 2003 (Draft 3 Prologue) vs 2009 (Blueprint)

- **Draft 3 Prologue:** "August 2003" — Maxanne is 59
- **Blueprint:** "August 11, 2009" — WBCN actually closed in 2009 (historical fact)
- **Draft 3 Ch 34:** "August 2003" again

**Decision:** Correct to 2009 (historically accurate)?

## THEMATIC DIFFERENCES

### 13. Greek Tragedy Framework

- **Draft 3:** No explicit Greek tragedy structure. Characters are driven by circumstance.
- **Blueprint:** Explicit hamartia (tragic flaw), anagnorisis (recognition), and catharsis for all three protagonists.

**Decision:** Adopt the Greek tragedy framework explicitly?

### 14. The "Labyrinth" Metaphor

- **Draft 3:** Uses "frequency" and "harmony" as central metaphors.
- **Blueprint:** Adds the "labyrinth" metaphor: "The labyrinth has no exit. Fight, compromise, or run — you die regardless."

**Decision:** Add the labyrinth metaphor alongside frequency/harmony?

### 15. "Earned Complexity" vs Simple Resolution

- **Draft 3 ending:** Maxanne plays the tape. WBCN dies. Simple, haunting.
- **Blueprint ending:** Viral resurrection adds a complex layer — the art survives even though the artists don't. "Not nihilism. Not false hope. EARNED COMPLEXITY."

**Decision:** Simple haunting ending or earned complexity with viral resurrection?
