# Craft Toolkit

**Universal principles + inventions from Senseless workshop work**

---

## Timothy's Inventions (Shelf 1 — Ours)

### Money Sentences

The rare line that makes a reader **stop**.

- Not every paragraph needs one
- Earned through specificity, not decoration
- Often mundane detail inside extraordinary context
- Example: "you should know that there is mold in your bagels this morning too" — inside a coma patient's desperate inner monologue

**Agent use:** Flag existing money sentences. Suggest where one is missing. Never force one per paragraph.

---

### Money Details

Specific, concrete images that carry more weight than their size suggests.

- "Walking banana"
- "Southwest corner"
- Kangaroo gloves feeling more natural than his own hands
- Jellylike feel of Jackie-O's eyeballs through closed eyelids
- Moldy bagels in the Toothpaste Factory

**Test:** Would a reader remember this detail tomorrow? If yes, protect it.

---

### Refrain Technique

Repeated phrase or question that **accumulates meaning** across the novel.

- Example: "What else did Sally say?"
- Each recurrence adds a layer the reader feels but cannot yet name
- Works with nursery rhymes, hums, Humpty Dumpty fragments
- Do not explain the refrain — let repetition do the work

---

### Three Writing Modes (Timothy's Strength Order)

| Mode | Strength | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Dialogue** | Strongest | Natural, specific, subtext-capable |
| **2. Stream of consciousness** | Most original | Toothpaste Factory = peak voice |
| **3. Narrator explanation** | Weakest — default trap | Exposition-as-shield; fix by converting to 1 or 2 |

When editing: **convert narrator blocks → dialogue or stream.** Do not polish exposition — replace it.

---

### Backstory Weaving vs. Dumping

**Dumping:** Character arrives with full biographical package in one block  
**Weaving:** Action first; biography in fragments across multiple scenes

**Example (Jackie-O):**

- Dump: Nickname history, bleached hair, Onassis resemblance, pearl necklace — all on first appearance
- Weave: Thomas calls her Jackie-O → she winces → party someone starts "You look just like—" → she cuts them off → old photo three chapters later

Same information. Ten times the impact.

---

### Revelation Parceling

Hold nuclear secrets until **maximum devastation**.

- Sally's rope, pregnancy, intentional act = Greek tragedy — do not detonate in exposition paragraph
- Plant through: flinches, avoidance, unconscious touch, strange reactions
- Reader assembles picture — hits harder because THEY figured it out
- BPD: show through behavior (mood shifts, reality distortion, Humpty Dumpty repetitions) — not clinical narrator diagnosis

---

### Stay in the Body

Emotion is physical. King principle, now universal.

- Fear tastes like pennies
- Attraction is warmth seeping through skin
- Memory is smell of fabric store, oak leaves, salt air
- Diagnosis tells; body shows
- "Thomas has vasculitis" = tell | Kangaroo gloves feel like his real hands = holy shit

---

### Blue Sky Mode

Rare 4th intensity. Hidden inside Channel/creative work.

- Throw caution out; high temperature
- Ketamine of writing — opens uncharted territory
- Senseless color sequences (RED, GREEN, BLUE, PURPLE...) are natural Blue Sky
- Not for daily use. Dangerous. Sometimes brilliant.

## Universal Principles (Shelf 1 — Seed Crystal)

From craft education work; no longer King-branded.

### Telepathy Engine

Writing = refined telepathy. Writer sends image; reader receives.

| Signal | Noise |
| --- | --- |
| Specific detail | Adverbs |
| Concrete imagery | Passive voice |
| Active verbs | Over-description |
| Earned emotion | Abstract philosophy |
| Ordinary detail in extraordinary moment | "Literary" pretension |

### Show Don't Tell

If you need to explain it, you didn't show it well enough. **Fix the showing, not the explaining.**

Delete sentences that EXPLAIN what character just DID or SAID.

### Character Drives Plot

Put characters in situations; let them work free. Never force characters to serve plot points.

### POV Rule

One POV per scene break. Thomas primary for Senseless. Sally, Jackie-O, Rashford get their own chapters/sections.

### Door Closed / Door Open

- First draft: for writer (door closed)
- Revision: for reader (door open)
- Fresh eyes between passes

### Revision Formula

2nd draft = 1st draft minus 10% (Scalpel baseline)

### Scalpel Targets

Adverbs, passive voice, overwriting, telling-after-showing, purple prose, darlings that don't serve

### Hose Targets

Sensory detail, character-specific fear made concrete, money sentences, ordinary detail that makes extraordinary believable

---

## The Writer's Toolbox Metaphor (Four Shelves)

1. **Foundation:** Vocabulary, grammar — first word that comes to mind if appropriate
2. **Style:** Active voice, kill adverbs, specific verbs, conversational prose
3. **Scene craft:** Situation-based storytelling, character through action, closed-door first draft
4. **Theme:** Bones holding the body — emerge, never force

---

## When Scene Is Weak — Diagnostic Checklist

1. Is this telling when it should show?
2. Is POV stable for this scene?
3. Is backstory dumped instead of woven?
4. Is a secret revealed too early?
5. Is emotion labeled instead of bodied?
6. Is narrator explaining what dialogue already carried?
7. Would dialogue or stream work better than explanation mode?

Fix in order. Show diff. One principle at a time when teaching.
