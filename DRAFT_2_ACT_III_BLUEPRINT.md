# DRAFT 2: ACT III BLUEPRINT

## ACT III: THE FALL (Chapters 26-34)

**Purpose**: The Battle of the Bands night arrives. Three bands perform, the town witnesses the collision, Patty wins by reminding everyone what music is for. Nick and Jackie briefly reunite for a recording session, proving the harmony still exists—then Dr. Marsh intervenes and the friendship dies for good. Thirty years later, Maxanne plays their demo tape on WBCN's final broadcast.

**Time Period**: May 15, 1979 (Battle night) / March 16, 1979 (Recording session) / 2009 (Maxanne's final broadcast)

**Arc**:
- The Battle: Green room tension, three performances, Patty's victory
- Brief redemption: Midnight garage session, WBCN recording, the demo tape
- Final separation: Dr. Marsh intervenes, Jackie to Juilliard, Nick to Army
- Epilogue: 2009, Maxanne plays the tape, WBCN goes dark, "The harmony still exists"

**Key Locations**: Lincoln-Sudbury auditorium (green room + stage), Nick's garage, WBCN Studio C

**POV**: Third person (green room focus)

### Chapter 27: Jackie's Performance (Crown Jewels)
**Timeline**: May 15, 1979, 8:20 PM  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Crown Jewels perform with technical perfection. Jackie plays "Killer Queen," "Somebody to Love" (letting polish slip to show real emotion), and "Bohemian Rhapsody" (roaring, unfiltered, the rock gospel spilling out). Dr. Marsh watches from front row. By final chord Jackie is spent, chest heaving. Crowd stands—some shouting, some open-mouthed. Dr. Marsh's hands unclench. Jackie doesn't smile. He's left everything on stage.

**Key Beats**:
- 8:20 PM sharp, Crown Jewels take stage
- Dr. Marsh front row: spine straight, tie perfect, jaw set
- Jackie with Martin D-28, Elizabeth on violin, Ace drums, Max bass
- "Killer Queen": crisp precision, sideways grin, swagger
- "Somebody to Love": halfway through, Jackie lets polish go, voice trembles
- Dr. Marsh's face flickers: confusion, maybe a memory
- "Bohemian Rhapsody": the one his father called "too gaudy, not serious"
- Jackie roars, bangs keys until pitch bleeds into emotion
- Operatic falsetto—he'd been holding his breath all year for this
- Elizabeth's violin ascending sharp and high, nearly stinging
- Final chord: Jackie spent, chest heaving, can't tell if it landed
- Someone stands clapping, wedding ring flashing—then everyone
- Dr. Marsh looks up, hands unclenched, realizing something unrepeatable
- Jackie doesn't smile: "Win or lose, he'd played the music he loved"
- Nick watches from wings, unreadable

### Chapter 28: Patty's Performance (Rumours)
**Timeline**: May 15, 1979, 8:40 PM  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Patty Lally takes stage barefoot with secondhand guitar. She plays "Dreams," "Landslide," "Silver Springs," "Go Your Own Way"—not for points or posture, but to remind everyone why music matters. No showmanship, just Patty bringing out wishes for things to be made right. By "Silver Springs" the revolution starts quietly: competition seems small, everyone part of something larger. Applause is tidal. Years later they'll argue who was best, but all agree Patty gave the truest gift—communion over contest.

**Key Beats**:
- 8:40 PM, Patty barefoot on dark riser, hair loose, honey in lights
- Secondhand guitar, smaller than others until she finds microphone
- No one expected revolution, just nice Fleetwood Mac covers
- "Dreams": softer and more defiant, reminded crowd freedom = letting others in
- "Landslide": nearly whispered, every kid facing change felt seen
- Delicate but sturdy with hope, played to mend rifts
- "Silver Springs": fierce and clear, "You'll never get away from the sound..."
- Revolution started here: competition seemed small, everyone invited into refrain
- "Go Your Own Way": rough, all feeling, cracked joy, honest
- Final chord: hush like awe, then tidal applause
- Chairs scraping, kids cheering, parents admitting they'd remember this forever
- Patty stunned: expected quiet ripple, met by rising sea
- "Raised music from contest to communion, rivalry into something hopeful"

### Chapter 30: The Announcement & Aftermath
**Timeline**: May 15, 1979, 9:30 PM  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Judges deliberate: Maxanne, Carter Alan, Bob Walker, Chuck Morrison can't score what just happened. "How do you give a number to something like that?" Chuck announces Rumours as winner. Crowd explodes—not just for the band, but for what they all felt. Social boundaries dissolve: Grace talks to Dr. Marsh, cousins from different quadrants connect, kids cross Hop Brook lines. Nick and Jackie look at each other: both lost, both won something more important. "She remembers."

**Key Beats**:
- Judges huddle: score sheets a mess, can't capture what happened

- Bob Walker: "How do I explain the whole town just got lit up?"
- Chuck Morrison: "We're not picking a winner. We're picking what Lincoln-Sudbury even is"
- Maxanne: "What if we just acknowledge what happened?"

### Chapter 31: The Recording Session (Part 1 - Garage Reunion)
**Timeline**: May 15, 1979, midnight  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Midnight parking lot: Nick on Stephen's truck, Jackie approaches from the Mercedes. "I think I've been doing this for the wrong reasons." They walk to Nick's garage together. Milk crates facing each other like when they were thirteen. They play tentatively—"Sweet Emotion" meets "Bohemian Rhapsody"—and it works. An hour, maybe two, rediscovering songs, melodies, the mashup from middle school. "That's what we could have been."

**Key Beats**:
- Parking lot empty except Grace's Pinto, Dr. Marsh's Mercedes
- Nick on truck hood, staring at stars, exhausted
- "Patty had won. Not him, not Jackie."
- Jackie approaches from darkness: "Hey"
- Four years of silence in one word
- "I think I've been doing this for the wrong reasons"
- "Me too. I forgot why I love music."
- Walk to garage together
- Same milk crates, same stained concrete, Dan's tools waiting
- Nick's Stratocaster, Jackie borrows Elizabeth's acoustic
- "Remember how we used to argue?"
- Start playing tentatively, then finding each other
- Hour or two, losing track of time
- "Dream On," "Somebody to Love," "Walk This Way," "Killer Queen"
- Middle school mashup that won talent show
- "That's what we could have been."

### Chapter 32: The Recording Session (Part 2 - WBCN Studio)
**Timeline**: May 16, 1979, 2:00 AM  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Maxanne calls at 2 AM: "Studio C. Right now. Record that mashup." Nick and Jackie drive to WBCN. Studio C feels like cathedral, walls covered with gold/platinum records. Engineer Mike sets up two mics facing each other. "One take. No overdubs." They record the Queen/Aerosmith mashup—emotionally perfect, authentically perfect. "This is what we could have been." Then Dr. Marsh and Grace arrive. The spell breaks. They walk out separate directions. Maxanne preserves the tape for 30 years.

**Key Beats**:
- 2 AM phone call from Maxanne: "I think it's time we made it official"
- "Studio C at WBCN. Right now. Proof that the resonance still exists."
- Nick and Jackie drive together
- Studio C: smaller than imagined, feels like cathedral
- Maxanne waiting: 73 years old, bourbon, eyes that remember everything
- Engineer Mike: 25, enthusiasm of working at dying station
- Two microphones positioned so they face each other
- "One take. No overdubs. Just you two, making music the way it was meant."
- "Some music can't survive being made professional. But tonight, we try."
- Red light on, they play the mashup
- Perfect: not technically, but emotionally, authentically
- "This is what we could have been"
- Listen to playback: hearing their voices blend
- Door opens: Dr. Marsh steps in like he'd been waiting
- "Jackie, it's time to go home"
- Grace appears: "Nick, honey, it's late"
- Spell broken: "Reality barged in—parents, tiredness, promises never meant to keep"
- Dr. Marsh's hand on Jackie's shoulder: claiming, not comforting
- "Different worlds"
- Walk out separate directions
- Maxanne alone with master tape: "We preserve it because someone needs to remember"
- Slips tape in purse, carries it 30 years

## ACT III CONCLUSION

By the end of Act III:
- **The Battle delivered**: Three performances, each authentic in its own way
- **Patty won by losing**: Reminded everyone music is communion, not competition
- **Brief redemption happened**: Midnight garage, 2 AM recording, the proof exists
- **America won**: Dr. Marsh's hand, Jackie to Juilliard, Nick to Army, friendship dead
- **30 years later**: Maxanne plays the tape before WBCN dies, preserves the memory
- **The resonance still exists**: Not in reality, but in memory, in the tape, in anyone who remembers to listen

**Final Message**: The labyrinth wins. Division always wins. But someone has to document what existed before it was destroyed. Someone has to say: "The harmony still exists" even when it doesn't anymore. Someone has to remember. That's all we have. That's everything.
