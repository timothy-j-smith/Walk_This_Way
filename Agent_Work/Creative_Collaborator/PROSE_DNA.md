## WHAT WORKS (Examples from Act I Edits)

### **Direct, Earned Emotion:**
✅ "Grace went back to work the next day. She had to."  
✅ "Nick's suit didn't fit."  
✅ "The garage smelled like sawdust."  
✅ "Dan came downstairs at six-fifteen, already dressed in his work clothes—heavy boots, jeans, and a flannel shirt that smelled like sawdust. He kissed Grace goodbye, told her he'd be home by six, and picked up his lunch the way he did every morning."

### **Class Divide Through Detail:**
✅ The Marshes' tailored funeral clothes vs. Nick's ill-fitting hand-me-down suit  
✅ "Dove Gray" walls vs. oil-stained concrete garage floor  
✅ Grace packing Dan's lunch in wax paper  
✅ "where you didn't have to go back to work the next day just to keep the lights on"

### **Emotional Weight in Simple Facts:**
✅ Dan was eating the tuna sandwich Grace made when he died  
✅ Nick stopped playing guitar because every note reminded him of Dan  
✅ Jackie and Nick make eye contact across cafeteria, then both look away

**Pattern:** Concrete detail carries emotion. Facts are devastating. No need to explain.

## WHAT DOESN'T WORK (What We Cut)

### **Purple Prose & Overwrought Metaphors:**
❌ "clutching guitars like talismans against social hierarchies"  
❌ "like magnets with opposite charges"  
❌ "sawdust and possibility"  
❌ "cathedral-like effect," "magical sound"  
❌ "the frequency, as Patty had called it"

### **Abstract Philosophy:**
❌ "emotional truth" (too vague)  
❌ "working-class authenticity enhanced Jackie's classical training"  
❌ "harmonic sophistication elevated Nick's raw emotion"  
❌ "with the kind of [abstract quality]" (overused construction)

### **Naming Emotions:**
❌ "Nick felt sad" → ✅ "Nick stared at his hands"  
❌ "Grace was devastated" → ✅ "Grace went back to work"  
❌ "Jackie was nervous" → ✅ "Jackie's fingers tight on guitar strap"

### **Trying Too Hard:**
❌ "The main hallway stretched like a spine through the school, with classrooms branching off like ribs"  
❌ "fluorescent lights cast everything in that pallor that makes even the most hopeful performances look like medical procedures"

**Pattern:** If it sounds "literary" or self-conscious, cut it. Direct is better.

---

## KEY CONSTRAINTS (What NOT to Do)

### **Purple Prose Assassin Rules:**
❌ No overwrought metaphors  
❌ Don't name emotions - show through action/detail  
❌ No pretentious vocabulary  
❌ Don't over-explain themes  
❌ Avoid "with the kind of" constructions  
❌ Cut repetitive phrases across chapters  
❌ No preaching about class/music/friendship

### **Repetition to Avoid:**
- "authenticity/authentic" (use: real, genuine, honest)
- "raw talent" + "technical skill" (use: instincts + precision)
- Mortgage references (vary the language)
- "with the kind of" (cut or rephrase)

### **Themes to NEVER Use:**
❌ **"Frequency" language** - User explicitly hated this concept  
❌ Any mystical/spiritual music connection language  
❌ Abstract "universal truths" about music

## THEMES TO PROTECT (Don't Over-Explain)

### **Core Themes (Show, Don't Preach):**
1. **Class divide is real** - shown through concrete details
2. **Friendship fractured by forces beyond control** - inevitable tragedy
3. **Music = temporary bridge** - but not magic solution
4. **The garage = neutral ground** - where class doesn't matter (for a while)
5. **Dan Johnson matters** - his death is devastating, not sentimental

### **How to Handle Themes:**
✅ **Show through detail:** Nick's ill-fitting suit at funeral  
❌ **Don't explain:** "This showed how class divided them"

✅ **Show through action:** Grace going back to work next day  
❌ **Don't explain:** "She had no choice in a working-class world"

**Rule:** If you're tempted to explain a theme, you're probably over-writing. Trust the reader.
