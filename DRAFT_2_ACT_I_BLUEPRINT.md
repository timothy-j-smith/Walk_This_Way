# DRAFT 2: ACT I BLUEPRINT

## NOVEL THEME

**Core Story**: Two talented teenage musicians from opposite sides of a class divide—best friends who create magic together—are torn apart by America's class machinery, parental expectations, and the death that changes everything. Their friendship becomes a rivalry, their music becomes a weapon, and what could have been harmony becomes division.

**Central Question**: Can music bridge the class divide, or does America's social machinery always win?

**Thematic Elements**:
- Class division in suburban America (Hop Brook as literal and metaphorical divide)
- Music as bridge vs. music as battlefield
- Loss of innocence (friendship → rivalry)
- Parental expectations crushing authentic dreams
- Grief and how it builds walls
- The cost of authenticity in a world that values conformity

---

## ACT I: THE RISE (Chapters 0-12)

**Purpose**: Establish the 2009 frame story (Maxanne's final broadcast) and show the complete arc of Nick and Jackie's friendship from its magical beginning through middle school triumph to high school fracture and Dan's death. By the end of Act I, we understand what was lost and why it matters.

**Time Period**: 2009 (frame) / 1974-1977 (flashback story)

**Arc**: 
- Innocence: Two 12-year-olds meet via belt buckles, united by hatred of disco and love of real rock
- Magic: Middle school friendship, garage sessions, summer camp with Patty, talent show victory
- Fracture: High school social forces pull them apart
- Shattering: Dan's death destroys Nick and any chance of the friendship surviving

**Key Locations**: WBCN Studio B (2009), Johnson garage (Dutton Road), Marsh house (good side of Hop Brook), Ephraim Curtis Middle School, Camp Winaukee, Lincoln-Sudbury High School

## ACT I CHAPTERS

### Chapter 0: Prologue - The Last Voice of Boston Rock
**Timeline**: October 2009 (WBCN's final broadcast night)  
**POV**: Maxanne Sartori (1st person)

**Summary**: Maxanne Sartori broadcasts WBCN's final show with 4 hours left before the station goes dark. She introduces the story of Nick Johnson and Jackie Marsh—two Sudbury kids who should have changed everything but got caught in America's class war. She holds the only demo tape of them playing together.

**Key Beats**:
- WBCN closing after 56 years
- Maxanne holding "Demo - Nick/Jackie - un-Authorized - March 1979" tape
- Setup: Two boys, same town, different worlds
- Hop Brook as dividing line
- Promise to tell the story before the station dies
- Transition to flashback: "But first, we need to talk about a garage on Dutton Road..."

---

### Chapter 1: Nick's World - The Garage
**Timeline**: March 1979 (but showing Nick at 17, established patterns)  
**POV**: Third person (through Maxanne's visit)

**Summary**: Maxanne drives to the working-class side of Sudbury to check out rumors about a kid who plays guitar like he's trying to resurrect the dead. She discovers Nick Johnson in his late father's garage, playing Aerosmith with raw authenticity, surrounded by Dan's unused tools and the weight of being man of the house at 17.

**Key Beats**:
- Grace Johnson at the door, Friendly's uniform, protective
- Garage as sanctuary and shrine to Dan
- Nick playing "Dream On" with soul that can't be taught
- Working-class authenticity vs. technical precision
- Maxanne gives Nick her card: "Come by the station"
- Tools on pegboard = Dan's ghost watching
- Transition: "Less than a mile away... a different kind of cage"

---

### Chapter 2: Jackie's World - The Gilded Cage
**Timeline**: Same week as Chapter 1 (March 1979)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Jackie Marsh wakes in his perfect bedroom, prepares for a piano recital orchestrated by Dr. Marsh. At the recital, after performing classical pieces flawlessly but emptily, Jackie rebels by playing "Bohemian Rhapsody"—stunning the room and revealing his true self. Maxanne witnesses it and gives Jackie her card.

**Key Beats**:
- Jackie's perfect, controlled morning routine
- Recital audience: prep school parents, university faculty
- Classical pieces (Chopin, Bach, Debussy, Rachmaninoff) technically perfect but soulless
- Rebellion moment: "Bohemian Rhapsody" on the Steinway
- Dr. Marsh's controlled anger, guest shock
- Maxanne recognizes trapped talent, gives him her card
- "You play what matters. Sometimes it comes from kids in garages. Sometimes from piano prodigies."
- Maxanne realizes she's setting them on collision course

---

### Chapter 3: The Geography of Sudbury
**Timeline**: Context/background (1974-1979)  
**POV**: Third person omniscient

**Summary**: Explanation of Sudbury's four-quarter division created by Hop Brook (north-south) and The Dump (east-west). Each quadrant represents different America: northeast (working-class), southeast (Cavicchio pig farm), southwest (protected estates), northwest (Revolutionary War wealth). The geographic lines that will determine Nick and Jackie's fate.

**Key Beats**:
- Hop Brook and The Dump as dividing lines
- Four distinct Sudburys with different relationships to American dream
- Northeast: working-class families, single homes, fathers in construction
- Southwest: Colonial homes with names, professors, assumed excellence
- Conservation land and mini-bike trails as contested territory
- The Dump as democratic meeting ground (everyone's garbage stinks equally)
- Town center as colonial film set
- Geography shapes the coming Battle of the Bands
- "When Nick and Jackie met in 1974, they were crossing more than a room"

---

### Chapter 4: The Belt Buckles That Started Everything
**Timeline**: September 1974 (age 12, middle school)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick and Jackie meet in the Ephraim Curtis Middle School cafeteria when Nick's chrome Aerosmith belt buckle reflects sunlight into Jackie's eyes (with his gold Queen buckle). United by hatred of disco music blasting in the cafeteria, they discover they're musical soulmates despite coming from opposite sides of Hop Brook.

**Key Beats**:
- Cafeteria scene: disco music everywhere (ABBA, Bee Gees, Chic)
- Belt buckle reflection moment (sun catching chrome)
- "Queen? Seriously?" / "What's wrong with Queen?"
- Mutual disgust at disco: "Can we agree that disco sucks?"
- First alliance: against disco, not against each other
- Jackie's Queen buckle = sophistication + accessibility
- Nick's Aerosmith buckle = raw power + blues danger
- "They'd found each other through music, and music would be the thing that brought them together and the thing that would eventually tear them apart"

---

### Chapter 5: Best Friends (Middle School)
**Timeline**: 1974-1975 (ages 12-13)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick and Jackie's friendship deepens over cassette tapes shared in the library, conservation land bike rides, dinners at each other's houses, and defending each other from bullies. Their differences (Jackie's new Raleigh vs. Nick's salvaged Schwinn) don't matter yet. Music is their bridge.

**Key Beats**:
- Library listening sessions: shared headphones, arguing Queen vs. Aerosmith
- Conservation land adventures (different bikes, same trails)
- Grace's initial suspicion, then acceptance of Jackie
- Mrs. Marsh welcoming Nick, seeing his genuine talent
- Doug Malone confrontation in hallway
- Nick defending Jackie: "People who mess with my friends have problems with me"
- First garage jam session: discovering their sounds blend
- Grace watches, proud but worried about dreams they can't afford
- "They were best friends in the way that only thirteen-year-old boys could be"

---

### Chapter 6: Jackie's World
**Timeline**: 1975 (age 13, established friendship)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Breakfast at the Marsh house reveals Dr. Marsh's hidden musical past and his pressure on Jackie toward Juilliard over rock dreams. Elizabeth (Jackie's younger sister) experiments with violin, blending classical and rock. Jackie plays "Bohemian Rhapsody" in his perfect bedroom, discovering freedom within the cage.

**Key Beats**:
- Formal Marsh breakfast: precision, order, expectations
- Dr. Marsh mentions Juilliard interest
- Jackie defends rock dreams: "That's what I want to do with my life"
- Revelation of Dr. Marsh's abandoned musical dreams (guitars traded for economics textbooks)
- Elizabeth upstairs playing violin with rock influences
- Jackie's perfect bedroom = beautiful cage
- Jackie at piano: classical training → "Bohemian Rhapsody" escape
- "Beauty wasn't the same as freedom. And freedom was what he needed."

---

### Chapter 7: Nick's World - The Garage and The Dream
**Timeline**: 1975 (age 13, before Dan's death)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Morning at the Johnson house shows Grace's financial worries and protective instincts about Nick's music dreams. Dan comes home from construction work, finds Nick practicing in the garage, and gives the blessing that will define everything: "Practice until your fingers bleed... That's how you make it in this world, son."

**Key Beats**:
- Grace at kitchen table with bills spread out like cards
- Dan still alive, coming home from construction site
- "Don't fill his head with dreams we can't afford" (Grace's fear)
- Garage session: Dan teaching Nick, "Let the notes breathe"
- Dan's philosophy: "The only regret I've ever seen is from people who never tried at all"
- Dan's hands: callused from construction, gentle with guitar
- Family dinner: Dan defending Nick's dreams to Grace
- Nick's promise: "I'll practice until I'm so good nobody can ignore me"
- Dan's tools still in use, Dan still teaching, Dan still alive
- The garage as sanctuary when father and son are both present

---

### Chapter 8: Maxanne - Before the Talent Show
**Timeline**: May 1975 / October 2009 (intercutting)  
**POV**: Maxanne (1st person, radio broadcast)

**Summary**: Maxanne reflects on the middle school talent show that changed everything. She explains how Nick and Jackie decided to enter together, created their Queen/Aerosmith mashup, and prepared to go public with their garage magic. She introduces Karen Bowers as the expected winner and the establishment's champion.

**Key Beats**:
- 2009 studio: 3 hours 47 minutes left
- Flashback to May 1975 decision to enter talent show
- Jackie's proposal: "We should enter... because nobody knows we're good except us"
- Three weeks of preparation: mashup of "Bohemian Rhapsody" and "Walk This Way"
- Karen Bowers introduced: private lessons, "Tomorrow" from Annie, inevitable winner
- Day of talent show: Nick and Jackie with guitars, no expectations
- "They weren't trying to win—they were trying to share"
- The risk of going public with something that mattered
- Setup for the performance that would change everything

---

### Chapter 9: The Talent Show
**Timeline**: May 15, 1975 (age 13)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick and Jackie perform their Queen/Aerosmith mashup at the Ephraim Curtis Middle School talent show and win, defeating the technically perfect Karen Bowers. The performance proves that collaboration creates something bigger than individual talent. This is the golden moment before the fall—captured in the photograph Wentworth will later show them.

**Key Beats**:
- Ephraim Curtis Middle School gymnasium, 7:30 PM
- Backstage: Karen Bowers warming up, Nick and Jackie quiet and ready
- First five acts: predictable middle school performances
- Nick and Jackie take the stage: acoustic guitars, nervous but determined
- Mashup performance: "Walk This Way" riff + "Bohemian Rhapsody" melody
- Nick's raw vocals + Jackie's classical harmonies = perfect fusion
- Audience transformation: complete silence, then standing ovation
- Grace crying (proud but worried), Dr. Marsh impressed but dismissive
- Karen Bowers performs "Tomorrow" perfectly but it's not enough anymore
- Victory: "Johnson & Marsh, First Place"
- The photograph: two 13-year-olds, arms around each other, guitars, smiling, innocent
- "This was the golden moment. This was the high point before the fall."

---

### Chapter 10: Summer Camp
**Timeline**: Summer 1975 (age 13-14)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick and Jackie attend Camp Winaukee music intensive, where they meet Patty Lally—a 14-year-old singer who sees their magic and warns them it won't survive the real world. The three create perfect harmonies and perform "Your Song." Patty's warning: "The world we're going back to isn't set up for this kind of thing."

**Key Beats**:
- Bus to Camp Winaukee: climbing away from Sudbury's class lines
- Camp director Mr. Freedman: "Music intensive. You'll be sharing with six other kids who think music is more important than sleep"
- Cabin 7: Nick and Jackie talking about their fathers' different philosophies
- Patty Lally arrives: wheat-colored hair, voice that makes everyone else try too hard
- The three become inseparable
- Working on "Your Song": Nick's raw power + Jackie's precision + Patty's heart
- End-of-camp performance: silence, then recognition
- Lakeside conversation: Patty's warning about the real world
- "This is real. Don't forget it."
- Last night promises: stay in touch, form a band, change the world
- Driving away in different directions: Nick in Grace's car, Jackie in Dr. Marsh's car
- Nick's realization: beautiful but temporary

---

### Chapter 11: The First Fracture
**Timeline**: Fall 1975 (freshman year, age 14)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: High school at Lincoln-Sudbury divides students into territories (Rampers, Rats, Regs, Rollers). Despite starting together, social forces pull Nick toward the Rollers' tables and Jackie toward the Rampers. The first real fracture: they sit at different lunch tables without anyone making a conscious decision. The garage sessions continue but feel strained.

**Key Beats**:
- First day at Lincoln-Sudbury: different universe from middle school
- Cafeteria territories explained: Rampers (elevated platform), Rats (glass corridor), Regs (middle tables), Rollers (auto shop entrance)
- Week two: Nick gravitating toward Rollers, kids who understand working with hands
- Carl Dukowski: "Your dad work construction?" / "He's a carpenter now"
- Jackie drawn toward Rampers, kids who assume college
- David Bellenghi: "Your dad teach at BU?"
- Week three: First time sitting at different tables
- Eye contact across cafeteria, neither waves
- Garage session feels strained: "You hanging out with those car kids?"
- Letters from Patty to both boys, both lie by omission about the growing distance
- November: Grace's car breaks down, Nick doesn't tell Jackie
- Jackie doesn't mention parental pressure about college
- "The cracks were forming"

---

### Chapter 12: Dan's Death
**Timeline**: November 14, 1977 (age 17, junior year)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Dan Johnson dies in a construction accident when a crane cable snaps, crushing him under steel beams. Grace gets the call at Friendly's during lunch rush. Nick is pulled from calculus class. The funeral shows the class divide even in grief. Jackie tries to help but Nick pushes him away: "I don't need your pity." The friendship that was already fracturing shatters completely.

**Key Beats**:
- Normal Monday morning: Dan leaves for Prudential Tower job site with tuna sandwich Grace made
- Nick asleep, stayed up late practicing "Dream On" solo Dan taught him
- 11:30 AM: Grace gets call at Friendly's during lunch rush
- "Accident. Crane. Steel beams. Instant death. I'm so sorry for your loss."
- Nick pulled from calculus class, Mrs. Patterson's careful tone
- Hospital: won't let Nick see body, too much trauma, closed casket
- Grace signing papers with shaking hands
- Next day: Jackie hears at school, feels the chasm, doesn't know what to say
- Jackie visits: "I'm here if you need anything"
- Nick can't speak, doesn't know how to explain this kind of loss
- Three days later: funeral at St. Mary's Church
- Working-class neighborhood turns out: union brothers, neighbors
- Grace in black, stoic, accepting condolences with dignity
- Nick in Dan's hand-me-down suit that doesn't fit
- Jackie and Dr. Marsh: perfect clothes, composed grief, different world
- Nick watches them and something hardens: "Even at his father's funeral... they looked perfect"
- Grace returns to work next day: can't afford time off
- Nick becomes man of house at 17
- Garage becomes place of ghosts: Dan's tools unused, guitar silent
- Jackie tries to reach out, Nick pushes away: "I don't want to talk about it"
- The words come out harsher than intended
- By summer: friends in name only, distance feels inevitable and heartbreaking
- "The friendship that was already fracturing shatters completely"

## ACT I CONCLUSION

By the end of Act I, we have:
- **Established the frame**: Maxanne's 2009 broadcast with 4 hours left
- **Shown the magic**: Belt buckles, talent show victory, summer camp perfection
- **Revealed the machinery**: Geography, class, high school social forces
- **Delivered the tragedy**: Dan's death and the friendship's death

**Transition to Act II**: Two years of silence. Nick and Jackie are now seniors. They don't speak. They've built separate lives. And then a poster appears that will force them into direct confrontation.
