# DRAFT 2: ACT II BLUEPRINT

## ACT II: THE BUILD (Chapters 13-25)

**Purpose**: Two years after Dan's death and the friendship's fracture, senior year brings the poster that will force Nick and Jackie into direct confrontation. The Battle of the Bands transforms from music competition into class war as the entire town chooses sides. By Act II's end, we're standing at the edge of the precipice—tomorrow night, everything explodes.

**Time Period**: April-May 1979 (with 2009 Maxanne interludes)

**Arc**:
- Setup: Maxanne's 2009 reflection on when friendship started dying
- Catalyst: The Battle of the Bands poster appears
- Escalation: School divides, town invests, parents battle at PTA
- Preparation: Three bands rehearse in three different worlds
- Precipice: Eve of the Battle—everything about to collide

**Key Locations**: WBCN Studio B (2009), Lincoln-Sudbury High School, Friendly's Restaurant, PTA meeting, Nick's garage, Jackie's basement, St. Michael's Church (Patty)

## ACT II CHAPTERS

### Chapter 13: Maxanne - When Friendship Started Dying
**Timeline**: November 2024 (3 hours 47 min left) / Flashback to 1977  
**POV**: Maxanne Sartori (1st person, radio broadcast)

**Summary**: Maxanne reflects on Dan Johnson's death (November 14, 1977) and how it shattered Nick, making grief build walls faster than friendship could climb them. She explains how Jackie tried to help but Nick pushed him away with "I don't need your pity." By spring 1978, they stopped even pretending—cafeteria became a map of class divisions, Nick with working-class kids, Jackie with college-bound crowd.

**Key Beats**:
- 3 hours left on WBCN's final broadcast
- Dan's death: construction accident, November 14, 1977
- "Grace went back to work the next day because the mortgage doesn't care"
- Nick stopped playing guitar after Dan died—every chord a eulogy
- Jackie tried to help: "I don't need your pity" / "I don't need anything from you"
- By spring 1978: hallway nods to strangers, separate cafeteria sections
- Music became language of separation (Aerosmith = survival, Queen = excellence)
- "Division always wins. Always."
- Transition: "Before I tell you about the Battle, you need to know about a bridge..."

### Chapter 14: The Bridge Where Friendship Died
**Timeline**: Fall 1978 (one year after Dan's death)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick on dirt bike meets Jackie on foot at the wooden bridge over Hop Brook. Dr. Marsh appears, calls Nick and friends "these boys," the pronoun that builds walls. Jackie steps aside. Nick revs his engine and rides across at full throttle with his friends, turning the walking bridge into a statement. Dr. Marsh's hand on Jackie's shoulder: not comforting—claiming. The friendship dies in that moment.

**Key Beats**:
- Saturday afternoon, October 1978, conservation land
- Nick leading pack on Honda CR125 rebuilt from scrap
- Jackie walking alone, thinking, trying to escape his father's house
- They meet at wooden bridge over Hop Brook
- Dr. Marsh emerges: "These boys bothering you, son?"
- The pronoun: "these boys" not classmates, not neighbors
- "Perhaps you boys could find another trail more appropriate for your vehicles"
- Jackie steps aside, gives ground, chooses peace
- Nick rides across at full throttle, four bikes screaming defiance
- Dr. Marsh's hand on Jackie's shoulder: claiming, not comforting
- Jackie looks back once, says nothing, follows his father
- "Some walls last longer than the people who build them"

### Chapter 15: The Poster
**Timeline**: April 2, 1979 (senior year)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: The Battle of the Bands poster appears on the Lincoln-Sudbury music room bulletin board. $500 prize + recording session, sponsored by WBCN, May 15th. Nick and Jackie see it simultaneously from opposite sides. Bob Wentworth tries to reunite them, shows them the 1975 talent show photograph. They refuse: "That was middle school. We're different." The entire school divides into Team Aerosmith vs Team Queen, crossing old social boundaries.

**Key Beats**:
- 7:30 AM, Lincoln-Sudbury music room
- Poster: "$500 and professional recording session / WBCN sponsored"
- Nick and Jackie approach from opposite sides
- Bob Wentworth: "Sometimes the best way forward is to remember where you started"
- Wentworth shows 1975 photograph: two 13-year-olds, arms around each other
- "That was middle school. This is different." / "We're different." / "Yeah. We are."
- "May the best band win" / "You can count on it"
- Posters multiply throughout school by lunch
- School divides: Rampers with Rats, Regs with Rollers—new alliances forming
- Kids from different worlds talking for first time in years
- "The Battle of the Bands was six weeks away. Lincoln-Sudbury had chosen sides."

### Chapter 16: Senior Year - Nick's World
**Timeline**: April 1979 (three weeks after poster)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick sits with Lincoln boys (Stephen, Mark) in cafeteria. Jackie stands up and performs "Somebody to Love" a cappella—technically flawless. Nick responds with "Dream On"—rougher but honest. Principal Sargent calls them both to his office, threatens to cancel competition, but ultimately allows it to continue. That evening, Nick practices alone in Dan's garage while Grace listens from the doorway.

**Key Beats**:
- Cafeteria divided into two camps, not four quadrants
- Jackie stands: "Somebody to Love" performed for cafeteria
- Nick responds: "Dream On" a cappella with Stephen and Mark
- Both performances brilliant in different ways
- Principal Sargent's office: threatens to shut down competition
- "I should shut the whole thing down. But we've got other schools entering..."
- "Keep your musical differences off school property until May 15th"
- Evening: Nick in garage with Dan's tools, poster above workbench
- Grace appears in doorway, Friendly's uniform, hands red from dishwater
- "Your father would've liked this. You competing."
- "Play it like Dan's watching"
- Nick plays alone until after midnight

### Chapter 17: Senior Year - Jackie's World
**Timeline**: April 1979 (same week as Chapter 16)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Dinner at the Marsh house reveals Jackie's Juilliard acceptance (early admission, full scholarship). Dr. Marsh adds pressure: Jordan Hall recital April 20th (three weeks before Battle). Jackie insists he can do both. Elizabeth sees her brother's trap—he's already proven himself but keeps trying for their father's approval. Jackie practices Chopin upstairs, then plays "Bohemian Rhapsody" alone—the way Mercury wrote it, without making chaos civilized.

**Key Beats**:
- 6:30 PM dinner, four people at table that seats twelve
- Eileen announces: "Jackie received his acceptance letter from Juilliard today"
- Dr. Marsh adds Jordan Hall recital April 20th (classical, prestigious)
- Jackie: "I can do both"
- Dr. Marsh: "This high school competition... it's beneath your level"
- "Help me understand why this high school competition matters so much to you"
- Jackie can't explain: *Because I need to know if I can make something real*
- Elizabeth upstairs after dinner: "You know he doesn't ask me to practice anymore, right?"
- "You're trying to make him hear the music. But he doesn't hear music. He counts notes."
- Jackie alone: plays "Bohemian Rhapsody" the way Mercury intended
- Photograph from 1975 talent show—he puts it face-down in drawer
- "Some performances you give for other people. Some prisons you build yourself."

### Chapter 18: The Town Weighs In
**Timeline**: Late April 1979 (two weeks before Battle)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Grace notices Sudbury choosing sides—grocery store, dry cleaners, everyone has opinions. Mary Murphy tells Grace that Dr. Marsh wants to cancel the competition at Thursday's PTA meeting. Grace attends (still in Friendly's uniform) and delivers devastating speech: "Dr. Marsh wants to cancel because his son might lose." PTA votes 42-11 to keep competition. The Battle is nine days away.

**Key Beats**:
- Grace at Star Market, cashier asking if Nick is "ready for that kind of pressure"
- Sudbury Diner: people betting on high school kids
- Mary Murphy: "Frank at the barber shop has a pool going"
- Dr. Marsh calling principal: "turning a musical competition into class warfare"
- Thursday PTA meeting emergency session about "tension in school"
- Dr. Marsh's proposal: cancel or postpone to "restore harmony"
- Grace arrives late, still wearing Friendly's uniform
- Grace's speech: "My son practices in a garage his father built before he died"
- "Dr. Marsh wants to cancel because his son might lose to someone like mine"
- "If Jackie Marsh—with all his advantages—loses to a kid from a garage, what does that say about the world he believes in?"
- Vote: 42-11 to keep competition
- Grace in car afterward, hands shaking
- Nine days until Battle

### Chapter 19: Grace Loses Her Grace
**Timeline**: May 1, 1979 (eight days before Battle)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: At Friendly's, Dr. Taub lectures Grace about "realistic expectations" for working-class kids in music. Grace finally snaps: "You're telling me my son shouldn't compete because he's working class. Because his mother's a waitress and his father died in an accident." She tells him off publicly. Manager Tom Andrews lectures her about professionalism, then gives her a 15-cent raise. Story spreads through Sudbury. Nick comes to Friendly's after practice. Battle is eight days away.

**Key Beats**:
- Friendly's booth twelve: Dr. Taub reading about Battle in Sudbury Weekly
- Taub's condescension: "talent alone isn't enough... proper background"
- Grace's response: "Proper background. My son has been playing since he was eight."
- "You're telling me my son shouldn't compete because he's working class?"
- Grace's eruption: "You're being condescending. You're telling me success goes to people who can afford it."
- Tom Andrews calls her to back room
- Lecture about professionalism... then 15-cent raise
- Story spreads through Sudbury by Monday
- Nick comes to Friendly's: "Did you really tell off Dr. Patterson?"
- "I told him the truth. That you deserve a chance. Same as anyone."
- "You didn't have to do that." / "Yes, I did."

### Chapter 20: Teachers Who Fanned the Flames
**Timeline**: May 5, 1979 (two days before Battle)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Wentworth tries to reunite Nick and Jackie by having them play Brubeck's "Take Five" together in jazz band. For 30 seconds it works—the old harmony returning. Then Don Gould walks in, turns it into a history lesson about "American democracy," and destroys the moment. Nick explodes, Jackie walks out cold. The confrontation turns personal: bridge incident referenced, years of pain exposed. Forty-one hours until Battle.

**Key Beats**:
- Tuesday jazz band rehearsal, two days before Battle
- Wentworth: "Sometimes the best way forward is to remember where you started"
- "Take Five" in 5/4 time—Jackie's piano, Nick's guitar
- For 30 seconds: magic returns, the old harmony
- Don Gould walks in: "Sounds like democracy in action!"
- "Lincoln-Douglas debates... Jacksonian vs. Jeffersonian ideals..."
- Nick snaps: "Between the kids who matter and the kids who don't?"
- "Maybe die quick like him too. That American enough?"
- Jackie: first time saying Nick's name in two years
- Nick: "Your dad made it pretty clear. These boys don't understand boundaries."
- Jackie: "Because I was trying to avoid this!"
- Nick: "Without admitting Hop Brook might as well be the Atlantic Ocean?"
- Jackie walks out: "Fine. Friday night, we'll see whose America wins."
- Wentworth alone with 1975 photograph
- "You can't bridge a divide by explaining it"

### Chapter 21: Nick's Rehearsal
**Timeline**: May 8, 1979 (day before Battle)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Nick and his band (Stephen, Mark, David) rehearse in Dan's garage for four hours. They run through "Dream On," "Sweet Emotion," "Walk This Way," "Toys in the Attic." Band members express loyalty: not because of class war, but because Nick made them believe they could be a band. Grace listens from driveway, worried about what winning or losing will do to Nick. Battle is eighteen hours away.

**Key Beats**:
- Garage smells like old concrete and machine grease
- Four hours of practice: Walking the Line
- "What if we're not good enough?" / "We are."
- Running through Aerosmith set: raw, powerful, authentic
- Stephen: "We're with you. Not because of your mom's speech... because you made us believe"
- Mark: "You didn't laugh when I showed up with my grandmother's old Casio"
- David: "You taught me how to hold the sticks properly"
- Grace outside listening, afraid of what happens if he loses OR wins
- "Would music take him away from her too? Would he chase that dream until it killed him?"
- Grace comes to garage: "Your father would be proud"
- "You already are good enough. Tomorrow's not about proving anything."
- Nick alone afterward: Dan's Craftsman toolbox, latches rusted, Grace can't open it
- "He wasn't playing for Dan's memory anymore. He was playing for the woman who'd stood up to the world."
- Eighteen hours until Battle

### Chapter 23: Maxanne - The Eve of Battle
**Timeline**: October 2024 (3 hours into broadcast, 1 hour left) / May 9, 1979  
**POV**: Maxanne Sartori (1st person, radio broadcast)

**Summary**: Maxanne confesses: the Battle of the Bands was her idea. She and Tom Andrews pitched it as marketing genius—discover talent, drive listeners, attract sponsors. She thought she could control outcomes, make stars. Instead, she gave class warfare a microphone, turned private tragedy into public spectacle, sold tickets to watch innocence die. She left WBCN in 1980, went corporate, survived by becoming the machinery. Now, 44 years later, she tells the truth before the lights go out.

**Key Beats**:
- 3 hours in, 1 hour left, Studio B empty
- "The Battle of the Bands was my idea"
- Pitched fall 1978: regional competition, WBCN sponsorship, marketing campaign
- "I thought I could game the system. Use capitalism to fund rebellion."
- Track record: discovered Aerosmith 1973, broke The Cars, gave Boston airtime
- "I thought I was the architect of success. Turns out I was the architect of tragedy."
- March 15, 1979: 985 seats packed, judging between two other DJs
- Nick's band: "duct-taped equipment and more hunger than technical skill"
- Jackie's band: "technical precision... Empty as a mansion with no one home"
- "I watched their friendship continue to drown in front of 985 witnesses"
- Confession: "I didn't create their division. But I gave class warfare a microphone."
- "I took that slow, private death and turned it into public spectacle"
- Left WBCN in 1980, went corporate: "I was fleeing. Not from failure—from success."
- 44 years in corporate music: "The rebel DJ became a corporate suit"
- "Nick stayed real. System crushed him. Jackie compromised. System crushed him. I fled. System followed me."
- "The labyrinth has no exit"
- Playing "Dream On": "Twenty-four hours before I learned I couldn't save anyone. Including myself."

### Chapter 24: Patty Returns
**Timeline**: May 9, 1979 (day before Battle)  
**POV**: Third person

**Summary**: Patty sits alone in St. Michael's Church with letters from Nick and Jackie. Four years since Camp Winaukee, she's had lunch with both separately over the years. Nick's lunches at Friendly's—guitar became his father's ghost, grief wearing guitar strap. Jackie's lunches at café—piano became his prison, seeking approval that will never come. Tomorrow, she'll enter their war not to fix them, but to show what music looks like when you're not trying to prove anything.

**Key Beats**:
- St. Michael's Church, Concord, after bandmates leave
- Seven letters from two boys, folded and refolded in camp notebook
- Nick's handwriting: cramped, urgent, talking about Dan's death
- Jackie's handwriting: precise, measured, college prep and competitions
- Lunches over four years: separately, different stories, same events
- Nick at Friendly's: "I'm getting better... Mom says I'm obsessed"
- "This isn't music anymore. This is grief wearing a guitar strap."
- Jackie at café: "When I play Queen, I can breathe. Does that make sense?"
- "This isn't music anymore. This is seeking approval that will never come."
- Tomorrow: she'll play music as it's meant to be played
- "Not to save them. They were already lost. Just to show them what they were missing."
- "She was free and she knew it"

### Chapter 25: Eve of the Battle
**Timeline**: May 14, 1979 (night before Battle)  
**POV**: Third person (omniscient, three locations)

**Summary**: Final rehearsals in three locations. Nick's band in auto shop bay—practical, superstitious, fighting fate with Aerosmith covers. Jackie's band in school band room—engineering precision, perfection as armor, Queen songs dissected and perfected. Patty's band in St. Michael's Church—barefoot, faithful, Fleetwood Mac as grace and gut. Three bands, three worlds, all converging on tomorrow night's reckoning.

**Key Beats**:
- Three bands, three sanctuaries, three different preparations

**Nick & Walking the Line (Auto shop bay three):**
- Smell: hot metal, rubber, sweat, grease
- Joe Pacenka watching: grownup who saw music as keeping shadows away
- No sheet music, no tuning forks—just ears and memory
- "Walk This Way," "Dream On," "Sweet Emotion," "Train Kept A-Rollin'"
- "Nothing was neat, but everything was vital"
- Practical details: tape on cables, backup pick, amp protection
- Nick alone after: playing solo Dan taught him
- "Music sometimes just wasn't enough. Not to bring Dan back."

**Jackie & Crown Jewels (School band room):**
- Bright as operating theater, polished, antiseptic
- Elizabeth on violin, Ace on drums, Max on bass
- Setup pristine: fresh reeds, new strings, catalogued
- "Bohemian Rhapsody," "Somebody to Love," "We Will Rock You," "We Are the Champions"
- Every passage twice, then third time, half and full tempo
- "Perfection wasn't just a standard, it was armor"
- Jackie alone after: pressing keys with panic
- "Tomorrow's stakes: not just trophy, but finally answering what sort of music counted"

**Patty & Rumours (St. Michael's Church):**
- Incense, rain through stained glass, joy in rafters
- Patty barefoot on altar rug, Robin, Kristy, Steve
- No charts, no roadies, no plan—just four kids and faith
- "Dreams," "Go Your Own Way," "Rhiannon"
- Prep about feeling, not precision
- Patty alone after: "She couldn't save anyone, but she could remind them what was worth playing for"

**The Eve:**
- "Three bands, three rooms, each chasing excellence by their own definition"
- "Tomorrow wasn't just a concert—it was a reckoning"
- "Spotlights would amplify every crack and every triumph"
- "Three bands burned for a little glory and a shot at rewriting the ending"
- "Under yellow light and linoleum, that hope felt real enough to sing"
