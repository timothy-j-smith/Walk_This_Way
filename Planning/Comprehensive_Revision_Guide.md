# Comprehensive Revision Guide for "Walk This Way"

## Overview
This guide provides a systematic approach to revising your 80,000-word manuscript, focusing on eliminating repetitive language and developing Maxanne's authentic voice.

## Phase 1: Word Replacement Strategy

### Primary Targets for Replacement

#### "Frequency" (28 instances) → Alternatives:
- **signal** - radio term, fits Maxanne's background
- **wavelength** - radio metaphor, more concrete
- **connection** - simpler, more direct
- **harmony** - musical term, fits the story
- **vibration** - more visceral, physical
- **resonance** - deeper emotional meaning
- **channel** - radio terminology
- **tone** - musical/audio term

#### "Static" (15 instances) → Alternatives:
- **noise** - simple, effective
- **interference** - radio term, fits context
- **distortion** - audio term, more specific
- **crackle** - specific sound description
- **hiss** - concrete audio detail
- **clutter** - visual metaphor
- **chaos** - emotional term
- **confusion** - clearer meaning

### Implementation Strategy:
1. **Search and replace systematically** - one word at a time
2. **Vary the replacements** - don't use the same substitute repeatedly
3. **Consider context** - choose words that fit the specific scene
4. **Maintain voice consistency** - ensure replacements sound like Maxanne

## Phase 2: Maxanne's Voice Enhancement

### Voice Characteristics to Strengthen:

#### 1. Boston Rock Radio Veteran
**Current:** Basic radio references
**Enhanced:** Specific, insider knowledge
- "Steven Tyler's hair got caught in the mixing board back in '73"
- "you could buy the entire block of Lansdowne Street for that in '77"
- "sound system held together with duct tape and prayers"

#### 2. Stephen King-Style Descriptions
**Current:** Abstract or simple
**Enhanced:** Concrete, visceral, atmospheric

**Examples:**
- "The bourbon burns" → "The bourbon burns like liquid fire, the good kind that makes you remember why you started drinking"
- "Outside, Boston glitters" → "Outside, Boston glitters like broken glass in a parking lot, all sharp edges and dangerous beauty"
- "The ON AIR sign pulses" → "The ON AIR sign pulses like a dying heartbeat"

#### 3. Dark Humor & Self-Deprecation
**Current:** Some humor present
**Enhanced:** More consistent, sharper wit

**Examples:**
- "arthritis now, too many decades of spinning vinyl"
- "who's going to fire me now?"
- "The irony was so thick you could spread it on toast"

#### 4. Working-Class Authenticity
**Current:** Some references
**Enhanced:** More consistent working-class sensibility

**Examples:**
- Specific economic details
- Practical, no-nonsense language
- Respect for real work and struggle
- Disdain for pretense

### Voice Test Questions:
- Does this sound like a 73-year-old radio veteran?
- Would Maxanne really say this on air?
- Does it maintain her bitter-sweet personality?
- Is the humor authentic to her character?
- Does it add concrete imagery?

## Phase 3: Systematic Chapter Revision

### For Each Chapter:

#### Step 1: Word Replacement
1. Search for "frequency" instances
2. Replace with varied alternatives (signal, wavelength, connection, etc.)
3. Search for "static" instances  
4. Replace with varied alternatives (noise, interference, distortion, etc.)
5. Check for other repetitive words

#### Step 2: Voice Enhancement
1. Identify abstract descriptions
2. Replace with concrete, visceral imagery
3. Add Boston/radio-specific details
4. Strengthen humor where appropriate
5. Ensure working-class authenticity

#### Step 3: Read Aloud Test
1. Read the revised chapter aloud
2. Does it sound like Maxanne talking?
3. Is the pacing natural?
4. Are the descriptions vivid?
5. Does the humor land?

## Phase 4: Quality Checklist

### For Each Revised Section:
- [ ] No repetitive "frequency" or "static"
- [ ] Concrete, visceral descriptions
- [ ] Boston/radio-specific details
- [ ] Authentic Maxanne voice
- [ ] Appropriate humor
- [ ] Working-class sensibility

- [ ] Vivid imagery
