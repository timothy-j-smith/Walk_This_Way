## CHAPTER 29: PATTY'S PERFORMANCE

### **Rumours (8:40 PM)**

And then it was time for Rumours.

Patty Lally walked onto the stage alone, carrying her acoustic guitar like it was a weapon and a shield all at once. She wasn't from Lincoln-Sudbury—she was from Concord-Carlisle, the neighboring town that had produced its own share of musicians and dreamers. But she'd heard about the Battle of the Bands, and she'd decided that someone needed to remind everyone what music was really about.

She looked out at the audience, at the northeast section where Nick's people cheered for authenticity, at the southwest section where Jackie's people applauded technical excellence, at the northwest and southeast sections where everyone else tried to figure out which side they were supposed to be on.

And then she began to speak.

"I'm Patty Lally," she said, her voice carrying the particular authority that comes from knowing exactly who you are. "I'm from Concord, and I'm here to remind you that music isn't about competition. It's about connection."

The audience was silent, waiting to see what this girl from the next town over was going to do.

### **"Summer of Thirteen"**

Patty's first song was called "Summer of Thirteen," and it was about the summer when she'd met Nick and Jackie at Camp Winnipesaukee, when they'd all been kids who believed that music could bridge any divide.

*"Summer of thirteen, everything was possible*
*Summer of thirteen, we were all invincible*
*Summer of thirteen, music was our language*
*Summer of thirteen, before the world explained"*

The song was simple, honest, direct. Patty wasn't trying to impress anyone with technical skill or theatrical flair. She was trying to remind everyone of something they'd forgotten—that music was supposed to be about connection, not competition.

The audience began to understand. This wasn't just another band trying to win a prize. This was someone trying to remind them of what they'd lost.

### **"What We Lost"**

For her second song, Patty chose "What We Lost," and this was where the magic really began.

*"What we lost when we learned to compete*
*What we lost when we learned to divide*
*What we lost when we learned that music*
*Was something we had to win, not something we had to share"*

The song was about the Battle of the Bands itself, about how competition had turned music into a weapon instead of a bridge. Patty was singing about Nick and Jackie, about how their friendship had been destroyed by the very thing that should have strengthened it.

*"We used to harmonize, now we harmonize*
*We used to create, now we compete*
*We used to be friends, now we're enemies*
*Because someone told us that's how it's supposed to be"*

Nick and Jackie, standing in the wings, looked at each other. They'd never heard anyone sing about their friendship before, never heard anyone acknowledge what they'd lost. Patty was singing their story, and she was singing it with the kind of understanding that only comes from someone who'd been there when it all began.

### **"The Frequency Still Exists"**

For her final song, Patty chose "The Frequency Still Exists," and this was where she changed everything.

*"The frequency still exists, if you're brave enough to find it*
*The frequency still exists, if you're willing to listen*
*The frequency still exists, in the space between the notes*
*The frequency still exists, where all voices matter"*

The song was about the concept she'd introduced to Nick and Jackie at summer camp—the idea that there was a frequency where all voices could harmonize, where differences dissolved into something beautiful, where competition became collaboration.

*"Nick and Jackie, I know you're listening*
*I know you remember what we used to be*
*I know you remember the summer of thirteen*
*When music was magic, not a weapon"*

Patty was singing directly to them, calling them out by name, reminding them of what they'd once had. The audience was silent, understanding that they were witnessing something important, something that transcended the competition.

*"The frequency still exists, if you're brave enough to find it*
*The frequency still exists, if you're willing to listen*
*The frequency still exists, in the space between the notes*
*The frequency still exists, where all voices matter"*

When she finished, the auditorium was completely silent. Not the silence of confusion, but the silence of recognition. Everyone understood that they'd just heard something that mattered more than winning or losing.

Patty had just reminded them of what music was supposed to be about.

The applause was thunderous, but it wasn't about her performance. It was about the truth she'd spoken, the reminder she'd given them, the possibility she'd shown them.

The frequency still existed. They just had to be brave enough to find it.

---

**Transition:** As Patty left the stage, the energy in the auditorium was transformed. She'd reminded everyone of what music was supposed to be about—connection, not competition. But the night wasn't over yet. Walking the Line was about to take the stage, and Nick Johnson was about to prove that authenticity could be just as powerful as the truth Patty had spoken.
