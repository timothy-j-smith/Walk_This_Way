### **EXAMPLE: WALK THIS WAY (First 3 Sections)**

```markdown
# PROSE DNA - "WALK THIS WAY"
**Last Updated:** October 22, 2025
**Status:** Act II (Chapters 13-22 edited) | Target: 80,000 words

---

## VOICE TARGET

**Richard Russo + Dennis Lehane**
- Compassionate clarity (Russo)
- Working-class directness (Lehane)
- Emotional truth through concrete detail
- No sentimentality, no preaching

**Core Principle:** Show, don't tell. Trust the reader.

---

## WHAT WORKS (Examples from Act II Edits)

### **Direct, Earned Emotion:**
✅ "Classical earned his father's approval. Rock earned his breath. He couldn't have both."
✅ "He wasn't playing for Dan's memory anymore. He was playing for the woman who'd stood up to the world and said her son mattered."
✅ "Three years and Grace still couldn't bring herself to open it."

### **Class Divide Through Detail:**
✅ Equipment his father had bought, piece by piece—each purchase a down payment on Jackie's future
✅ The garage smelled like old concrete and machine grease—the kind that soaks into floors and never leaves
✅ Grace's Friendly's uniform, worn sneakers, smelling like fryer oil

### **Emotional Weight in Simple Facts:**
✅ "Don't hold back, he thought. Just this once, don't hold back."
✅ Dr. Marsh: "You've taken his chaos and made it... civilized."
✅ Elizabeth: "He doesn't hear music, Jack. He counts notes like credits on a transcript."

**Pattern:** Concrete detail carries emotion. Facts are devastating. No need to explain.

---

## WHAT DOESN'T WORK (What We Cut)

### **Purple Prose & Overwrought Metaphors:**
❌ "clutching guitars like talismans against social hierarchies"
❌ "something was missing" (repeated 4x in one chapter)
❌ "the frequency, as Patty had called it"

### **Abstract Philosophy:**
❌ "emotional truth" (too vague)
❌ "working-class authenticity enhanced Jackie's classical training"

### **Repetition:**
❌ "callused hands/fingers" (used 6x - replaced with: work-worn, rough, split-knuckled)
❌ "equipment that cost more than families' groceries" (cliché comparison)

**Pattern:** If it sounds "literary" or self-conscious, cut it. Direct is better.
```

## **TEMPLATE 2: CHARACTER_DEPTH_HIERARCHY.md**

**(This file is UNIVERSAL - same for every novel)**

```markdown
# CHARACTER DEPTH HIERARCHY
**The Scalpel & Hose System**
**Universal Template - Use for All Novels**

---

## THE THREE TIERS

### **TIER 1 - MAIN CHARACTERS**
**Examples:** Protagonist, Antagonist, Love Interest, Mentor, Primary Family Members

**REQUIREMENTS:**
- **Always show COST of their choices**
- **What are they AFRAID of?** (specific, not abstract)
- **One internal moment per chapter** showing WHY they choose what they choose
- **Motivations visible** through action + brief internal thought

**THE QUESTION TO ASK:**
*"What does this choice COST this character? What are they giving up?"*

**EXAMPLE (Walk This Way):**
- Nick: Afraid of letting Grace down after she risked everything for him
- Jackie: Afraid of never being enough for his father, no matter what he achieves
- Grace: Afraid Nick's obsession will destroy him OR take him away (dual fear)
- Dr. Marsh: Afraid of falling back to middle class (ying-yang: controlling + caring)

---

### **TIER 2 - SUPPORTING CHARACTERS**
**Examples:** Best friend, sibling, mentor, band member, rival

**REQUIREMENTS:**
- **ONE defining moment** that shows who they are
- **Reveals why they matter** to the protagonist
- **Specific detail** that makes them real, not cardboard

**THE QUESTION TO ASK:**
*"What is the ONE moment that shows this person's character?"*

**EXAMPLE (Walk This Way):**
- Elizabeth (13, violin prodigy): "He doesn't hear music. He counts notes like credits on a transcript."
- Stephen (bass player): "We're with you because you made us believe we could be a band."
- Mark (keyboard): "You were the only kid who didn't laugh at my grandmother's Casio."

---

### **TIER 3 - BACKGROUND CHARACTERS**
**Examples:** Teachers, parents at PTA, customers, town gossips

**REQUIREMENTS:**
- **They are FORCES, not people**
- **Show WHAT they do, not WHY**
- **Concrete details** (how they look, what they say) but NO internal life

**THE QUESTION TO ASK:**
*"Does this character's motivation affect the plot? If NO → Tier 3."*

**EXAMPLE (Walk This Way):**
- Dr. Taub: Shows up, says condescending things, creates pressure (we don't need his backstory)
- PTA parents: React to Grace's speech (atmosphere, not people)

---

## THE GOLDEN RULE

**If a character makes a choice that AFFECTS THE PLOT:**
→ Show WHY (Tier 1 or 2)

**If a character is BACKDROP or ATMOSPHERE:**
→ Show WHAT (Tier 3)

---

## HOW TO APPLY THIS

### **When Writing a New Scene:**
1. Identify which tier each character is
2. For Tier 1: What's at stake? What's the cost? What are they afraid of?
3. For Tier 2: Is this their defining moment? If not, keep them brief.
4. For Tier 3: Concrete detail, then move on.

### **When Editing:**
1. Scan for "cardboard" characters (no defining trait)
2. Ask: Should they be Tier 2? If yes, add ONE moment.
3. Ask: Are we over-explaining Tier 3? If yes, cut to basics.

---

## THE TEST

**Read a chapter. Ask:**
- Can I picture each Tier 1 character's FEAR?
- Does each Tier 2 character have ONE moment I remember?
- Are Tier 3 characters staying in the background?

**If YES to all three → You nailed it.**

---

**This system works for every novel. Don't change it. Just apply it.**
```

## **TEMPLATE 3: SCALPEL_HOSE_CHECKLIST.md**

**(This file is UNIVERSAL - same for every novel)**

```markdown
# SCALPEL & HOSE CHECKLIST
**The Blood Brothers Editing System**
**Universal Template - Use for All Novels**

---

## 🔪 SCALPEL (What to Cut)

### **BEFORE YOU START:**
□ Read chapter once through (don't edit yet)
□ Note word count (you'll cut 40-60%)
□ Identify the "money moment" (the scene that HAS to work)

### **CUT THESE:**
□ **Repetitive phrases** - Scan for words/phrases used 3+ times
□ **Purple prose** - Overwrought metaphors, "literary" language, showing off
□ **Abstract emotions** - "He felt sad" → Show through action/detail
□ **Named emotions** - "She was nervous" → "Her hands shook"
□ **Over-explaining themes** - Trust the reader to get it
□ **Section headers** - They break narrative flow
□ **Redundant descriptions** - First mention only, then shorthand
□ **Cliché comparisons** - "Like a [overused metaphor]"
□ **Telling what we just showed** - If you showed it, don't explain it

### **PATTERNS TO WATCH:**
□ Same descriptive word used multiple times (rough/callused/worn)
□ "With the kind of..." construction (usually unnecessary)
□ "[Abstract quality] that couldn't be [verb]" (vague)
□ Sentences over 40 words (break them up)
□ Multiple metaphors in one paragraph (pick one, kill the rest)

---

## 🚿 HOSE (What to Add)

### **ADD THESE:**
□ **Character's specific fear** - Not "afraid to fail" but "afraid of seeing that look on Mom's face"
□ **COST of their choice** - What are they giving up? What's at stake?
□ **Concrete sensory detail** - Smell, texture, sound (not generic)
□ **One "money sentence"** - The line readers will underline
□ **Motivation BEFORE action** - Brief internal thought showing WHY, then WHAT
□ **Defining moment** (Tier 2 characters) - ONE scene that shows who they are
□ **Ying-yang complexity** (Tier 1) - Show contradictions, not caricatures

### **THE MONEY SENTENCE:**
□ Does this chapter have ONE line that hits the chest?
□ Does it EARN the emotion (concrete detail, not abstract)?
□ Example: "He wasn't playing for Dan's memory anymore. He was playing for the woman who'd stood up to the world and said her son mattered."

### **CHARACTER DEPTH CHECK:**
□ Tier 1: Do we see their FEAR? Do we see the COST?
□ Tier 2: Do they have ONE defining moment?
□ Tier 3: Are they forces, not people? (Don't over-develop)

---

## 📏 TARGET METRICS

### **WORD COUNT:**
- **Target per chapter:** 1,200-1,500 words
- **Starting draft:** Usually 2,000-3,000 words (you'll cut 40-60%)
- **Every sentence must EARN its place**

### **RHYTHM:**
- **Vary sentence length** - Short for impact. Long for complexity.
- **One-sentence paragraphs** - Use sparingly for emphasis
- **White space** - Break up dense paragraphs

### **PACING:**
- **Each chapter = 3-5 beats** (mini-scenes/shifts)
- **Clear transitions** between beats
- **End on forward momentum** (question, choice, or change)

---

## 🎯 THE EDITING PROCESS

### **STEP 1: ASSESS (Don't edit yet)**
1. Read chapter through
2. Identify repetition (words/phrases used 3+ times)
3. Find the "money moment" (scene that HAS to work)
4. Note which characters appear (Tier 1/2/3?)

### **STEP 2: SCALPEL (Cut 40-60%)**
1. Remove section headers
2. Cut repetitive phrases
3. Eliminate purple prose
4. Remove abstract emotions
5. Delete over-explaining

### **STEP 3: HOSE (Add depth)**
1. Add character's specific fear
2. Show COST of their choice
3. Add concrete sensory detail
4. Craft money sentence
5. Give Tier 2 characters defining moment

### **STEP 4: POLISH**
1. Read aloud (catches awkward rhythm)
2. Check sentence variety
3. Verify timeline/consistency
4. Confirm word count (1,200-1,500)

---

## ✅ FINAL CHECKLIST

**Before calling a chapter "done":**
□ Word count: 1,200-1,500
□ Repetition eliminated (no word used 3+ times)
□ Purple prose cut
□ Tier 1 characters: FEAR and COST visible
□ Tier 2 characters: ONE defining moment
□ Money sentence present and earned
□ Every sentence earns its place
□ Timeline consistent
□ Reads smoothly aloud

**If all boxes checked → Chapter is SINGING.**

---

**Use this checklist for EVERY chapter edit. The system works. Trust it.**
```
