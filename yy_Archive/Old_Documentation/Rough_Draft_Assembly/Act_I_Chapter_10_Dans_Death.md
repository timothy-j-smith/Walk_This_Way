Dan Johnson had died on November fourteenth, 1977 [CHANGE TO SPRING 1976], a Tuesday morning so ordinary that nobody saw tragedy coming until it arrived with sirens and flashing lights. He was working the Prudential Tower project, thirty-seven stories of Boston ambition rising from the Back Bay like a middle finger pointed at the sky. Dan ran a crane, one of those massive yellow beasts that could lift ten tons of steel beam like it was picking up a pencil.

The cable snapped at 10:17 AM. OSHA would later determine that the cable had been scheduled for replacement two weeks earlier, but the general contractor had delayed the order to save money on a project already running over budget. The load—four steel I-beams destined for the thirty-second floor—fell like God's judgment onto the construction trailer where Dan had been checking blueprints, eating a tuna sandwich Grace had made that morning, probably thinking about Nick's algebra grades or the mortgage payment due the following week.

They said he died instantly, which is what they always tell the families because instant death is supposed to be a mercy, as if the speed of dying somehow makes up for the fact of it. Grace never believed them. She knew Dan well enough to know he would have fought it, would have tried to dive under the desk or behind the file cabinet, would have spent his last seconds thinking about getting home to his wife and seventeen-year-old [CHANGE TO FIFTEEN-YEAR-OLD] son who was learning guitar in the garage Dan had built with his own hands.
