# ARTIFACT 1: POV Writing Approach for "Walk This Way"

## Summary of POV Discussion

### Key Issues Identified:
- **Current Problem:** Maxanne narrates everything, creating emotional distance from Nick and Jackie
- **Missing Element:** Direct access to main characters' thoughts and feelings
- **Solution:** Hybrid POV structure with clear transitions

### Recommended Structure:
- **Maxanne's Voice:** Framing device (opening, major transitions, closing)
- **Third-Person Omniscient:** Main 1979 story with full character access
- **Clear Transitions:** Bridging lines between POV shifts

## POV Transition Examples

### Example 1: Maxanne → Third-Person (Chapter Opening)

**Maxanne's Voice (2024):**
```
"I need to tell you about two kids who never made it onto my playlist. But first, I need to take you back to when those legends were just beginning to cast shadows, when the real battles were fought not in recording studios but in the divided territories of suburban high schools."
```

**Transition Line:**
```
March, 1979. Disco was dying its overdue death. The Clash were about to teach America that punk had something to say. And in Sudbury, Massachusetts—that's about twenty miles west of here for you newcomers—two seventeen-year-old boys were about to go to war over a question that seems quaint now: What is authentic rock and roll?
```

**Third-Person Scene Begins:**
```
Nick Johnson's fingers found notes that shouldn't have been possible on a guitar held together with electrical tape and determination. The instrument had cost twenty-five dollars at a pawn shop in Framingham, but in his hands it became something else entirely.

He was thinking about Jackie Marsh, about how they'd gone from inseparable best friends to strangers who barely acknowledged each other in the high school halls. Four years. That's all it had taken for America's class divisions to do what they'd always been designed to do—separate kids who should have stayed together.
```

### Example 2: Third-Person → Maxanne (Chapter Transition)

**Third-Person Scene Ends:**
```
When they finished, the silence in that garage was holy. Not church holy—none of us were the church type—but sacred in the way that only truth can be sacred, the kind of quiet that happens after something real has been spoken in a world full of lies.
```

**Transition Line:**
```
Back in 2024, I adjust the turntable speed with practiced precision, understanding that even memory requires proper calibration to be heard accurately.
```

**Maxanne's Voice Resumes:**
```
"The thing about Nick Johnson's garage," I tell my invisible audience, "was that it remembered his father."
```

## POV Guidelines

### Maxanne's Voice Should:
- **Set up scenes** with historical context
- **Provide transitions** between major sections
- **Offer commentary** on music industry and cultural context
- **Frame the story** with her 2024 perspective

### Third-Person Should:
- **Show scenes directly** from Nick and Jackie's perspectives
- **Provide interior thoughts** and emotional access
- **Maintain narrative flow** without interruption
- **Focus on character development** and relationship dynamics

### Transition Principles:
- **Use bridging lines** that connect the two perspectives
- **Maintain story momentum** through POV changes
- **Keep transitions smooth** and natural
- **Preserve emotional continuity** across POV shifts

## Key Benefits of This Approach:
- **Unique framing device** (Maxanne's WBCN perspective)
- **Full character access** (Nick and Jackie's thoughts and feelings)
- **Historical authenticity** (1970s music industry context)
- **Emotional intimacy** with main characters
- **Systematic structure** for consistent application

## Implementation Notes:
- **Start with Maxanne** for setup and context
- **Transition smoothly** to third-person for main story
- **Return to Maxanne** only at major breaks
- **Maintain consistency** across all 25 chapters
- **Use Power Moves** to ensure quality transitions
