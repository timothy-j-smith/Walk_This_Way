In the distance, around the bend of the island, he could see an orange light bouncing just above the horizon.  He heard distorted voices.  They had come once again, so Oakley decided to walk counterclockwise along the rocky coast, back toward the dock, where he had earlier caught twenty-one crabs, but had let eight of them go.  The larger crabs he helped prepare, along with his older sister, Sissy, and her new boyfriend, Sam the Redhead, for dinner.  They steamed the crabs on thin sheet of rock placed over a driftwood fire.  The piled up damp seaweed on the rock so that it insulated the crabs, protecting the shells from cracking with the intense heat.

His mother and father each had a two pound lobster, steamed alive, wheezing, until the greenish maroon shell had turned almost a shade of pink.  They dipped their  lobster tails in real butter, wore lobster aprons, and sipped white wine from sexy wine glasses.  

Oakley liked walking along the rocky coast.  Jumping from rock to rock, watching the last of the sailboats make their way home with the dimming light.  Every day it seemed like the skies took on a different color, a different shade.  The mainland was close to fifteen miles off shore, and in the evening the winds calmed to tranquillity.  He wasn't sure why this was.  He was sure that his father could explain it to him, but he decided to just accept the  fact.  Being on the island he hadn't really noticed the winds, it was only when the winds stopped, and the seas calmed, that he missed the sound of the floating bell in the distance which chimed with every major swell, ringing throughout the day so that it was easy to hear it when it had stopped ringing.

At the bonfire Oakley looked out at the huge pile of driftwood on fire.  The ranger allowed fires in the rocky beach, because the high tide was sure to put it out.  All were welcome on the island as long as you picked up your garbage after staying.  Some came to stay for weeks, without running water and electricity.  Others came for day hikes throughout the different sections of the island.  And others just anchored the ir boats off the shore and paddled to shore for a cookout.  This is what the college kids did.  It seemed like this summer more than any other that Oakley could think of, college kids had all heard about the island.  Group after group throughout the week had come for a bonfire and dinner.  He figured they must all be related because a trend was started to through a torch wrapped with bark in the tip, into the water.  They all did this.

This particular night when he arrived there were more college kids then usual, close to twenty of them.  

When Oakley returned from the bonfire, he walked the narrow wooded trail back to camp.  He could have taken the shore route, but hopping the rocks at high tide was dangerous even on a full moon.  A fog had rolled in, encasing the island, glossing the rocks along the shore, and the blinking red beacon light ten miles off shore was barely visible.  
	Oakley had Maggie on the mind.  Maggie Dionne.  She had paddled ashore with the other "college kids", as his father liked to describe them, for their nightly bonfire, tossing of the ceremonial torch, and a feast filled with alcohol.  They would then row dinghies back to their yacht, for a good night sleep, roughing it on the island, without electricity and running water, was out of the question.  Oakley had never thought a lot about girls, until now, and he liked the way Maggie breathed, it seemed different, and exhilarating.   
	For the past two nights Oakley had watched this particular group of college kids as he had all the others, listening to stupid and sometimes comical stories of frat parties, keg parties, toga parties ... and observing as the night went on, and the sound of empty aluminum beer cans tinkled the rocky shore, how pairs gravitated together, a man and a woman.  This certainly beat hanging around his own family camp.  Tonight one of the collegians tried jumping the bonfire.  Everyone told him not to, especially the woman, which only spurred him on more.  After convincing himself that this feat was possible he landed smack in the middle of the flames, caught on fire, but was luckily tossed into the ocean by a few of his buddies standing nearby.  They all had a good laugh about it, except for the jumper, who ended up with singed eyebrows, and second degree burns on his hands.  This was Maggie's ex, she explained.

	When he arrived at the family camp his mother and father were sitting on an old tree trunk that had been washed ashore, the wood now soft to touch.  In front of them was the makeshift stove used for dinner, where they had cooked the lobsters bought in the mainland, and the fresh crabs that Oakley had caught earlier in the day.  The coals in the fire was still surging orange, like molten lava.  His parents were finishing off their second bottle of white wine, still drinking out of sexy wine glasses, and appeared to be getting along.  His mother had changed from shorts, a tee shirt, and hiking boots, into a blue silk evening dress, and she wore black high heels, matching her undergarments.
